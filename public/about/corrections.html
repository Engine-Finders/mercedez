Our Correction Policy

We correct errors as soon as we become aware of them, and we do so transparently. Every substantive change is logged, and minor clarifications are noted where appropriate.

We define a substantive correction as any change that alters a factual claim - a price, a model year, a failure pattern, a specification, or a data tag. Typographical errors, grammar fixes, and formatting adjustments are not logged unless they affect meaning.

When a correction is made, the affected page carries a visible note (typically in the summary box or near the corrected data) that states the nature of the change and the date. This entry in the corrections log provides the full context, including the original error, the corrected version, and the reason for the change.

Our policy is fully integrated with our editorial standards and our data methodology - we correct data errors by re-running queries against the source data, never by "adjusting" numbers without verification.

How to Report an Error

If you spot an error on any page, we want to hear about it. Here's how to tell us.

Email: corrections@mercedesengines.uk (preferred) - please include the page URL, the specific claim you believe is incorrect, and any supporting evidence or source material.

Contact form: Use our contact page and select "Correction" from the subject dropdown.

What happens next: We acknowledge receipt within 48 hours. We then investigate - consulting our data sources, checking against the proprietary data file, and reviewing any third-party references. If we confirm the error, we correct the page and log the change. If we disagree, we explain our position.

We treat every report seriously and appreciate the time taken to help us maintain accuracy.

How We Verify a Correction

No correction is made on a single report alone. Every change goes through a verification step.

When an error is reported, we cross-check it against:

  • The original source data - our proprietary EM_Proprietary_Data_2025.txt file, if a data claim is involved
  • Third-party sources such as manufacturer TSBs, AutoTrader vehicle values, or official technical bulletins
  • Our own editorial records - what was originally written and why
  • Consultation with our expert panel for technical claims

If the error is confirmed, we update the page, add a visible correction note, and log the change here. If we cannot confirm the error, we may request further evidence or explain our reasoning to the reporter. We never change a figure without being certain.

All corrections are reviewed by a second editor before publication. No single person has authority to make a substantive correction unilaterally.

Our Commitment to Accuracy

Accuracy is not a one-time check - it is an ongoing practice. We review our content regularly and update it as new data becomes available.

We publish a failure index with annual updates, and we refresh cost data quarterly. When we make a correction due to new data, we log it here and note the updated date on the affected page.

We also welcome feedback from our readers. If you believe we have missed something or that a claim is no longer accurate, please contact us - we treat every report as an opportunity to improve.

Our full editorial standards, including our approach to sourcing, data verification, and correction, are set out on our editorial standards page and our data verification page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I report an error?

Email corrections@mercedesengines.uk with the page URL, the specific claim, and any supporting evidence. Alternatively, use our contact form.

How quickly are corrections made?

We aim to acknowledge any report within 48 hours. Substantive corrections are typically made within 5 working days, subject to verification.

Do you correct errors in older articles?

Yes. We correct errors regardless of the age of the article. All substantive corrections are logged here and noted on the page itself.

How can I verify that a correction has been made?

Each correction is listed in this log with a date, page link, and description of the change. The affected page also carries a visible correction note.

Do you correct data that later becomes outdated?

Yes. When new data becomes available, we update the relevant pages and log the change as an "update" rather than a "correction" - but both are recorded here.
