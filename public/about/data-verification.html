Our Data Sources

We draw on three primary sources of data, each with a distinct purpose and verification method.

1. Proprietary enquiry data ([EM-VERIFIED])
This is our core asset. 24,650 unique quote requests from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, full year 2025. It includes engine and model request volumes, condition splits (reconditioned vs used), and enquiry trends. Every count in this data is exact - we never approximate.

2. Supplier and mechanic interviews ([EM-QUOTE], [EM-INTERVIEW], [EM-SURVEY])
We speak directly with specialists who quote and rebuild these engines every day. This gives us pricing ranges, labour hours, and repair patterns that aren't visible in enquiry data alone. Post-sale surveys (100+ customers, 3–6 months after replacement) provide outcome data.

3. Third-party public data ([THIRD-PARTY])
Where we need market context - vehicle values, manufacturer technical bulletins, official specifications - we cite named, dated external sources such as AutoTrader, Parkers, and manufacturer TSBs.

All three are cross-referenced before publication. If the data doesn't agree, we investigate further before making a claim.

Our Verification Process

A number is not published until it has passed three checks.

Check 1 - Internal consistency: Does the number match the source data? A claim about OM651 request volume must match the exact figure in our CRM. If it doesn't, we don't publish it.

Check 2 - Cross-reference: For [EM-QUOTE] figures, we cross-check with supplier interviews and marketplace data. A price range is not published unless it's corroborated by at least two independent sources.

Check 3 - Editorial review: Every number is reviewed by a second editor before publication. This person checks the tag, verifies the source, and ensures the claim is appropriately contextualised.

If we cannot verify a figure, it does not appear on the site. Our corrections log records any instances where a figure was revised after publication.

How We Handle Data Updates

Data changes over time. We update our content when new data becomes available.

Our core enquiry data is refreshed annually. Pricing figures are reviewed quarterly, using recent supplier quotes and marketplace checks. When we update a figure, we log the change in our corrections log as an "update" rather than a "correction" - the change is still recorded, but it reflects new data rather than an error.

We also monitor external data sources - manufacturer TSBs, vehicle value databases - and update our content accordingly. Any page that makes a data claim carries a dateModified in its schema markup so search engines and readers can see when it was last reviewed.

Our annual failure index is updated each year with the latest data, and we note the period covered in the methodology.

What We Don't Claim

Honesty about limitations is as important as confidence in verified data.

Request volume ≠ failure rate. High enquiry numbers may reflect large vehicle parc or audience bias, not engineering quality. We never claim "Engine A is less reliable than Engine B because it has more quotes." Instead, we state the volume fact and use the failure pattern table to support engineering claims.

Pricing ranges are indicative, not guaranteed. We publish ranges from real supplier quotes, but actual costs vary by region, condition, and specialist. We recommend obtaining a specific quote before committing.

We don't invent data. If a claim cannot be substantiated with a tag, we either use [PATTERN DATA] with a clear disclaimer, or we omit the claim. We never present an estimate as a verified number.

Our methodology page and editorial standards set out our approach in full, including how we handle conflicting sources and what we do when data is unavailable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does [EM-VERIFIED] mean?

It means the figure is an exact count or percentage from our internal CRM - 24,650 unique quote requests from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, full year 2025.

How do you verify [EM-QUOTE] figures?

Pricing figures are cross-checked against supplier quotes, marketplace data, and specialist interviews. A price range is only published if it is corroborated by at least two independent sources.

Why do you use [PATTERN DATA]?

Some claims - such as common failure patterns or typical mileage ranges - are widely documented in the trade but not captured in our proprietary data. These are marked [PATTERN DATA] so you know they are expert-observed rather than precisely measured.

How often is the data updated?

Enquiry data is refreshed annually. Pricing is reviewed quarterly. All updates are noted in our corrections log.

Can I see the raw data?

Our methodology page sets out sample size, period, inclusion criteria, and how we handle outliers. While we don't publish the raw CRM data for commercial and privacy reasons, we publish the key aggregated figures on our data pages.
