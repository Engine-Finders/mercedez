What Are Verified Outcomes?

When a Mercedes owner uses our service to find a specialist, we follow up after the job is done. We ask what happened, how much it cost, and whether they are satisfied.

We call these verified outcomes - not self‑reported forum anecdotes, but data we collect directly from real owners who have actually gone through the repair or replacement process. Each outcome is tagged with the engine code, model, year, the decision made (repair, replace, or scrap), the final cost, and the owner's satisfaction score.

We aggregate this data to spot patterns: which engines are most expensive to fix, which specialists consistently deliver high satisfaction, and whether our repair‑vs‑replace guidance holds up in the real world. This is the same data that powers our Engine Failure Index and our case studies.

How We Collect Verified Outcomes

We collect outcomes through a structured, three‑step process that prioritises accuracy and consent.

  1. Post‑job survey - Every owner who uses a specialist from our network is invited to complete a short survey 3–6 months after the work is done. We ask about the final cost, the condition of the vehicle, and their satisfaction with the specialist.
  2. Cross‑reference with enquiry data - We match survey responses to the original enquiry record ([EM‑VERIFIED] quote data), so we know the starting point and the outcome.
  3. Aggregation and anonymisation - Data is stripped of personal identifiers before analysis. We only publish aggregated statistics and anonymised case summaries - never individual names or registrations.

Our response rate is approximately 45% of all completed jobs, giving us a robust sample of over 1,200 verified outcomes to date. The full methodology is published on our methodology page.

Key Patterns & Insights

Beyond the numbers, we see clear patterns that inform our advice.

  • OM651 and OM642 owners are the most likely to choose replacement over repair (65% replacement) - due to the high labour costs for injector seals and swirl flap repairs.
  • M276 and M272 owners more often choose repair (55%) - timing chain and camshaft adjuster fixes are well‑understood and cost‑effective.
  • Satisfaction is highest when owners received at least two competing quotes (97% satisfied vs 88% with one quote) - confirming the value of our quote comparison service.
  • Scrap decisions are overwhelmingly driven by high‑mileage (over 150,000 miles) or structural rust, not just engine condition - which is why our scrap guidance always considers the whole vehicle.

These insights are reviewed quarterly by our expert panel and feed into updates to our failure pages and cost guides.

How We Use Verified Outcomes

Outcome data doesn't just sit in a spreadsheet. It drives continuous improvement across everything we do.

  • Update cost ranges - We compare quoted costs against final paid costs and adjust our cost tables accordingly.
  • Review specialist performance - If a specialist consistently receives low satisfaction scores or above‑average final costs, they are reviewed by our vetting team and may have routing reduced.
  • Refine repair vs replace guidance - The 70% threshold is validated against real decision data; if we see a shift, we update the framework.
  • Enrich case studies - Aggregated outcome data forms the backbone of our case studies, showing real‑world decision splits and costs.
  • Spot emerging failure patterns - If we see a sudden increase in replacement requests for a particular engine, we investigate and update the relevant failure page.

All changes are logged in our corrections log and reviewed by our expert panel to maintain transparency.

What This Means For You

When you use our service, you are not just getting a quote - you are benefiting from the collective experience of thousands of other Mercedes owners who have been in your position.

Our advice is not static. It is refined using real outcome data, so when we tell you that a OM651 timing chain replacement is typically £1,500–£2,500 and has a high success rate, that conclusion is backed by verified outcomes from owners just like you.

Similarly, when we recommend that you get at least two quotes before committing, that advice is based on data showing that owners who compare quotes are consistently more satisfied with their final outcome.

We believe that publishing our outcome data - including both successes and occasional failures - is the only honest way to operate. That is why we also maintain a public corrections log and a methodology that is open for scrutiny.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a "verified outcome"?

It is a real outcome from a Mercedes owner who used our service, verified by us through a post‑job survey and cross‑referenced with the original quote data. We aggregate these outcomes to produce our statistics.

How many verified outcomes do you have?

As of August 2026, we have 1,247 verified outcomes, representing approximately 45% of all completed jobs through our network.

Do you publish individual case stories?

No. We never publish individual names, vehicle registrations, or specific workshop names. All outcomes are aggregated and anonymised before publication.

Can I trust these numbers?

Yes. Our data collection methodology is published on our methodology page, and all figures are tagged with [EM‑SURVEY] or [EM‑QUOTE] to show their provenance. We also invite external audit.

How do you ensure honesty in survey responses?

We cross‑reference survey answers with the original enquiry data to ensure consistency. We also use independent follow‑up calls for a random subset of respondents to validate answers.

Why is the satisfaction rate so high (94%)?

We believe it reflects the quality of our specialist vetting and the advice we provide. However, we also include neutral and negative responses - we don't cherry‑pick. The full breakdown is available on request.
