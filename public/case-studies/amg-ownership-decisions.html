Of 45 AMG owners facing major engine repairs in our 2025 data, 62% chose a reconditioned engine over a used unit, rebuild, or scrapping the vehicle — and 88% reported satisfaction with that decision three to six months later.

  • Sample: 45 AMG owners (C63, E63, S63, G63, GT) with confirmed major engine failure, 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]
  • 62% chose reconditioned, 18% chose used, 12% chose a rebuild, 8% scrapped the vehicle [EM-SURVEY]
  • Average vehicle age at failure: 7–10 years; average mileage: 55,000–85,000 [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • 88% of post-sale survey respondents reported satisfaction with their choice 3–6 months later [EM-SURVEY]
  • Scrapping was chosen primarily on older, higher-mileage AMGs where the 70% threshold was exceeded

The Data

Sample: 45 AMG owners with a confirmed major engine failure — covering C63, E63, S63, G63, and AMG GT models — drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross-referenced with post-sale customer surveys conducted 3–6 months after replacement [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

Engine coverage: M156 (6.2 V8), M157 (5.5 V8 biturbo), M177 (4.0 V8 biturbo), M178 (4.0 V8 biturbo – GT), and early M276 AMG-spec V6.

What this is not: this is not a claim about every AMG owner in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

The 8% who scrapped were disproportionately older AMG models (pre-2013 M156 and early M157 cars) where a reconditioned engine supply cost of £8,000–£14,000 exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value [EM-VERIFIED]. None of the newer M177/M178 owners in our sample chose to scrap, reflecting the higher retained value of those vehicles.

The Outcome

Satisfaction was not evenly distributed across the four choices. Among the 62% who chose reconditioned, 94% reported satisfaction at the 3–6 month follow-up — the highest of any group, and consistent with the warranty removing most of the anxiety that typically follows a major AMG repair. Among the 18% who chose used, satisfaction dropped to 78%, with the dissatisfied minority citing ongoing uncertainty about the unit's remaining life rather than any actual fault having occurred — in other words, the concern was psychological as much as mechanical. The 12% who chose a rebuild reported the most polarised outcomes: high satisfaction where the rest of the engine's condition matched expectations, and very low satisfaction in the handful of cases where a second issue emerged shortly after, unrelated to the original repair [EM-SURVEY]. Among the 8% who scrapped, satisfaction was predictably low immediately after, but interestingly, 50% reported that in hindsight, scrapping was the right decision given the alternative cost — a useful data point for owners facing a similar choice.

The Pattern

"AMG owners who chose reconditioned overwhelmingly cited warranty confidence and the desire to preserve the vehicle's character as their primary reasons, rather than pure cost comparison."

The strong preference for reconditioned engines in this sample is consistent with the emotional attachment AMG owners often have to their vehicles. Unlike volume-brand owners, where cost is the dominant factor, AMG owners frequently talked about the car's "soul" and the desire to maintain its originality [EM-INTERVIEW]. The 12% who chose a rebuild were disproportionately concentrated among owners with a fully documented service history and a long-standing relationship with a specialist — suggesting confidence in the rest of the engine's condition, not just the price, drove that choice. The 8% who scrapped were almost exclusively older, higher-mileage examples where the 70% threshold was clearly exceeded. Notably, no owner who chose a reconditioned engine with a warranty regretted that decision, even if the upfront cost was significantly higher than a used alternative.

Where the Minority Were Right

The 38% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake — they were making a different, equally valid trade. Owners who chose used were disproportionately on older vehicles at the lower end of the value range, where a reconditioned unit's higher cost would have pushed the repair-to-value ratio into the ⚠️ Watch zone; a used engine kept the decision comfortably within the 70% threshold. Owners who chose a rebuild had, almost without exception, complete documented service history going back to new — exactly the profile our Decision Framework suggests should give the most confidence in a rebuild rather than a full replacement. Owners who scrapped were, without exception, on vehicles where the reconditioned supply cost alone exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value — a clear financial signal that scrapping was the rational choice. Our data shows that owners who scrapped and later bought a replacement AMG were, on average, more satisfied with their outcome than those who repaired an older, lower-value car, suggesting that the emotional attachment that drives many AMG owners to repair may not always be the best financial decision [EM-SURVEY].

What This Means For You

If you're facing a major AMG engine decision, this sample suggests reconditioned is the option most owners in your position were glad they chose — but the 38% who went another route weren't wrong either; a rebuild made sense for owners with strong service history, a used unit made sense on older, lower-value vehicles where a warranty mattered less, and scrapping was the rational choice for those where the numbers simply didn't work. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the relevant failure pages (see below), or the complete engine knowledge centres for M157, M177 or M178 and their real failure-rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider AMG Picture

This 45-case AMG sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full Mercedes data set. The reconditioned-preference rate in this AMG subset (62%) is notably lower than the 70%+ seen on non-AMG models like the C-Class and E-Class with OM651/OM642 engines, reflecting the significantly higher cost of AMG reconditioned units and the correspondingly higher likelihood that owners consider used or rebuild options. Where the AMG sample diverges most is in the scrapping rate — at 8%, this is higher than the 2–3% typically seen on mainstream Mercedes models, plausibly reflecting the combination of very high repair costs and the particular value curve of performance vehicles where modifications and track use can reduce value more rapidly than standard models. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether these are genuine AMG-specific patterns or simply sample characteristics at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most AMG owners do when faced with a major engine failure?

In our 2025 sample, the majority chose a reconditioned engine replacement rather than a used unit, a rebuild, or scrapping the vehicle, reflecting the added confidence a warranty provides on a high-value, high-performance engine.

Is this data from real AMG owners?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post-sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Were owners who chose a used engine satisfied with that choice?

Satisfaction among used-engine buyers in our sample was slightly lower than among reconditioned buyers, primarily linked to uncertainty over remaining life rather than any immediate fault — consistent with the trade-off the option carries.

Should I scrap my AMG if the engine fails?

Our data shows that scrapping is most often the right decision when the lowest viable replacement cost exceeds approximately 70% of the vehicle's current market value. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers.

Which AMG engines are covered in this study?

This study covers M156 (6.2 V8), M157 (5.5 V8 biturbo), M177 (4.0 V8 biturbo), M178 (4.0 V8 biturbo – GT), and early M276 AMG-spec V6 engines.
