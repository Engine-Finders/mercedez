Of 100 C‑Class owners facing major engine repairs in our 2025 sample, 68% chose a reconditioned engine over a used unit or scrapping - and 89% reported satisfaction with that decision three to six months later.

  • Sample: 100 C‑Class owners (W204, W205, W206) with confirmed major engine failure, 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]
  • 68% chose reconditioned, 22% chose used, 10% scrapped the vehicle [EM-SURVEY]

The Data

Sample: 100 C‑Class owners (spanning W204, W205, and W206 chassis) with a confirmed major engine failure, drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross‑referenced with post‑sale customer surveys conducted 3‑6 months after repair [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

Engine coverage: primarily OM651 (2.1 diesel) and OM654 (2.0 diesel), with a small number of petrol M274 and M276 units. OM651 accounted for the majority of cases, consistent with its high request volume (1,250 UK enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]).

What this is not: this is not a claim about every C‑Class owner in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

The 10% who scrapped were almost exclusively older W204 models (pre‑2014) where the cost of a reconditioned OM651 (£2,800–£4,800 supply only) approached or exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value. In contrast, no W206 owners in our sample chose to scrap, reflecting the higher retained value of newer cars [EM-VERIFIED].

The Outcome

Satisfaction was highest among those who chose reconditioned: 94% of that group reported being satisfied at the 3–6 month follow‑up, with the warranty cited as the primary reason. Among used‑engine buyers, satisfaction dropped to 76%, with the dissatisfied minority expressing concern about the unit's unknown history. Scrapping owners reported the most mixed feelings - while 60% said they were satisfied with the decision in hindsight, the immediate aftermath was often emotionally difficult [EM-SURVEY]. Notably, owners who chose reconditioned on a W205 or W206 were significantly more likely to recommend the same path to others than those on older W204 models, suggesting that vehicle value plays a strong role in post‑decision confidence.

The Pattern

"C‑Class owners who chose reconditioned overwhelmingly cited warranty confidence and the desire to keep the car for several more years as their primary reasons."

The strong preference for reconditioned engines in this sample is consistent with the nature of the most common failures: OM651 timing chain stretch and injector seal leaks are well‑understood, but both can cause consequential damage if not addressed promptly. Owners who chose reconditioned frequently mentioned the peace of mind that a warranty provides, especially given the age of many C‑Class vehicles in our sample. The 22% who chose used were concentrated on older W204 models where the vehicle's value was below £6,000, making a reconditioned unit less financially viable [PATTERN DATA]. Scrapping was almost always a response to a combination of high repair cost and low vehicle value - a classic "beyond economic repair" scenario.

Where the Minority Were Right

The 32% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake - they were making a different, equally valid trade. Owners who chose used were disproportionately on vehicles at the lower end of the value range, where a reconditioned unit's higher cost would have pushed the repair‑to‑value ratio into the ⚠️ Watch zone; a used engine kept the decision comfortably ✅ Safe. Owners who scrapped were, without exception, on vehicles where the reconditioned supply cost alone exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value - a clear financial signal that scrapping was the rational choice. Our data shows that owners who scrapped and later bought a replacement car were, on average, more satisfied with their outcome than those who repaired a very low‑value car, suggesting that the emotional attachment that drives many owners to repair may not always be the best financial decision [EM-SURVEY].

What This Means For You

If you're facing a major C‑Class engine decision, this sample suggests reconditioned is the option most owners in your position were glad they chose - but the 32% who went another route weren't wrong either; a used unit made sense on older, lower‑value vehicles where a warranty mattered less, and scrapping was the rational choice for those where the numbers simply didn't work. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the relevant failure pages (see below), or the complete engine knowledge centres for OM651 and OM654 and their real failure‑rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider C‑Class Picture

This 100‑case C‑Class sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full Mercedes data set. The reconditioned‑preference rate in this C‑Class subset (68%) is broadly in line with the 70%+ seen on other mainstream models like the E‑Class with OM642, but notably higher than the 62% we observed in our AMG sample - reflecting the significantly higher cost of AMG reconditioned units. Where the C‑Class sample diverges most is in the scrapping rate - at 10%, this is higher than the 2–3% typically seen on newer models, but consistent with the age profile of the cars in our sample. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether these are genuine C‑Class‑specific patterns or simply sample characteristics at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most C‑Class owners do when faced with a major engine failure?

In our 2025 sample, the majority chose a reconditioned engine replacement rather than a used unit or scrapping the vehicle, reflecting the added confidence a warranty provides on a high‑value, high‑mileage engine.

Is this data from real C‑Class owners?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post‑sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Were owners who chose a used engine satisfied with that choice?

Satisfaction among used‑engine buyers in our sample was slightly lower than among reconditioned buyers, primarily linked to uncertainty over remaining life rather than any immediate fault - consistent with the trade‑off the option carries.

Should I scrap my C‑Class if the engine fails?

Our data shows that scrapping is most often the right decision when the lowest viable replacement cost exceeds approximately 70% of the vehicle's current market value. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers.

Which C‑Class engines are covered in this study?

This study covers primarily OM651 (2.1 diesel) and OM654 (2.0 diesel), with a small number of petrol M274 and M276 units. OM651 accounted for the majority of cases.
