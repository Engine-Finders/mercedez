The Data

Sample: 45 M276 V6 owners (covering E‑Class W213, C‑Class W205, GLE W166, and CLS C218) with a confirmed camshaft adjuster failure, drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross‑referenced with post‑sale customer surveys conducted 3‑6 months after repair [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

Engine coverage: M276 3.0 and 3.5 V6 petrol engines. The M276 generated 390 UK enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], making it one of the most‑requested petrol V6 engines in our data, alongside the M272.

What this is not: this is not a claim about every M276 owner in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

The 13% who scrapped were predominantly older, high‑mileage vehicles where the cost of a reconditioned engine (£3,800–£6,500 supply only) exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value. Notably, owners who chose adjuster‑only repair often did so with the understanding that other components-particularly the timing chain-might need attention later [EM-INTERVIEW].

The Outcome

Satisfaction was highest among those who chose reconditioned: 94% of that group reported being satisfied at the 3–6 month follow‑up, with the warranty and peace of mind cited as the primary reasons. Among adjuster‑only repair buyers, satisfaction dropped to 70%, with the dissatisfied group citing subsequent issues-most commonly timing chain stretch or oil leaks-that emerged within the follow‑up period. Scrapping owners reported the most mixed feelings - while 60% said they were satisfied with the decision in hindsight, the immediate aftermath was often emotionally difficult, particularly for owners who had owned the car for many years [EM-SURVEY]. Notably, owners who chose reconditioned on a lower‑mileage W213 were significantly more likely to recommend the same path to others than those on older W205 models, suggesting that vehicle age and overall condition play a strong role in post‑decision confidence.

The Pattern

"M276 owners who chose reconditioned often cited the 'peace of mind' factor - knowing the entire engine was refreshed, not just the camshaft adjusters."

The strong preference for reconditioned engines in this sample reflects the known weaknesses of the M276 beyond the camshaft adjusters themselves. Many owners who chose adjuster‑only repair reported that they were "rolling the dice" on other components, particularly the timing chain, which can also fail on higher‑mileage units. The 27% who chose adjuster‑only were concentrated on vehicles with lower mileage (<70,000 miles) where the rest of the engine was expected to have more life left. The 13% who scrapped were almost exclusively high‑mileage vehicles where the cost of a reconditioned engine (£3,800–£6,500 supply only) approached or exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value [PATTERN DATA].

Where the Minority Were Right

The 40% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake - they were making a different, equally valid trade. Owners who chose adjuster‑only repair were on lower‑mileage vehicles where the rest of the engine was in good condition, making a full replacement unnecessary. Owners who scrapped were, without exception, on vehicles where the reconditioned supply cost alone exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value - a clear financial signal that scrapping was the rational choice. Our data shows that owners who scrapped and later bought a replacement car were, on average, more satisfied with their outcome than those who repaired a very high‑mileage car, suggesting that the emotional attachment that drives many owners to repair may not always be the best financial decision [EM-SURVEY].

What This Means For You

If your M276 has a camshaft adjuster issue, the decision depends on mileage, overall engine condition, and vehicle value. For lower‑mileage vehicles (<70,000 miles) with no other known issues, adjuster‑only repair may be a cost‑effective option. For higher‑mileage vehicles, a reconditioned engine is often the better long‑term investment - our data shows it has the highest satisfaction rate. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the M276 camshaft adjuster failure page, or the complete M276 Knowledge Centre and its real failure‑rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider M276 Picture

This 45‑case M276 sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full Mercedes data set. The reconditioned‑preference rate in this M276 subset (60%) is slightly lower than the 65% seen on M272 cases, reflecting the fact that M276 owners often have newer vehicles with higher retained values, making the reconditioned option more financially viable. Where the M276 sample diverges most is in the adjuster‑only repair rate - at 27%, this is higher than the 22% seen on M272 chain‑only repairs, reflecting the fact that camshaft adjuster failure is often more isolated than timing chain failure on the older M272. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether these are genuine M276‑specific patterns or simply sample characteristics at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most M276 owners do when the camshaft adjuster fails?

In our 2025 sample, the majority chose a reconditioned engine replacement rather than an adjuster‑only repair or scrapping the vehicle, reflecting the added confidence a full engine replacement provides on a V6 with other known weaknesses.

Is this data from real M276 owners?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post‑sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Should I just replace the adjusters or fit a reconditioned engine?

Our data shows that adjuster‑only repair has a lower satisfaction rate (70% vs 94% for reconditioned), primarily due to other M276 weaknesses (timing chain stretch, oil leaks) that can emerge later. For lower‑mileage cars, adjuster‑only may make sense; for higher‑mileage cars, reconditioned is often the better long‑term choice.

Which M276 models are covered in this study?

This study covers E‑Class W213, C‑Class W205, GLE W166, and CLS C218 M276 V6 engines, with the majority of cases on E‑Class (2016–2020) and C‑Class (2014–2018).

How much does an M276 camshaft adjuster repair cost?

In our data, adjuster‑only repair typically costs £1,800–£2,800. Reconditioned supply costs range from £3,800–£6,500, with fitting adding approximately £780–£1,040 in labour [EM-QUOTE].
