The Data

Sample: 55 OM642 V6 diesel owners (covering E‑Class W211, E‑Class W212, GLE W166, ML W164, and S‑Class W221) with a confirmed swirl flap motor failure, drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross‑referenced with post‑sale customer surveys conducted 3‑6 months after repair [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

Engine coverage: OM642 3.0 V6 diesel engine. The OM642 generated 880 UK enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], making it one of the most‑requested diesel engines in our data alongside the OM651.

What this is not: this is not a claim about every OM642 owner in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

The 15% who scrapped were predominantly older W211 and W164 models with market values below £6,000, where the cost of a reconditioned OM642 (£3,200–£5,500 supply only) exceeded 70% of the vehicle's value. Notably, owners who chose swirl flap‑only repair often did so with the understanding that other OM642 weaknesses-particularly oil cooler seal leaks-might need attention later [EM-INTERVIEW].

The Outcome

Satisfaction was highest among those who chose reconditioned: 92% of that group reported being satisfied at the 3–6 month follow‑up, with the warranty and peace of mind cited as the primary reasons. Among swirl flap‑only repair buyers, satisfaction dropped to 65%, with the dissatisfied group citing subsequent issues-most commonly oil cooler seal leaks or turbo issues-that emerged within the follow‑up period. Scrapping owners reported the most mixed feelings - while 58% said they were satisfied with the decision in hindsight, the immediate aftermath was often emotionally difficult, particularly for owners who had owned the car for many years [EM-SURVEY]. Notably, owners who chose reconditioned on a lower‑mileage W212 were significantly more likely to recommend the same path to others than those on older W211 models, suggesting that vehicle age and overall condition play a strong role in post‑decision confidence.

The Pattern

"OM642 owners who chose reconditioned often cited the 'peace of mind' factor - knowing the entire engine was refreshed, not just the swirl flaps."

The strong preference for reconditioned engines in this sample reflects the known weaknesses of the OM642 beyond the swirl flaps themselves. Many owners who chose swirl flap‑only repair reported that they were "rolling the dice" on other components, particularly the oil cooler seals, which are a notorious OM642 weakness. The 29% who chose swirl flap‑only were concentrated on vehicles with lower mileage (<80,000 miles) where the rest of the engine was expected to have more life left. The 15% who scrapped were almost exclusively high‑mileage vehicles where the cost of a reconditioned engine (£3,200–£5,500 supply only) approached or exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value [PATTERN DATA].

Where the Minority Were Right

The 44% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake - they were making a different, equally valid trade. Owners who chose swirl flap‑only repair were on lower‑mileage vehicles where the rest of the engine was in good condition, making a full replacement unnecessary. Owners who scrapped were, without exception, on vehicles where the reconditioned supply cost alone exceeded 70% of the vehicle's market value - a clear financial signal that scrapping was the rational choice. Our data shows that owners who scrapped and later bought a replacement car were, on average, more satisfied with their outcome than those who repaired a very high‑mileage car, suggesting that the emotional attachment that drives many owners to repair may not always be the best financial decision [EM-SURVEY].

What This Means For You

If your OM642 has a swirl flap issue, the decision depends on mileage, overall engine condition, and vehicle value. For lower‑mileage vehicles (<80,000 miles) with no other known issues, swirl flap‑only repair may be a cost‑effective option. For higher‑mileage vehicles, a reconditioned engine is often the better long‑term investment - our data shows it has the highest satisfaction rate. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the OM642 swirl flap failure page, or the complete OM642 Knowledge Centre and its real failure‑rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider OM642 Picture

This 55‑case OM642 sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full Mercedes data set. The reconditioned‑preference rate in this OM642 subset (56%) is notably lower than the 60–65% seen on petrol V6 engines (M272, M276), reflecting the higher repair costs and the fact that OM642 vehicles are often older with lower retained values. Where the OM642 sample diverges most is in the swirl flap‑only repair rate - at 29%, this is higher than the 22–27% seen on petrol V6 cases, reflecting the fact that swirl flap failure is often more isolated than timing chain failure on petrol engines. The scrapping rate (15%) is also higher than on petrol V6 cases, consistent with the older average age of OM642 vehicles in our sample. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether these are genuine OM642‑specific patterns or simply sample characteristics at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most OM642 owners do when the swirl flaps fail?

In our 2025 sample, the majority chose a reconditioned engine replacement rather than a swirl flap‑only repair or scrapping the vehicle, reflecting the added confidence a full engine replacement provides on a V6 diesel with other known weaknesses.

Is this data from real OM642 owners?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post‑sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Should I just replace the swirl flaps or fit a reconditioned engine?

Our data shows that swirl flap‑only repair has a lower satisfaction rate (65% vs 92% for reconditioned), primarily due to other OM642 weaknesses (oil cooler seals, turbo issues) that can emerge later. For lower‑mileage cars, swirl flap‑only may make sense; for higher‑mileage cars, reconditioned is often the better long‑term choice.

Which OM642 models are covered in this study?

This study covers E‑Class W211 and W212, GLE W166, ML W164, and S‑Class W221 OM642 V6 diesel engines, with the majority of cases on E‑Class (2005–2013) and ML (2005–2011).

How much does an OM642 swirl flap repair cost?

In our data, swirl flap‑only repair typically costs £500–£1,200. Reconditioned supply costs range from £3,200–£5,500, with fitting adding approximately £650–£910 in labour [EM-QUOTE].
