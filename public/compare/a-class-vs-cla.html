A-Class vs CLA

Hatchback practicality against four-door coupe style. Both share platforms and engines, but which one fits your life and budget? Real 2025 UK data and a clear verdict below.

The A-Class wins for most buyers. It offers the same engine range, nearly identical running costs, and significantly more rear headroom and practicality — all for a lower purchase price than the equivalent CLA. The CLA's advantage is purely styling: a lower, sleeker four-door coupe profile that costs more and compromises rear passenger space.

The Verdict

The A-Class is the stronger choice for the vast majority of buyers. It delivers the same engines, the same running costs, and virtually identical ownership economics — but with more rear space, a more practical boot, and a lower purchase price. The CLA's only genuine advantage is its exterior design: a lower, wider, more coupe-like profile that commands a premium and compromises rear headroom and visibility. Unless that styling is non-negotiable for you, the A-Class is the smarter buy [EM-VERIFIED].

The CLA isn't a bad car — it's a very good one, and its Shooting Brake version adds genuine estate practicality. But in the standard four-door form, the compromise is clear: you pay more for less space. The data supports that, with A-Class owners reporting consistently higher satisfaction with practicality and day-to-day usability [PATTERN DATA].

A-Class Deep Dive

The Mercedes A-Class is the brand's entry-level hatchback, available in W176 (2012–2018) and W177 (2018–present) generations. It shares the MFA platform with the CLA and B-Class, and its engine range is near-identical: OM651 1.8/2.1 diesels, M270/M274 1.6/2.0 petrols, and AMG variants (A35, A45) with M133 and M139 engines. The most common UK engine is the OM651 diesel, which generated 1,250 enquiries across the Mercedes range in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. The A-Class's defining strength is its hatchback practicality: a tall, square boot opening, usable rear seats with genuine headroom for adults, and a driving position that feels more traditional and car-like than the CLA's lower, more reclined seating [EM-INTERVIEW].

In the used market, the A-Class offers better value than the CLA at equivalent age and mileage — typically £1,500–£3,000 less for a similarly specified car. This makes it the more accessible entry point to Mercedes ownership, and the one that holds its value better against the higher purchase price premium of the CLA. Full specs, fitment and reliability ratings live on the OM651 Knowledge Centre and the M270/M271 Knowledge Centre.

CLA Deep Dive

The Mercedes CLA is the four-door coupe version of the A-Class, available in C117 (2013–2019) and X118 (2019–present) generations. It shares the same MFA/MFA2 platform, the same engine range, and the same OM651 diesel chain-tensioner risk. What it adds is a significantly more distinctive exterior design — lower roof, wider stance, frameless doors, and a sportier rear roofline — at the cost of rear headroom, rear visibility, and a smaller boot opening [PATTERN DATA].

The CLA also offers a Shooting Brake estate version, which genuinely resolves the practicality gap and is worth considering if you need estate space but prefer the CLA's styling. However, the standard four-door CLA's rear seats are noticeably less accommodating for adults on longer journeys, and the sloping roofline makes entry and exit more awkward. The CLA's purchase price premium over the A-Class is typically £2,000–£4,000 on the used market, with no meaningful difference in running costs or engine reliability to justify it [EM-INTERVIEW]. Cost of Ownership Difference

Over a typical three-to-five-year ownership period, the cost difference between the A-Class and CLA comes down to three things: purchase price, insurance group, and depreciation. Running costs — fuel, servicing, tax, and repair bills — are functionally identical, given they share engines, platforms, and nearly all mechanical components. The A-Class is cheaper to buy upfront, typically sits in a lower insurance group, and tends to depreciate less dramatically as a percentage of its lower purchase price [THIRD-PARTY]. The CLA's depreciation is sharper in absolute terms because it starts from a higher price point and appeals to a narrower buyer pool.

When an engine replacement is required, both cars use the same OM651 or M270/M274 engines, so the supply cost is identical: £2,800–£4,800 for a reconditioned OM651, or £3,800–£6,500 for an M276 V6 petrol if applicable [EM-QUOTE]. The repair vs replace framework applies equally to both, and the 70% Mercedes threshold is the same for both models.

Which Should You Buy?

🚗

Buy the A-Class if…

You need usable rear seats, practical hatchback access, and the best value for money. The A-Class does everything the CLA does, for less money, in a more usable package.

✨

Buy the CLA if…

Style is your priority and you rarely carry rear passengers. The CLA's four-door coupe silhouette is genuinely distinctive, and the Shooting Brake version offers a compromise worth considering.

If you want CLA styling but need estate practicality, the Shooting Brake version resolves the boot and rear-space compromise without losing the design appeal.

Budget-focused buyers

The A-Class is the clear winner. Lower purchase price, lower insurance, and the same running costs. The CLA's premium is purely aesthetic.

One important nuance: the CLA's styling premium does hold value with a specific buyer demographic — if you're buying one, you're buying it for the look, not the logic. There's nothing wrong with that, but be honest about the trade-off. The A-Class is the rational choice; the CLA is the emotional one. Both are good cars, but the A-Class is the better value [PATTERN DATA].

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the A-Class more practical than the CLA?

Yes, significantly. The A-Class hatchback has more rear headroom, a taller boot opening, and better rear visibility. The CLA's sloping roofline compromises rear passenger space and the boot opening is narrower. If you regularly carry rear passengers or bulky items, the A-Class is the better choice.

Which is more reliable, A-Class or CLA?

They share the same engines and platforms, so reliability is functionally identical. Both are subject to the OM651 timing chain tensioner risk (pre-2016) and the OM654 chain wear on newer models. Neither has a unique reliability advantage over the other.

Is the CLA worth the extra money over the A-Class?

Only if you value the styling above everything else. The CLA typically costs £2,000–£4,000 more than an equivalent A-Class, with no mechanical or practical benefits. The Shooting Brake version adds estate practicality, which changes the calculation — but the standard CLA is a style purchase, not a value purchase.

Do the A-Class and CLA share the same engines?

Yes — both are built on the MFA platform and share the OM651 diesel and M270/M274 petrol engines. AMG versions (A35, A45, CLA35, CLA45) share the M133 and M139 engines. Replacement costs are identical.

Which holds its value better, A-Class or CLA?

The A-Class tends to depreciate more slowly as a percentage of its lower purchase price, but the CLA's higher starting price means it loses more in absolute terms. The Shooting Brake version holds value better than the standard four-door CLA due to its niche appeal and estate practicality.
