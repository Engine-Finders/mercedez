Diesel vs Petrol Running Costs

The definitive guide to Mercedes diesel vs petrol running costs in 2025. Fuel economy, tax, servicing, DPF, AdBlue and the real cost of ownership - with a clear verdict based on real UK data.

Petrol wins for most UK buyers in 2025. Diesel's fuel economy advantage has eroded with higher fuel prices, while petrol engines are cheaper to service, avoid DPF and AdBlue complexities, and face fewer urban restrictions. Diesel only makes sense for high-mileage motorway drivers (over 15,000 miles per year) where the fuel savings genuinely offset the higher running costs.

The Verdict

Petrol is the better choice for most UK buyers in 2025. The traditional advantages of diesel - lower fuel cost per mile and better fuel economy - have been eroded by higher diesel prices, increasing maintenance costs (DPF, AdBlue, injector servicing), and growing urban restrictions. For the average driver doing 8,000–12,000 miles per year, the petrol engine will be cheaper to own over three to five years [PATTERN DATA].

Diesel remains the better choice for high-mileage motorway commuters (over 15,000 miles per year) where the fuel savings genuinely offset the higher servicing and repair costs. The OM651 and OM654 diesels are excellent engines for long-distance work - quiet, refined, and extraordinarily efficient at a steady 70 mph. But for most buyers, petrol's simplicity and lower running costs make it the smarter decision.

Diesel Deep Dive

Mercedes diesel engines - particularly the OM651 (2.1L four-cylinder) and OM654 (2.0L four-cylinder) - are among the most popular engines in the UK. The OM651 alone generated 1,250 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], fitted across the C-Class, E-Class, GLC, and GLA. The diesel's real-world fuel economy (45–55 mpg mixed) is its primary advantage, with motorway cruising often exceeding 55 mpg. The CO2 advantage also means lower VED (road tax) on older models.

However, diesel running costs are significantly higher than petrol. Servicing is more expensive (20–30% higher) due to DPF regeneration cycles, AdBlue top-ups, and more complex injector systems. The OM651 is also known for timing chain tensioner wear, and the OM642 V6 (in larger models) suffers from oil cooler seal and swirl flap failures [PATTERN DATA]. Diesel's DPF system requires regular long runs to regenerate properly - short urban journeys will clog the DPF and lead to expensive repairs (£400–£1,200) [EM-QUOTE]. Full details on the OM651 Knowledge Centre and OM654 Knowledge Centre.

Petrol Deep Dive

Mercedes petrol engines - particularly the M274 (2.0L four-cylinder) and M276 (3.0L V6) - are less common but increasingly popular. The M276 generated 390 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], fitted across the E-Class, CLS, and GLE. Petrol's real-world fuel economy (30–38 mpg mixed) is lower than diesel, but fuel prices are similar or lower, narrowing the cost-per-mile gap significantly.

The major advantage of petrol is lower maintenance costs. Petrol engines don't have DPF or AdBlue systems, so servicing is simpler and cheaper. The M276's known weakness is water pump and cooling system issues, but these are generally less catastrophic than diesel's DPF and injector failures [PATTERN DATA]. Petrol engines are also better suited to urban driving - they warm up faster, don't require DPF regeneration, and face fewer restrictions in low-emission zones. Full details on the M276 Knowledge Centre and M274 Knowledge Centre.

Five-Year Cost Comparison

Over a typical five-year ownership period (10,000 miles per year), the petrol engine is cheaper to own. The diesel's fuel advantage is around £200–£300 per year, but this is offset by higher servicing costs (£100–£200 per year), higher repair costs (DPF regeneration, AdBlue, injector servicing), and the risk of a catastrophic DPF or injector failure (£500–£1,200). The diesel's higher purchase price (typically £1,000–£2,000 more for an equivalent car) also adds to the ownership cost.

For high-mileage drivers (over 15,000 miles per year), the diesel's fuel savings begin to outweigh the higher running costs. At 20,000 miles per year, the diesel saves £600–£800 per year in fuel, which easily covers the higher servicing and repair costs. The decision is fundamentally about your annual mileage and driving pattern [PATTERN DATA]. Which Should You Buy?

🛣️

Buy Diesel if…

You cover over 15,000 miles per year, mostly on motorways. The fuel savings will offset the higher running costs, and the DPF will stay clear with regular long runs.

🏙️

Buy Petrol if…

You do mostly urban or mixed driving, or your annual mileage is under 12,000 miles. Petrol is cheaper to run, simpler to maintain, and faces fewer restrictions.

⚠️

Avoid Diesel if…

You mainly do short urban trips. The DPF will clog, leading to expensive repairs, and you'll never see the fuel economy benefits. Petrol is the better choice.

🌍

ULEZ and Clean Air Zones

Most modern Euro 6 diesels (2016+) are ULEZ-compliant, but older diesels (Euro 5 and below) face daily charges. Petrol cars generally face fewer restrictions.

The key takeaway: mileage is the deciding factor. If you're doing high mileage on motorways, diesel still makes sense. For everyone else, petrol is the smarter, cheaper, and simpler choice in 2025.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is diesel or petrol cheaper to run in 2025?

Petrol is cheaper for most drivers. While diesel offers better fuel economy, higher fuel prices, more expensive servicing, and DPF/AdBlue maintenance costs offset the savings. Diesel only makes financial sense for high-mileage motorway drivers doing over 15,000 miles per year.

Which is more reliable, diesel or petrol Mercedes?

Petrol engines are generally more reliable. They have fewer emissions-related systems (no DPF, AdBlue, or complex injectors) and fewer catastrophic failure modes. The OM651 diesel chain tensioner and OM642 oil cooler failures are well-documented; petrol M276 water pump issues are less severe and cheaper to fix.

Is diesel worth it for low mileage?

No. For low mileage (under 10,000 miles per year), diesel's higher fuel cost, higher servicing cost, and DPF clogging risk make it a poor choice. Petrol is cheaper, simpler, and better suited to urban driving.

Are Mercedes diesels ULEZ-compliant?

Most Euro 6 diesels (2016 onwards) are ULEZ-compliant. Older Euro 5 diesels (2015 and earlier) are not compliant and face daily charges in London and other clean air zones. Check the specific vehicle's Euro standard before buying.

What's the typical fuel consumption difference?

A diesel OM651 will typically return 45–55 mpg in mixed driving, with motorway runs exceeding 55 mpg. A petrol M274/M276 will return 30–38 mpg in mixed driving. The difference is about 15–20 mpg, but diesel fuel is more expensive, narrowing the cost gap.
