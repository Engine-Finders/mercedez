The Verdict

The GLC is the smarter choice for the overwhelming majority of buyers. It offers nearly all the same tech, engines and refinement as the GLE, but in a smaller, lighter package that costs less to buy, tax, insure and run. The GLE's extra space and presence only become essential if you regularly carry five adults plus luggage, tow a heavy trailer, or simply prefer the taller, more commanding drive. Our data shows GLC enquiries outnumber GLE by 2:1 — a clear reflection of its broader appeal.

GLC (X253 / X254) — The Compact All‑Rounder

The GLC is Mercedes' best‑selling SUV globally, and for good reason. It blends car‑like handling with SUV practicality, offering a spacious cabin for four adults and a boot that easily swallows a family's weekly shop. Engine choices mirror the GLE: the OM654 2.0‑litre diesel (194–265 bhp) is the most popular, delivering 44–48 mpg in real‑world driving, while the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (362 bhp) and plug‑in hybrid options cater to different needs. The GLC is lighter, more agile and cheaper to service than its larger sibling — and our data confirms it's the more financially sensible choice for most households. Key risks are shared with the GLE: AdBlue system issues, timing chain stretch on the petrol, and carbon build‑up. OM654 Knowledge Centre and M276 Knowledge Centre cover the engine‑specific detail. The GLC's smaller footprint also makes it easier to park and manoeuvre in urban environments, a significant advantage for city dwellers.

GLE (W166 / V167) — The Large‑Scale Cruiser

The GLE sits a full size above the GLC, offering significantly more interior space, a larger boot (690 litres) and the option of third‑row seating, making it a genuine seven‑seater. It shares the same engine family, but the extra weight (around 200–300 kg) reduces fuel economy by 4–5 mpg across the board. The GLE also offers the more powerful OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (258 bhp) in older W166 models — a robust engine but one with known swirl flap issues — while newer V167 models use the OM654 and the inline‑six petrol M256. The GLE's ride is more refined on motorways, with optional air suspension smoothing out rougher roads. However, its size makes it less wieldy in town, and running costs are higher across the board. The GLE is the right choice if you regularly carry a full car of passengers or tow a caravan, horsebox or heavy trailer — its towing capacity of up to 3,500 kg far exceeds the GLC's 2,000 kg limit. For owners who need that capability, the extra cost is easily justified. OM642 swirl flap page and M276 Knowledge Centre provide more detail.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The biggest practical difference between the GLC and GLE is the cost of ownership. The GLC is cheaper to buy, cheaper to tax (lower CO₂), cheaper to insure (lower group) and cheaper to run on fuel — by about 10–15% per mile. Over a typical five‑year ownership period of 60,000 miles, the GLC can save you £2,500–£4,000 compared to the GLE, depending on engine and specification. That saving is mainly driven by fuel, but also by lower servicing costs (less oil, smaller filters) and cheaper tyre and brake part prices. However, if you need the GLE's extra space or towing capability, those savings become irrelevant — the GLE is the only choice for those jobs. Our repair vs replace framework applies equally to both: if a major engine failure occurs, the GLC's lower replacement cost (£3,000–£5,500 vs £3,200–£6,500) makes it more likely to be financially viable to repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the GLC bigger than the GLE?

No — the GLE is noticeably larger in every dimension: longer, wider, taller, with a bigger boot and more rear legroom. The GLC is a compact SUV, while the GLE is a mid‑size/large SUV.

Which is more reliable, GLC or GLE?

They share the same engines and core components, so reliability is broadly identical. The GLE's extra weight may cause slightly faster wear on brakes and suspension, but the difference is marginal.

Which is cheaper to insure, GLC or GLE?

The GLC is cheaper to insure as it sits in lower insurance groups, reflecting its lower value, lower power outputs (in most variants) and lower repair costs. Expect a 10–20% premium for the GLE.

Can the GLC tow a caravan?

Yes, the GLC can tow up to 2,000 kg, which covers many medium‑sized caravans and trailers. The GLE's 3,500 kg towing capacity is better suited for heavier caravans, horseboxes or large trailers.

Which is better for a family of five?

If you regularly carry five adults, the GLE's extra width and rear legroom make it far more comfortable. For a family with younger children, the GLC is perfectly adequate.
