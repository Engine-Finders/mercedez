The Verdict

The GLC is the better choice for almost everyone. It's more modern, more efficient, safer and more spacious than the GLK, with lower running costs and a far more refined driving experience. The GLK's only remaining advantage is its lower purchase price and, for some, its more utilitarian, boxy design. If you're on a tight budget and find a well‑maintained GLK with full service history, it can still be a decent buy — but be prepared for higher fuel bills and the potential for age‑related repairs. For the vast majority, the GLC's extra upfront cost is easily justified by lower long‑term ownership costs and better resale value.

GLC (X253 / X254) — The Modern Compact SUV

The GLC is Mercedes' current compact SUV, launched in 2015 (X253) and updated in 2022 (X254). It shares its platform with the C‑Class, offering a car‑like driving experience with SUV practicality. Engine choices are modern and efficient: the OM654 2.0‑litre diesel (194–265 bhp) delivers 44–48 mpg in real‑world use, while the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (362 bhp) offers smooth power for those who prefer petrol. The GLC also comes as a plug‑in hybrid, which can further reduce running costs for urban drivers. Key reliability risks are well‑documented: AdBlue/SCR system faults, EGR cooler leaks, and timing chain stretch on the petrol V6 — all covered in our OM654 and M276 Knowledge Centres. The GLC's interior is significantly larger than the GLK's, with more rear legroom and a bigger boot (550 litres vs 450 litres). It also features modern safety tech, Apple CarPlay, and a much more refined ride. Our data shows 526 enquiries in 2025, making it one of the most‑requested Mercedes SUVs in the UK.

GLK (X204) — The Rugged Original

The GLK was Mercedes' first compact SUV, produced from 2008 to 2015 (facelift in 2012). Its boxy, angular design gave it a rugged, off‑road appearance that still has a loyal following. Under the bonnet, you'll find the OM651 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (170–204 bhp) or the OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (231–265 bhp) in the GLK 350 CDI. The OM651 is the more common and generally reliable engine, though it has known balance‑shaft issues on earlier models. The OM642 is smooth and torquey but prone to swirl flap failures — a costly repair if not caught early (see our OM642 swirl flap page). The GLK's interior is dated, with smaller screens and less space than the GLC, but it's still well‑built and comfortable. Its real‑world fuel economy is lower: 35–40 mpg for the V6 and 42–46 mpg for the four‑cylinder, so running costs are higher. Enquiry volumes in 2025 were just 152, reflecting its age and the shift in buyer preference towards newer models. However, for buyers on a tight budget, a well‑maintained GLK can still be a reliable workhorse, especially the OM651 version.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The GLC is cheaper to own over a typical five‑year period, despite its higher purchase price. Lower fuel consumption, lower tax (VED), and lower insurance groups make a significant difference. The GLK's lower entry price is quickly eroded by higher fuel bills and the risk of age‑related repairs — especially on the OM642 V6. Our data shows that GLC owners typically spend £1,500–£2,500 less on running costs over five years than GLK owners, assuming 10,000 miles per year. That saving more than offsets the GLC's higher initial purchase cost, making it the more financially sensible choice in the long run. For those set on a GLK, we recommend choosing the OM651 four‑cylinder diesel over the OM642 V6, as it's cheaper to maintain, more fuel‑efficient, and has fewer known catastrophic failure modes. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the GLC bigger than the GLK?

Yes — the GLC is longer, wider and has more interior space, particularly rear legroom and boot capacity (550 litres vs 450 litres). The GLC also has a more modern, aerodynamic design.

Which is more reliable, GLC or GLK?

The GLC uses newer engine designs (OM654, M276) that have fewer fundamental flaws than the older OM642 and early OM651 units. However, the GLK's OM651 (post‑2012) can be very reliable if properly maintained. The GLC's AdBlue/SCR system adds complexity not present on the GLK.

Which is cheaper to insure, GLC or GLK?

The GLK is generally cheaper to insure because its lower market value and older design place it in lower insurance groups. The GLC's higher value and more powerful engine options push premiums up slightly.

Should I buy a GLK with the V6 diesel?

Only if you're prepared for potential swirl flap and oil cooler seal repairs, which can be costly. The four‑cylinder OM651 is more reliable and cheaper to run; we recommend prioritising that engine in a GLK.

Is the GLC worth the extra money over a GLK?

For most buyers, yes. The GLC is more fuel‑efficient, safer, more spacious and has far better resale value. The extra upfront cost is recouped over a few years of lower running costs, making it a better long‑term investment.
