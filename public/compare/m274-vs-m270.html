The Verdict

The M274 is the better engine for the vast majority of buyers. It's more powerful (in most variants), more fuel‑efficient, more refined and used in newer, more desirable Mercedes models. The M270 is an older design with known timing chain stretch issues that can be catastrophic. While the M270's lower entry price is appealing, the M274's superior reliability and efficiency make it a better long‑term investment. Unless you're specifically chasing the older engine's character or are on a very tight budget, the M274 is the clear winner.

M270 - The Proven Turbo Four

The M270 is Mercedes' 2.0‑litre turbo petrol engine, produced from 2011 to 2019. It was used across the compact range: A‑Class (W176), B‑Class (W246), CLA (C117), GLA (X156) and GLC (X253). It produces 181–208 bhp and is known for its punchy performance and decent fuel economy. The engine's biggest weakness is timing chain stretch - the chain can stretch and cause timing issues, leading to poor running and, in severe cases, catastrophic engine failure. Carbon build‑up on intake valves (due to direct injection) and fuel injector failures are also known issues. Replacement cost for a reconditioned unit is £1,500–£2,800 [EM‑QUOTE], making it relatively affordable to replace. It's an engine that can still be a good buy if you find one with the timing chain already addressed. See our M270 Knowledge Centre for more detail.

M274 - The Evolved Turbo Four

The M274 is Mercedes' 2.0‑litre turbo petrol engine, introduced in 2013 and used until 2021. It's a significant evolution of the four‑cylinder concept, with better fuel economy, lower emissions and improved refinement. The M274 is used in the C‑Class (W205), E‑Class (W213), GLC (X253), GLE (W166) and SLC (R172). Power outputs range from 181 to 241 bhp depending on the variant. The M274 is more fuel‑efficient (38–42 mpg vs 35–39 mpg) and has lower CO₂ emissions than the M270. Known issues include timing chain tensioner wear (especially on earlier models), water pump failure, and carbon build‑up on intake valves. These issues are generally less severe than the M270's timing chain stretch problem. Replacement cost is slightly higher at £2,000–£3,800 [EM‑QUOTE], reflecting the engine's newer design and higher parts costs. The M274 is the engine to choose if you want modern performance, refinement and lower running costs. Our M274 Knowledge Centre has full specs and fitment details.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The M274 is cheaper to run than the M270 over a typical five‑year period, despite similar purchase prices for comparable models. The M274's better fuel economy saves around £200–£400 per year in fuel costs, and its superior reliability (fewer catastrophic failure modes) means fewer unexpected repair bills. The M270's lower entry price on older models is tempting, but the risk of timing chain stretch (costing £1,500–£3,000 to fix) can quickly erode any upfront saving. Over 50,000 miles, the M274 typically costs its owner £1,000–£2,000 less to run than the M270, making it the more financially sensible choice in the long run. For those on a tight budget, a well‑maintained M270 with documented timing chain work can still be a good option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which four‑cylinder is more reliable, M270 or M274?

The M274 is more reliable. The M270 suffers from timing chain stretch that can be catastrophic. The M274's timing chain tensioner issues are generally less severe and easier to catch early.

Which is cheaper to run, M270 or M274?

The M274 is cheaper to run - it uses less fuel (38–42 mpg vs 35–39 mpg) and has lower servicing costs. The M270's lower purchase price is offset by higher fuel bills and the risk of timing chain repairs.

Which has more tuning potential, M270 or M274?

Both have strong tuning potential, but the M274's more advanced engine management and turbocharger design make it easier to extract reliable power. Up to 300 bhp is achievable with a remap and hardware upgrades.

Can I fit an M274 into an older Mercedes?

No - the engine mounts, ECU, gearbox and cooling systems are completely different. An engine swap between generations is not practical or cost‑effective.

Is the M274 timing chain issue as bad as the M270?

No - the M274 timing chain tensioner wear is generally less severe, more predictable and easier to catch early. The M270 timing chain stretch can happen suddenly and without warning, often destroying the engine.
