The Verdict

The OM651 is the better engine for the vast majority of buyers. It's more powerful, more fuel‑efficient, more refined and used in newer, more desirable Mercedes models. The OM646 is a proven engine but its age shows in higher running costs and the increasing scarcity of parts. Unless you're on a very tight budget or specifically want a simpler, older diesel, the OM651 is the clear winner.

OM646 — The Proven 2.0 Diesel

The OM646 is Mercedes' 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel engine, produced from 2003 to 2011. It was used extensively across the range: C‑Class (W203/W204), E‑Class (W211/W212), CLK (C209), ML (W164), Sprinter and Vito. It produces 120–170 bhp depending on the variant, with peak torque of 270–400 Nm. The engine is known for its robustness and simplicity compared to newer units. The most common failure is injector seal leakage — the famous "Black Death" — where carbon builds up around the injector, leading to poor running, smoking and potential engine damage if left unchecked. Timing chain wear and EGR clogging are also common. These issues are generally well‑understood by independent specialists and relatively inexpensive to address. Replacement cost for a reconditioned unit is £2,500–£4,000 [PATTERN DATA], making it one of the more affordable engines to replace. The OM646 is a good choice for budget‑conscious buyers who want a straightforward, reliable diesel and don't mind the older driving experience. See our OM646 Knowledge Centre and OM646 injector seal failure page for more detail.

OM651 — The Modern 2.1 Diesel

The OM651 is Mercedes' 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel engine, introduced in 2008 and used until 2021. It's a significant evolution of the diesel concept, with better fuel economy, lower emissions and improved refinement. The OM651 is used in the C‑Class (W204/W205), E‑Class (W212/W213), GLC (X253), GLE (W166), CLS (C218), S‑Class (W222), Sprinter and Vito. Power outputs range from 136 to 204 bhp depending on the variant — the 204 bhp version in the C250 CDI is a particularly sweet spot. The OM651 is more fuel‑efficient (48–52 mpg vs 44–48 mpg) and has lower CO₂ emissions than the OM646. Known issues include balance shaft wear (especially on earlier models), timing chain tensioner failure, and injector seal issues. These can be more expensive to fix than the OM646's issues. Replacement cost is slightly higher at £2,800–£4,800 [EM‑QUOTE]. The OM651 is the engine to choose if you want modern diesel performance, refinement and lower running costs. Our OM651 Knowledge Centre and OM651 balance shaft failure page have full details.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The OM651 is cheaper to run than the OM646 over a typical five‑year period, despite slightly higher replacement costs. The OM651's better fuel economy saves around £200–£400 per year in fuel costs, and its more modern design means lower servicing costs in some areas. The OM646's lower entry price on older models is tempting, but the risk of injector seal failures and EGR issues — while relatively inexpensive to fix — can still add up. Over 50,000 miles, the OM651 typically costs its owner £1,000–£1,500 less to run than the OM646, making it the more financially sensible choice in the long run. For those on a tight budget, a well‑maintained OM646 with documented injector seal work can still be a good option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which diesel is more reliable, OM646 or OM651?

The OM646 is arguably more robust in some ways — its main issue (injector seals) is relatively cheap and easy to fix. The OM651's balance shaft wear can be a more expensive repair. However, the OM651 is a more modern engine with better overall engineering. It's a trade‑off between simplicity and refinement.

Which is cheaper to run, OM646 or OM651?

The OM651 is cheaper to run — it uses less fuel (48–52 mpg vs 44–48 mpg) and has lower emissions. The OM646's lower purchase price is offset by slightly higher fuel bills.

Which has more tuning potential, OM646 or OM651?

The OM651 has more tuning potential due to its more advanced engine management and common‑rail system. Up to 250 bhp is achievable with a remap and hardware upgrades. The OM646 is less tunable.

Can I fit an OM651 into an older Mercedes?

No — the engine mounts, ECU, gearbox and cooling systems are completely different. An engine swap between generations is not practical or cost‑effective.

Is the OM651 balance shaft issue as bad as the OM646 injector seal?

No — the OM646 injector seal issue is a relatively straightforward, low‑cost repair. The OM651 balance shaft wear can be a more significant repair, costing £1,000–£2,000 to fix. However, the OM651 is generally a more refined and efficient engine.
