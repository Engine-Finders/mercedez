The Verdict

The OM651 is the better engine for the vast majority of buyers. It offers excellent performance, class‑leading fuel economy and much lower running costs than the OM642. The OM642 is a smooth, torquey V6 that delivers effortless refinement, but its thirst and the risk of expensive swirl flap failures make it a specialist choice. Unless you specifically want the V6's smoothness and torque for towing or cruising, the OM651 is the smarter, more practical choice.

OM651 — The Efficient 2.1 Diesel

The OM651 is Mercedes' 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel, introduced in 2008 and used until 2021. It's one of the most widely used diesel engines in the Mercedes range, fitted to C‑Class (W204/W205), E‑Class (W212/W213), GLC (X253), GLE (W166), CLS (C218), S‑Class (W222), Sprinter and Vito. Power outputs range from 136 to 204 bhp, with peak torque of 350–500 Nm. The engine is known for its excellent fuel economy (48–52 mpg in real‑world driving) and strong performance. Key issues include balance shaft wear (especially on earlier models), timing chain tensioner failure, and injector seal leaks. These are generally well‑understood by independent specialists. Replacement cost is £2,800–£4,800 [EM‑QUOTE], making it a relatively affordable engine to maintain. The OM651 is the engine to choose if you want modern diesel performance without V6 running costs. See our OM651 Knowledge Centre and OM651 balance shaft failure page for more detail.

OM642 — The Smooth V6 Diesel

The OM642 is Mercedes' 3.0‑litre V6 diesel, produced from 2005 to 2019. It's the engine of choice for those who want smooth, effortless power and the refinement of a V6. It was used in the E‑Class (W211/W212), S‑Class (W221), CLS (C218), GLE (W166), GLS (X166), ML (W164/W166), GL (X164/X166), Sprinter and Vito. Power outputs range from 190 to 265 bhp, with peak torque of 440–620 Nm. The OM642 is a torquey engine that makes long journeys effortless. However, it's also thirsty — 35–40 mpg in real‑world driving — and has some well‑known failure points. The most infamous is swirl flap failure — the intake manifold flaps can break and be ingested into the engine, causing catastrophic damage. Oil cooler seal leaks (requiring significant labour to access) and turbocharger issues are also common. Replacement cost is £3,200–£5,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. The OM642 is for those who want V6 refinement and torque and are prepared to pay for it. Our OM642 Knowledge Centre and OM642 swirl flap failure page have full details.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The OM651 is significantly cheaper to own than the OM642 over a typical five‑year period. The difference is driven primarily by fuel — the OM651's 48–52 mpg saves around £1,200–£1,800 per year compared to the OM642's 35–40 mpg. Servicing costs are also lower on the four‑cylinder, as it uses less oil and has fewer components. The OM642's higher insurance premiums and VED add to the cost gap. Over 50,000 miles, the OM651 typically costs its owner £4,000–£6,000 less to run than the OM642, making it the more financially sensible choice for the vast majority of buyers. However, if you want the smooth V6 experience and can afford the extra costs, the OM642 remains an exceptional engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which diesel is more reliable, OM651 or OM642?

The OM651 is generally more reliable, with fewer catastrophic failure modes. The OM642's swirl flap failure can cause catastrophic engine damage. The OM651's balance shaft issues are less severe and more predictable.

Which is cheaper to run, OM651 or OM642?

The OM651 is significantly cheaper to run — it uses much less fuel (48–52 mpg vs 35–40 mpg) and has lower servicing and insurance costs. The OM642 is a thirsty V6.

Which sounds better, OM651 or OM642?

The OM642 V6 has a smooth, refined diesel note that the four‑cylinder can't match. If engine sound matters to you, the V6 wins.

Can I fit an OM642 into a car that originally had an OM651?

No — the engine mounts, ECU, gearbox, cooling and exhaust systems are completely different. An engine swap between 4‑cyl and V6 platforms is not practical or cost‑effective.

Is the OM651 powerful enough for a large car like the S‑Class?

Yes — the OM651 in its higher‑output forms (204 bhp) provides more than adequate performance for the S‑Class and GLE. The OM642 offers extra refinement and torque, but the OM651 is perfectly capable.
