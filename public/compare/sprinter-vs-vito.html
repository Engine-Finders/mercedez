The Verdict

The Vito is the better choice for the vast majority of UK van buyers. It offers a perfect balance of size, load capacity and running costs. The Sprinter's only advantage is its massive load volume and higher payload, making it essential only for those who genuinely need to carry huge amounts of goods or convert the van into a camper or specialist vehicle. For most tradespeople, delivery drivers and families, the Vito is the smarter, more practical and more cost‑effective choice.

Vito - The Versatile Mid‑Sized Van

The Vito is Mercedes' mid‑sized van, produced from 2003 to the present (currently in its W447 generation, 2014–present). It sits between the smaller Citan and the larger Sprinter, offering a car‑like driving experience with genuine load‑carrying capability. The Vito is available in panel van, crew cab and passenger car (V‑Class) forms, making it exceptionally versatile. The primary engine is the OM651 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (136–204 bhp), delivering 44–48 mpg in real‑world driving - excellent economy for a van of its size. It's also available with the OM622 1.7‑litre diesel in older models, though the OM651 is the engine to choose for its reliability and performance. Key issues include OM651 balance shaft wear (especially on earlier pre‑2012 models), injector seal failure, and EGR clogging from urban driving. These are well‑understood by independent specialists. Replacement cost for a reconditioned OM651 is £2,500–£4,500 [PATTERN DATA]. The Vito's smaller dimensions make it easy to park in urban areas, while its load volume (up to 6.6 m³) is ample for most tradespeople and delivery drivers. For full details, see our OM651 Knowledge Centre and Vito model pages.

Sprinter - The Full‑Sized Workhorse

The Sprinter is Mercedes' full‑sized van, produced from 1995 to the present (currently in its W907 generation, 2018–present). It's the van of choice for large‑volume haulage, removals, fleet operations and campervan conversions. The Sprinter is available in a huge range of sizes, from the compact (W906) to the mega‑sized (W907 extra‑long high‑roof), with load volumes from 9 m³ to over 17 m³ and payloads from 1,000 kg to over 2,500 kg. Engine choices include the OM651 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (136–204 bhp) and the OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (190–265 bhp) in older models. The OM651 is the more common and efficient choice, delivering 30–35 mpg in real‑world driving - significantly lower than the Vito due to the Sprinter's extra weight. The OM642 offers more torque for heavy loads but is thirstier and has its own issues (see our OM642 swirl flap failure page). Key issues across both engines include OM651 balance shaft wear, DPF clogging from urban driving, and injector seal failure. Replacement cost for a reconditioned OM651 is £2,800–£4,800 [EM‑QUOTE]. The Sprinter is a specialist tool - essential for those who need its capacity, but overkill for most. For more detail, see our OM651 Knowledge Centre, OM642 Knowledge Centre and Sprinter model pages.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The Vito is significantly cheaper to own than the Sprinter over a typical five‑year period, due to lower fuel consumption, lower VED, cheaper insurance and lower servicing costs. The Sprinter's extra size and weight drive up fuel costs (around £1,000–£1,500 more per year), and its larger tyres, brakes and suspension components are more expensive to replace. Over 60,000 miles, the Vito typically saves its owner £2,500–£4,000 compared to the Sprinter, assuming comparable engine choices and driving patterns. However, for businesses that genuinely need the Sprinter's capacity, the extra cost is easily justified by the increased revenue potential. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sprinter bigger than the Vito?

Yes - the Sprinter is significantly larger in every dimension: longer, wider, taller, with a much larger load volume. The Sprinter is a full‑sized van; the Vito is a mid‑sized van.

Which is more reliable, Sprinter or Vito?

They share many engines (OM651), so reliability is broadly similar. However, the Vito is generally simpler and cheaper to repair due to less weight and strain on components. The Sprinter's heavier usage can lead to faster wear on suspension, brakes and gearbox.

Which is cheaper to insure, Sprinter or Vito?

The Vito is cheaper to insure due to its lower value and lower repair costs. Expect a 15–25% premium for the Sprinter, reflecting its higher risk profile for commercial use.

Can the Vito be converted to a campervan?

Yes - the Vito is a popular base for smaller camper conversions, particularly the extra‑long (L2) version. However, the Sprinter offers far more space for a full‑size camper layout.

Which is better for urban driving?

Vito - its smaller dimensions make it far easier to park and manoeuvre in city streets. The Sprinter's size is a disadvantage in urban environments.
