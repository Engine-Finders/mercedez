The Verdict

The W205 is the better car for the vast majority of buyers. It offers superior refinement, better fuel economy, a much nicer interior and more modern technology. The W204 is a good car, but it's now two generations old and its age shows. The W205's higher purchase price is easily justified by its better driving experience, lower running costs and stronger resale value. Unless you're on a very tight budget or specifically want a simpler, older car, the W205 is the clear winner.

W204 - The Traditional C‑Class

The W204 is the third generation of the Mercedes C‑Class, produced from 2007 to 2014 (with a facelift in 2011). It was available as a saloon (W204), estate (S204) and coupe (C204). The W204 was the first C‑Class to feature direct‑injection petrol engines and a more dynamic chassis. Engine choices include the OM651 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (136–204 bhp), the M271 1.8‑litre turbo petrol (156–204 bhp), the M272 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (228–268 bhp) and the M276 3.5‑litre V6 petrol (302 bhp). The W204 is known for its robust build quality and good handling, but its interior is dated by modern standards. Key issues include OM651 balance shaft wear, injector seal failure ("Black Death"), timing chain stretch on the M271, and balance shaft wear on the M272. The W204 remains a popular choice for budget‑conscious buyers - our 2025 data shows 252 enquiries, reflecting its continued appeal. For a detailed breakdown of the engines, see our OM651 Knowledge Centre, M271 Knowledge Centre and M272 Knowledge Centre.

W205 - The Modern C‑Class

The W205 is the fourth generation of the Mercedes C‑Class, produced from 2014 to 2021 (with a facelift in 2018). It was available as a saloon (W205), estate (S205) and coupe (C205). The W205 sits on an all‑new platform and is significantly more refined than the W204, with a much more premium interior inspired by the S‑Class. Engine choices include the OM654 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (150–194 bhp) in later models, the OM651 2.1‑litre diesel (136–204 bhp) in earlier models, the M274 2.0‑litre turbo petrol (181–245 bhp) and the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (255–362 bhp) in the C43 AMG. The W205 is more fuel‑efficient (48–52 mpg on the OM654), more refined and better equipped. Key issues include OM654 AdBlue system faults and EGR cooler leaks, M274 timing chain tensioner wear, and M276 timing chain stretch. The W205 is the more desirable car - our data shows 556 enquiries in 2025, more than double the W204. For full engine details, see our OM654 Knowledge Centre, M274 Knowledge Centre and M276 Knowledge Centre.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The W205 is cheaper to own than the W204 over a typical five‑year period, despite its higher purchase price. The W205's better fuel economy saves around £200–£400 per year, and its more modern emissions systems mean lower VED in many cases. The W204's lower entry price is tempting, but the risk of OM651 balance shaft wear (£1,000–£2,000 to fix) or M272 balance shaft failure (£2,500–£4,500) can quickly erode any upfront saving. The W205's AdBlue/EGR issues are generally less expensive to fix and more predictable. Over 50,000 miles, the W205 typically costs its owner £1,000–£1,500 less to run than the W204, making it the more financially sensible choice in the long run. For those on a tight budget, a well‑maintained W204 with documented engine work can still be a good option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the W205 better than the W204?

Yes - the W205 is more refined, more efficient, has a better interior and more modern technology. The W205 is a significant step up in quality and driving experience. The W204 is a good car for its age, but the W205 is the better car in almost every way.

Which is more reliable, W204 or W205?

They have different issues. The W204's OM651 balance shaft and M272 balance shaft can be expensive. The W205's OM654 AdBlue issues are less catastrophic but can be frustrating. Overall, the W205's more modern design gives it better long‑term reliability, but both need careful maintenance.

Which is cheaper to insure, W204 or W205?

The W204 is generally cheaper to insure due to its lower value and older design. Expect a 10–15% premium for the W205, reflecting its higher value and more powerful engine options.

Is the W204 still a good car to buy in 2026?

Yes - if you're on a tight budget and find a well‑maintained example with full service history, the W204 can still be a reliable and enjoyable car. Just budget for potential engine repairs and be prepared for higher running costs than the W205.

Is the W205 worth the extra money over the W204?

For most buyers, yes - the W205 offers a much better driving experience, lower running costs and stronger resale value. The extra upfront cost is easily justified over a few years of ownership.
