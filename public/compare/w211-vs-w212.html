The Verdict

The W212 is the better car for the vast majority of buyers. It offers superior refinement, better handling, a much nicer interior and more modern technology. The W212 is a significant step up in quality and driving experience. The W211 is a good car for its age, but the W212 is the better car in almost every way. Unless you're on a very tight budget or specifically want the older E‑Class styling, the W212 is the clear winner.

W211 — The Classic E‑Class

The W211 is the third generation of the Mercedes E‑Class, produced from 2003 to 2009 (with a facelift in 2006). It was available as a saloon (W211) and estate (S211). The W211 is known for its classic E‑Class styling, comfortable ride and robust build quality. Engine choices include the OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (190–231 bhp) and the M272 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (228–268 bhp). The W211 is the last E‑Class to offer the traditional Mercedes grille with the standing star (pre‑facelift) and the first with the integrated grille (facelift). Key issues include OM642 swirl flap failure, oil cooler seal leaks, and injector seal failure. The M272 also suffers from balance shaft wear on pre‑2010 engines. The W211 remains a popular choice for budget‑conscious buyers — our 2025 data shows 234 enquiries. For more detail, see our OM642 Knowledge Centre and M272 Knowledge Centre.

W212 — The Modern E‑Class

The W212 is the fourth generation of the Mercedes E‑Class, produced from 2009 to 2016 (with a facelift in 2013). It was available as a saloon (W212) and estate (S212). The W212 is a significant step forward from the W211, with a sharper, more angular design, improved handling and a much more modern interior. Engine choices include the OM651 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (136–204 bhp), the OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (190–265 bhp) and the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (255–362 bhp). The OM651 four‑cylinder diesel offers excellent fuel economy (48–52 mpg) and strong performance. Key issues include OM651 balance shaft wear, OM642 swirl flap failure, and timing chain stretch on the M276. The W212 is more reliable overall and holds its value better. Our data shows 338 enquiries in 2025, reflecting its stronger demand. For more, see our OM651 Knowledge Centre, OM642 Knowledge Centre and M276 Knowledge Centre.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The W212 is cheaper to own than the W211 over a typical five‑year period, despite its higher purchase price. The W212's better fuel economy (especially with the OM651) saves around £200–£400 per year, and its more modern emissions systems mean lower VED in many cases. The W211's lower entry price is tempting, but the risk of OM642 swirl flap failure (£500–£1,200) and oil cooler seal leaks (£800–£1,500) can quickly add up. The W212's OM651 balance shaft is a known issue, but it's less common and generally less expensive than the OM642's swirl flap problems. Over 50,000 miles, the W212 typically costs its owner £1,000–£1,500 less to run than the W211, making it the more financially sensible choice in the long run. For those on a tight budget, a well‑maintained W211 with documented engine work can still be a good option.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the W212 better than the W211?

Yes — the W212 is more refined, better built, and offers a more modern driving experience. The W212 is a significant step up in quality and technology. The W211 is a classic but the W212 is the better car in almost every way.

Which is more reliable, W211 or W212?

The W212 is generally more reliable, with fewer major issues. The W211's OM642 swirl flap and oil cooler seal problems are common and expensive. The W212's OM651 balance shaft is less common and less catastrophic. Both need careful maintenance.

Which is cheaper to insure, W211 or W212?

The W211 is generally cheaper to insure due to its lower value and older design. Expect a 10–15% premium for the W212, reflecting its higher value and more powerful engine options.

Is the W211 still a good car to buy in 2026?

Yes — if you're on a tight budget and find a well‑maintained example with full service history, the W211 can still be a reliable and enjoyable car. Just budget for potential OM642 repairs and be prepared for higher running costs than the W212.

Is the W212 worth the extra money over the W211?

For most buyers, yes — the W212 offers a much better driving experience, lower running costs and stronger resale value. The extra upfront cost is easily justified over a few years of ownership.
