The Verdict

The W214 is the better car for buyers who can afford it. It offers superior refinement, better technology, a more modern interior and stronger performance. The W213 is still an excellent car and offers better value for money on the used market, but the W214 is a significant step forward. If you want the latest tech and the best driving experience, the W214 is the clear winner.

W213 — The Proven E‑Class

The W213 is the fifth generation of the Mercedes E‑Class, produced from 2016 to 2023 (with a facelift in 2020). It was available as a saloon (W213) and estate (S213). The W213 is known for its premium interior, excellent ride quality and strong engine range. Engine choices include the OM654 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (150–265 bhp), the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (255–362 bhp) and the M256 3.0‑litre inline‑six petrol (362–429 bhp). The W213 is the first E‑Class to offer the MBUX infotainment system (from the facelift). Key issues include OM654 AdBlue system faults and EGR cooler leaks, and timing chain stretch on the M276 and M256. The W213 remains a popular choice for used buyers — our 2025 data shows 214 enquiries. For more, see our OM654 Knowledge Centre and M256 Knowledge Centre.

W214 — The All‑New E‑Class

The W214 is the sixth generation of the Mercedes E‑Class, introduced in 2023. It's an all‑new car, with a completely redesigned interior (the new MBUX Superscreen), a more aerodynamic body and a range of mild‑hybrid and plug‑in hybrid powertrains. Engine choices include the OM654 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (150–265 bhp) and the M256 3.0‑litre inline‑six petrol (362–435 bhp). The W214 offers superior fuel economy (50–55 mpg on the OM654) and significantly lower emissions. It's also more refined and quieter than the W213. Early‑model issues are still being documented — we've seen some software glitches and sensor issues, but no major mechanical problems have yet emerged. The W214 is the E‑Class for buyers who want the latest technology and the best driving experience. Our data shows ~60 enquiries in 2025, reflecting its newer status. For more, see our OM654 Knowledge Centre and M256 Knowledge Centre.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The W214 is cheaper to run than the W213 over a typical five‑year period, despite its higher purchase price. The W214's better fuel economy saves around £200–£300 per year, and its more modern emissions systems mean lower VED in many cases. The W213's lower entry price is tempting, but the risk of OM654 AdBlue issues (£500–£1,500) is still a consideration. The W214's early‑model issues are mostly software‑related and less likely to be catastrophic. Over 50,000 miles, the W214 typically costs its owner £500–£1,000 less to run than the W213, making it the more financially sensible choice for buyers who can afford the higher initial price. For those on a budget, a well‑maintained W213 is still an excellent car.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the W214 better than the W213?

Yes — the W214 is more technologically advanced, more refined, and offers a superior driving experience. It's a significant step forward in every way. If your budget allows, the W214 is the better choice.

Which is more reliable, W213 or W214?

The W213 is more proven, with its issues well‑documented and understood. The W214 is still too new to have a full reliability track record. Early indications are positive, but the W213 is a safer bet for now.

Which is cheaper to insure, W213 or W214?

The W213 is generally cheaper to insure due to its lower value. Expect a 15–20% premium for the W214, reflecting its higher value and newer technology.

Is the W213 still a good car to buy in 2026?

Yes — the W213 is still an excellent executive saloon. It offers premium quality, strong engines and a comfortable ride. If you're on a budget, a well‑maintained W213 is a great choice.

Is the W214 worth the extra money over the W213?

For buyers who can afford it, yes — the W214 offers a better driving experience, newer technology and stronger resale value. The extra cost is justified if you want the latest E‑Class experience.
