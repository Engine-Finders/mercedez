The Verdict

The W223 is the better car for buyers who can afford it. It offers superior refinement, better technology, a more modern interior and stronger performance. The W222 is still an excellent flagship and offers better value for money on the used market, but the W223 is a significant step forward. W222 - The Acclaimed S‑Class

The W222 is the sixth generation of the Mercedes S‑Class, produced from 2013 to 2020. It was widely regarded as the best luxury saloon of its era, setting new standards for interior quality, ride comfort and refinement. Engine choices include the OM642 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (190–265 bhp), the OM654 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (194 bhp) in later models, the M276 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (255–362 bhp), the M278 4.7‑litre V8 petrol (408–455 bhp) and the M256 3.0‑litre inline‑six petrol (362–429 bhp) in the facelift. Key issues include OM642 swirl flap failure, M278 timing chain adjuster wear, air suspension compressor failure, and complex electronic faults. Replacement costs are higher than the E‑Class, reflecting the S‑Class's more complex engineering. The W222 remains a popular choice for used buyers - our 2025 data shows 55 enquiries. For more, see our OM642 Knowledge Centre, M278 Knowledge Centre and air suspension failure page.

W223 - The All‑New S‑Class

The W223 is the seventh generation of the Mercedes S‑Class, introduced in 2020. It's an all‑new car, with a completely redesigned interior (the new MBUX Superscreen), a more aerodynamic body and a range of mild‑hybrid and plug‑in hybrid powertrains. Engine choices include the OM654 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (194–265 bhp) and the M256 3.0‑litre inline‑six petrol (362–435 bhp). The W223 offers superior fuel economy (45–50 mpg on the OM654) and significantly lower emissions. It's also more refined and quieter than the W222. Early‑model issues are still being documented - we've seen some software glitches and sensor issues, but no major mechanical problems have yet emerged. The W223 is the S‑Class for buyers who want the latest technology and the best driving experience. Our data shows ~30 enquiries in 2025, reflecting its newer status. For more, see our OM654 Knowledge Centre and M256 Knowledge Centre.

Cost & Ownership Comparison

The W223 is cheaper to run than the W222 over a typical five‑year period, despite its higher purchase price, due to the OM654 engine's superior fuel economy. The W223's better fuel economy saves around £500–£800 per year compared to the OM642 V6 diesel W222, and its more modern emissions systems mean lower VED. The W222's lower entry price is tempting, but the risk of OM642 swirl flap failure (£500–£1,200), M278 timing chain adjuster work (£3,000–£5,000) and air suspension failures (£1,000–£2,000) can quickly add up. The W223's early‑model issues are mostly software‑related and less likely to be catastrophic. Over 50,000 miles, the W223 typically costs its owner £1,500–£2,500 less to run than a V6 diesel W222, making it the more financially sensible choice for buyers who can afford the higher initial price. For those on a budget, a well‑maintained W222 is still an excellent flagship.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the W223 better than the W222?

Yes - the W223 is more technologically advanced, more refined, and offers a superior driving experience. It's a significant step forward in every way. If your budget allows, the W223 is the better choice.

Which is more reliable, W222 or W223?

The W222 is more proven, with its issues well‑documented and understood. The W223 is still too new to have a full reliability track record. Early indications are positive, but the W222 is a safer bet for now.

Which is cheaper to insure, W222 or W223?

The W222 is generally cheaper to insure due to its lower value. Expect a 15–20% premium for the W223, reflecting its higher value and newer technology.

Is the W222 still a good car to buy in 2026?

Yes - the W222 is still one of the finest luxury saloons ever made. It offers incredible comfort, refinement and prestige. If you're on a budget, a well‑maintained W222 is a brilliant choice.

Is the W223 worth the extra money over the W222?

For buyers who can afford it, yes - the W223 offers a better driving experience, newer technology and stronger resale value. The extra cost is justified if you want the latest S‑Class experience.
