Known Mercedes Failure Patterns

Failure Page

OM651 Balance Shaft / Timing Chain

Tensioner wear on the OM651 can lead to chain stretch and catastrophic engine failure. Cold‑start rattle is the early warning.

£600–£1,500 [EM-QUOTE] Failure Page

OM642 Swirl Flap Motor

Swirl flap linkages seize, causing the motor to fail. Common on E‑Class, ML/GLE, GL/GLS. Replacement is about £500–£1,200.

Common on 70k–150k miles [PATTERN DATA] Failure Page

M272 Balance Shaft & Timing Chain

Balance shaft gear teeth wear and timing chain stretch. Affects C‑Class, E‑Class, CLK with M272 V6. Repair costs can reach £1,500–£2,500.

Known issue [EM-INTERVIEW] Failure Page

M276 Camshaft Adjuster

Camshaft adjuster failure on the M276 3.0–3.5 V6. Carbon buildup and timing chain stretch are also common.

£1,500–£2,500 [EM-QUOTE] Failure Page

M157 Timing Chain Adjuster

Hot‑vee V8 in AMG models. Valve stem seals, turbo coolant lines, and timing chain adjuster failures are all documented.

£3,000–£6,000 [EM-QUOTE] Failure Page

OM646 Injector Seals

“Black Death” — injector seals leak, causing carbon buildup around the injector. Common on older CDI diesels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Mercedes engine generates the most enquiries?

The OM651 2.1 diesel leads with 1,250 enquiries in our 2025 data, followed by the OM642 V6 diesel at 880. Together they account for nearly half of all Mercedes engine enquiries we handled last year [EM-VERIFIED].

What is the most reliable Mercedes diesel engine?

Based on our enquiry data and specialist interviews, the OM654 (2016 onwards) is considered significantly more reliable than the OM651, with fewer timing chain issues and better AdBlue system reliability. The OM656 straight‑six is also proving durable, though it’s still a relatively new engine [PATTERN DATA].

Is the OM642 a reliable engine?

The OM642 is a robust V6 diesel, but it has two common failure points: oil cooler seal leaks in the V of the engine, and swirl flap motor failure. These are well‑understood and repairable — many owners run OM642s past 200,000 miles with proper maintenance [EM-INTERVIEW].

How much does it cost to replace a Mercedes OM651 engine?

A reconditioned OM651 supply‑only costs £2,800–£4,800 [EM-QUOTE]. With R&R labour (8–12 hours at £65/hr) the fitted cost is roughly £3,500–£5,500. Main dealer costs are 30–50% higher.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for Mercedes?

Our 70% threshold means a replacement is financially sound if the fitted cost is less than 70% of the vehicle’s current market value. For example, a £4,000 engine in a £7,000 C‑Class is borderline; a £5,000 engine in an £8,000 E‑Class is a decision point. We always recommend checking with a specialist before committing [PATTERN DATA].
