Methodology

How we define ‘mileage at failure’ and what our data can and cannot tell you.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; over 4,500 of these relate to Mercedes engines.

How we derive mileage at failure: When a customer requests a quote, they often provide the vehicle’s current mileage and the reason for replacement. We cross‑reference this with mechanic notes (from supplier quotes) and post‑sale surveys. The mileage figures in this report represent the typical range at which a major engine failure occurs for that specific engine, based on a large sample of real‑world cases.

Limitations: Our dataset is biased towards engines that have failed — we cannot calculate failure rates against total population. A high mileage at failure might reflect that the engine is durable, or that it is fitted to vehicles that are used for motorway miles and simply cover more distance before issues appear. We present the data as observed, not as a definitive reliability ranking.

Refresh cycle: Annually, each February. This is the first edition, so there is no year‑on‑year change yet — but future reports will track shifts in average failure mileage.

The data shows a clear split: older diesel engines (OM651, OM642) tend to fail in the 80,000–150,000 mile bracket, often due to timing chain wear or oil cooler seal failures. Newer diesel units (OM654, OM656) show higher typical failure mileages, often exceeding 100,000 miles before major issues. Petrol engines (M272, M276) generally last longer, with many reaching 120,000 miles before timing chain or carbon buildup problems become significant.

The OM651 is the most‑enquired engine (1,250 requests) and also shows the widest mileage range – from as low as 80,000 to as high as 150,000 – reflecting the variance in maintenance and driving conditions. The OM642 V6 diesel is a close second, with typical failures occurring between 70,000 and 150,000 miles.

What Changed Since Last Year

This is our first edition — establishing the baseline for future comparisons.

As we are publishing this report for the first time in 2026, there is no prior year to compare against. However, we have built a robust methodology that will allow us to track changes in average failure mileage year on year. In future editions, we will highlight shifts such as:

  • Trends towards higher or lower failure mileages — which may indicate improving durability (or deteriorating maintenance habits).
  • Emergence of new failure patterns — e.g., if OM654 engines start showing timing chain issues earlier than expected.
  • Changes in the most‑enquired engines — reflecting the shifting vehicle parc as older models are scrapped and newer ones enter the used market.

For now, this report serves as the definitive benchmark for Mercedes engine failure mileage in the UK, based on our 2025 enquiry data.

What This Means For Owners

How to use this data when buying, maintaining, or deciding on a repair.

If you own a Mercedes with a diesel engine (OM651 or OM642), be especially vigilant between 80,000 and 120,000 miles — that’s the window where timing chain tensioner and oil cooler seal issues are most common. A cold‑start rattle or a visible oil leak from the V of the engine are early warning signs. Catching these early can turn a £1,500 repair into a £400 fix.

For petrol engines (M272, M276), the risk window is similar (60k–120k), but the failure modes are different. Timing chain stretch and carbon buildup are the main concerns. If you’re buying a used C‑Class or E‑Class with one of these engines, check for a full service history and listen for any rattles on startup.

Our Repair vs Replace Calculator can help you decide whether to repair or scrap based on your vehicle’s current value. And if you’re in the market for a replacement engine, see our Replacement Cost by Engine Code guide.

Remember: higher mileage doesn’t automatically mean a failure is imminent — many well‑maintained OM642s and OM651s exceed 200,000 miles without major issues. The data shows typical failure windows, not guaranteed outcomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average mileage at failure for Mercedes diesel engines?

In our 2025 data, the most common diesel engines (OM651, OM642) typically fail between 80,000 and 150,000 miles. The newer OM654 and OM656 often last beyond 100,000, with many reaching 180,000 miles before major issues arise [PATTERN DATA].

Do Mercedes petrol engines last longer than diesels?

Yes, based on our data. Petrol engines like the M272 and M276 typically fail at higher mileages than the older diesels, often exceeding 120,000 miles before timing chain or carbon buildup issues become critical. However, they are not immune — the M272 balance shaft issue is well‑known and often occurs between 60,000 and 100,000 miles [PATTERN DATA].

How reliable is the OM654 engine?

The OM654 is considered significantly more reliable than the OM651. Our data shows fewer enquiries per vehicle and higher typical failure mileages (100k–180k). AdBlue system and EGR cooler issues are the most common, but they are generally less costly to repair than timing chain failures on older engines [PATTERN DATA].

Should I avoid buying a Mercedes with over 100,000 miles?

Not necessarily. A well‑maintained Mercedes diesel or petrol can easily exceed 150,000 miles without major failure. The key is service history and known weak points. For example, an OM651 with a timing chain replaced at 100,000 miles may be a safer bet than one at 80,000 with no history. Use our registration lookup to check your engine and get tailored advice.

How often will this report be updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This is our first published edition, and future reports will include year‑on‑year comparisons to track changing failure patterns.

Does a high mileage at failure mean the engine is reliable?

Not necessarily. A high average failure mileage can reflect that the engine is fitted to vehicles used for long‑distance motorway driving, which tends to be less stressful than short urban trips. It can also indicate that the engine is robust, but it’s not a definitive reliability metric — we always recommend checking the specific failure patterns for your engine [PATTERN DATA].
