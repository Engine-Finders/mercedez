Mercedes engine enquiries in 2025 followed a distinct seasonal pattern, with January and February being the peak months — cold weather exacerbating known failure modes like OM651 timing chain rattle and OM642 swirl flap sticking. Methodology

How we track enquiry trends, what the data includes, and its limitations.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; 5,920 relate specifically to Mercedes-Benz. Enquiry timestamps were aggregated by month and quarter to identify seasonal and long-term patterns.

What we track: unique customer quote requests specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted. We track both absolute volumes and relative share across engine families.

Limitations: enquiry volume is a function of both failure rate and vehicle parc size. A rising trend for OM654 may mean the engine is becoming less reliable, or simply that more OM654-equipped vehicles are now entering the mileage window where failures typically occur. We use Known Failure Patterns data to distinguish between these explanations. We also note that winter months see more enquiries partly because cold weather exposes existing wear more dramatically, not because the underlying failure rate has actually increased.

Refresh cycle: annually, each February. This is our first edition and establishes the baseline year.

Headline Findings

The key trend patterns from our 2025 data.

❄️

Winter Peak

January and February account for 22% of annual enquiries — cold weather exposes timing chain rattle (OM651), swirl flap sticking (OM642), and oil seal brittleness (M276/M272).

📈

OM654 Rising

OM654 enquiries grew 24% Q1→Q4, reaching 520 total for the year. This reflects the 2016+ fleet entering the 80k–120k window where AdBlue/EGR issues typically appear.

📉

OM651 Plateauing

OM651 remains the most-enquired engine (1,250) but showed an 8% decline in H2 — older W204/W212 hosts are increasingly being scrapped rather than repaired as values fall below the 70% threshold.

⚡

Petrol/Diesel Shift

Petrol enquiries (M276, M274, M272, M177) accounted for 21% of total Mercedes engine enquiries — up from 18% in 2024 — as the diesel parcs age and some owners switch to petrol alternatives.

Key observation: January–February (22% combined) and November–December (19% combined) are the highest-volume periods. Summer months (June–August) consistently see the lowest enquiry volumes — likely a combination of warmer weather (fewer cold-start issues) and owners delaying repairs during holiday periods [PATTERN DATA]. OM651 timing chain enquiries are particularly seasonal, with 34% of all OM651 requests occurring in November–February.

Engine-by-Engine Trends

How each major engine family's enquiry volume changed across 2025.

OM651 (1,250 enquiries)

⬆️ Q1 peak (Jan–Feb), ⬇️ H2 decline (−8%). Older W204/W212 hosts increasingly scrapped. Balance shaft enquiries concentrated in 80k–120k examples. Expect gradual decline in 2026 as the parc ages out [PATTERN DATA].

OM642 (880 enquiries)

📈 Steady across the year, with a slight Q3 dip. Swirl flap enquiries peak in winter (cold start sticking). Oil cooler seal issues are consistent year-round. High-value GLE/ML hosts mean more repairs, fewer scrappages [PATTERN DATA].

OM654 (520 enquiries)

📈 +24% Q1→Q4. The clear growth story — 2016+ vehicles now entering 80k–120k window. AdBlue/EGR cooler issues are the primary drivers. Expect continued growth in 2026 as the fleet ages [PATTERN DATA].

M276 (390 enquiries)

📈 Modest Q3/Q4 increase. Timing chain stretch enquiries concentrated in 80k–120k examples. High-value E-Class and CLS hosts keep repair volumes steady despite age [PATTERN DATA].

M177 (370 enquiries)

📈 Consistent growth across H2. AMG GT, E63, and C63 enquiries rising as these vehicles age into higher-mileage territory. Oil leak and turbo seal issues dominate [PATTERN DATA].

M272 (260 enquiries)

📉 Gradual decline as W204 and W211 hosts are scrapped. Balance shaft enquiries remain common at 60k–120k. Expect continued decline in 2026 [PATTERN DATA].

What This Means For Owners

If you own a Mercedes, the seasonal pattern is worth knowing: cold weather exposes weaknesses that warm weather masks. If you hear a cold-start rattle from an OM651 in December, it may be a timing chain tensioner — the same rattle in July is less likely to appear. This doesn't mean the problem isn't real in summer; it means the symptom is less audible until the temperature drops.

The rising OM654 enquiry trend is a natural part of the 2016+ diesel fleet ageing — it doesn't mean the OM654 is unreliable, just that more of them are now at the mileage where AdBlue/EGR systems typically need attention. For owners of 2016–2019 C-Class, E-Class, and GLC diesels, this is the window where preventative maintenance makes the biggest difference. An EGR cooler check at 80,000 miles can prevent a much more expensive failure at 120,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are there more Mercedes engine enquiries in winter?

Cold weather exposes several known failure modes: timing chain rattle is more audible on cold start (OM651), swirl flaps stick more readily (OM642), and oil seals become more brittle (M276/M272). January and February consistently see the highest enquiry volumes — around 22% of the annual total.

Is the OM654 becoming less reliable?

Not necessarily — the rising enquiry volume (up 24% Q1→Q4) mainly reflects the 2016+ fleet entering the 80k–120k mileage window where AdBlue/EGR cooler issues typically appear. It's a natural ageing pattern, not a sign of inherent unreliability.

Why is OM651 enquiry volume declining?

OM651-powered W204 C-Class and W212 E-Class hosts are now old enough that many are being scrapped rather than repaired when a major failure occurs — the 70% repair-to-value threshold kicks in at around £4,000–£5,000 vehicle value, which many of these cars now sit below.

Are petrol Mercedes engines becoming more common in enquiries?

Yes — petrol engines (M276, M274, M272, M177) accounted for 21% of Mercedes engine enquiries in 2025, up from 18% in 2024. This reflects both the ageing of the diesel parc and some owners switching to petrol alternatives.

When is the best time to get a Mercedes engine inspected?

Autumn (September–October) is ideal — it's before the winter peak, so you can catch cold-start issues before they become catastrophic, and workshops are generally less busy than in the January–February rush.

How accurate are these trend predictions for 2026?

We don't predict — we report. The trends we've observed (OM654 rising, OM651 plateauing, winter peaks) are likely to continue but are not forecasts. We publish the data annually and let the numbers speak for themselves. Check back in February 2027 for the year-on-year comparison.
