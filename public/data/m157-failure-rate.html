100k–130k

130k–160k

The M157's mileage-at-failure distribution reflects its use in high-performance AMG vehicles — many are weekend or second cars, so mileage accumulates slowly. The peak failure window of 100,000–130,000 miles aligns with what specialists report as the point where the hot-vee design's heat-related issues (valve stem seals, coolant lines, turbo seals) become most apparent [EM-INTERVIEW].

Most Common Failure Modes (Ranked)

What actually fails on the M157, in order of frequency observed in our data.

1. Valve Stem Seals

The most common M157 failure — hot-vee design causes heat build-up that degrades valve stem seals, leading to oil consumption and blue smoke on startup. Typically appears at 80k–120k miles. Repair requires significant disassembly, often done in conjunction with coolant system work [PATTERN DATA].

2. Coolant System Leaks

Turbocharger coolant lines and the main cooling system are prone to leaks due to heat cycling. Often the first sign of trouble — catching it early prevents more serious issues. Replacement hoses and components are available, but labour is significant due to hot-vee access [PATTERN DATA].

3. Turbocharger Seal Leaks

The M157's twin turbos are mounted in the V of the engine, creating heat-soak issues that degrade seals over time. Oil consumption increases, and in severe cases, turbo failure can follow. New turbos are expensive — reconditioning is often the more economical route [PATTERN DATA].

4. Battery Drain / Electrical Issues

M157-powered cars are known for battery drain issues, often linked to the auxiliary battery system or failing alternator. While not a direct engine failure, it's a common complaint that often leads owners to investigate more serious issues [PATTERN DATA].

How Long Does an M157 Actually Last?

The data-driven answer on M157 lifespan.

Based on our 2025 enquiry data and cross-referenced with workshop feedback [EM-INTERVIEW], the M157 typically reaches 110,000–150,000 miles before a major repair is required. This is a solid lifespan for a high-performance twin-turbo V8 — many naturally aspirated V8s last longer, but the M157 is in the same bracket as its BMW competitors (the N63 and S63).

What separates a 110,000-mile M157 from a 150,000-mile one is almost always maintenance history. Engines with annual coolant system inspections, proactive valve seal work at the first sign of oil consumption, and strict adherence to oil change intervals (5,000–7,000 miles) consistently outlast those where the first sign of trouble was a warning light [EM-INTERVIEW].

The M157's hot-vee design means heat management is everything. Owners who keep the coolant system in top condition and address any leaks immediately are far more likely to see 150,000+ miles. Conversely, ignoring a small coolant weep can lead to turbocharger damage, oil seal degradation, and a much shorter lifespan — and a much higher repair bill.

Our Verdict — The M157 Decision

Is an M157 failure a reason to scrap the car? The data says no.

The M157 is a ✅ Safe engine for repair — here's why.

With host vehicle values ranging from £15,000 to £45,000 [THIRD-PARTY] and a reconditioned supply cost of £6,000–£10,000 [EM-QUOTE], the replacement cost typically sits well below the 70% threshold that would trigger a scrap decision. Even at the upper end of the replacement cost (£10,000 + £1,430 labour for an AMG specialist), the total fitted cost of around £11,400 is still only 25–40% of the vehicle's value on a typical M157-powered E63, CLS63 or S63.

But there's a caveat: the 70% threshold is a guide, not a rule. An older, higher-mileage M157-powered E63 with a rusty body and worn suspension might not be worth the repair — but that's a vehicle condition issue, not an engine issue. If the car is otherwise sound, the M157 is financially worth repairing. We recommend using our Repair vs Replace Calculator to run the numbers for your specific vehicle.

For owners facing an M157 failure, the key is early diagnosis. A valve stem seal issue caught at 80,000 miles is a much smaller repair than a turbocharger failure at 120,000 miles caused by the same underlying oil consumption. Regular inspections and addressing oil consumption early are the two single most effective actions an owner can take.

Frequently Asked Questions

How reliable is the Mercedes M157 engine?

The M157 is a solid high-performance V8 when properly maintained. It typically reaches 110,000–150,000 miles before needing major repair. The hot-vee design creates heat-related issues (valve stem seals, coolant leaks) that are well-understood by AMG specialists. These are predictable, not catastrophic — if caught early, they're repairable without replacing the whole engine.

What are the common signs of M157 failure?

Blue smoke on startup (valve stem seals), coolant loss with no visible external leak (coolant system issues), oil consumption between services (turbo seals or valve stem seals), and battery drain (auxiliary battery/alternator). A cold-start rattle can also indicate timing chain tensioner wear, though this is less common on the M157 than on earlier AMG V8s.

How much does an M157 replacement cost?

A reconditioned M157 supply costs £6,000–£10,000 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour for removal and replacement is 16–22 hours at AMG-specialist rates (£65/hr indie, £100+ at main dealer), adding £1,040–£1,430 for independent specialists. Total fitted cost ranges from £7,040 to £11,430. Main dealer costs would be significantly higher.

Is the M157 more reliable than the M156?

The M156 (naturally aspirated 6.2-litre V8) and M157 (5.5-litre twin-turbo) have different failure patterns. The M156 is known for camshaft adjuster and lifter issues at 60k–120k miles, while the M157's issues are heat-related. Enquiry volumes are similar (95 vs 85), suggesting comparable reliability — but the M157's turbo complexity makes repairs more expensive when they do occur. The M157 has a slightly longer typical lifespan (110k–150k vs 100k–130k for the M156) [PATTERN DATA].

Should I buy a high-mileage M157?

Yes, but with caveats. A high-mileage M157 (100,000+ miles) is a viable proposition if it has a full service history showing regular oil changes (every 5,000–7,000 miles) and coolant system work. Look for evidence of valve stem seal work — if it hasn't been done, budget for it. The host vehicle value at 100,000 miles is typically £15,000–£25,000, and a well-maintained M157 can easily reach 150,000 miles.

What's the difference between M157, M177 and M178?

The M157 is the 5.5-litre twin-turbo V8 used in E63, CLS63, S63 and some AMG GT variants up to 2018. The M177 is the newer 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 that replaced it, with better heat management and more power. The M178 is the AMG GT-specific version of the M177. The M157 is now an older engine but remains well-supported with good parts availability and specialist knowledge in the UK.
