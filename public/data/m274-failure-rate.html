Mileage-at-Failure Distribution

When do M274 engines typically need replacement or major repair?

50k–70k

Early failures — typically water pump issues or timing chain tensioner wear. Less common than on the M270, but still appears on poorly maintained examples [PATTERN DATA].

70k–110k

Peak failure window — most M274 enquiries fall in this range. Carbon buildup and water pump issues dominate. Proactive maintenance is the key differentiator [PATTERN DATA].

110k–160k

Extended-life examples — well-maintained M274s regularly exceed 140,000 miles. These have impeccable service histories with proactive cooling system work [PATTERN DATA].

The M274's mileage-at-failure distribution is similar to the M270 but with slightly better longevity — the revised timing chain and tensioner design reduces the risk of early failure. The peak failure window of 70,000–110,000 miles aligns with what specialists report as the point where the M274's cooling system and direct-injection carbon buildup issues become most apparent [EM-INTERVIEW].

Most Common Failure Modes (Ranked)

What actually fails on the M274, in order of frequency observed in our data.

1. Water Pump Failure

The most common M274 failure — water pump leaks or complete failure, typically appearing at 60k–100k miles. The M274's water pump is improved over the M270 but still a known weak point. Replacement is straightforward and relatively inexpensive, but ignoring it can cause overheating and head gasket failure [PATTERN DATA].

2. Carbon Buildup (Intake Valves)

Direct-injection engines like the M274 suffer from carbon buildup on intake valves, leading to rough idling, misfires, and reduced power. Walnut blasting is the standard remedy, typically needed at 60k–100k miles. The M274 is slightly less prone to carbon buildup than the M270, but it's still a known issue [PATTERN DATA].

3. Timing Chain Tensioner Wear

The M274's timing chain tensioner is improved over the M270 but can still wear, particularly on earlier examples (pre-2015). A cold-start rattle is the early warning sign. If caught early, the tensioner alone can be replaced; if ignored, the chain can jump, causing catastrophic damage [PATTERN DATA].

4. Thermostat Housing Failure

The thermostat housing can crack or fail, leading to coolant leaks and temperature instability. Usually replaced alongside the water pump as a preventative measure. Relatively inexpensive and well-documented repair [PATTERN DATA].

How Long Does an M274 Actually Last?

The data-driven answer on M274 lifespan.

Based on our 2025 enquiry data and cross-referenced with workshop feedback [EM-INTERVIEW], the M274 typically reaches 130,000–160,000 miles before a major repair is required. This is an excellent lifespan for a modern turbocharged petrol engine — comparable to the M270 and better than many competitors in the premium compact and mid-size segments.

The M274's longevity is a testament to its robust design and the improvements made over the M270. The revised timing chain, improved water pump design, and better engine management all contribute to a more durable engine. When properly maintained, the M274 is capable of very high mileage [EM-INTERVIEW].

What separates a 130,000-mile M274 from a 160,000-mile one is almost always maintenance history. Engines with annual coolant system inspections, proactive water pump replacement at 60,000–80,000 miles, and regular oil changes consistently outlast those where the first sign of trouble was a warning light. The M274's relatively moderate replacement cost (£2,500–£4,200) means that even when a major failure occurs, it's often financially viable to repair rather than scrap the car.

Our Verdict — The M274 Decision

Is an M274 failure a reason to scrap the car? The data says no.

The M274 is a ✅ Safe engine for repair — here's why.

With host vehicle values ranging from £6,000 to £22,000 [THIRD-PARTY] and a reconditioned supply cost of £2,500–£4,200 [EM-QUOTE], the replacement cost typically sits well below the 70% threshold that would trigger a scrap decision. Even at the upper end of the replacement cost (£4,200 + £715 labour for an independent specialist), the total fitted cost of around £4,915 is still only 22–82% of the vehicle's value on a typical M274-powered C-Class, E-Class, GLC or CLA.

But there's a caveat: the M274 is fitted to a wide range of vehicles with very different values. A £4,915 repair on a £6,000 W205 C-Class (82% of value) is borderline — if the car has other issues (suspension wear, high mileage, cosmetic damage), scrapping might be the better option. On a £15,000 E-Class or GLC, it's a clear "go". Use our Repair vs Replace Calculator to run the numbers for your specific vehicle.

For owners facing an M274 failure, the key is early diagnosis. A water pump leak caught at 60,000 miles is a £500–£1,000 repair. The same leak ignored until 100,000 miles can lead to head gasket failure and a much larger bill. Regular inspections and addressing any coolant loss immediately are the two single most effective actions an owner can take.

Frequently Asked Questions

How reliable is the Mercedes M274 engine?

The M274 is a very reliable modern turbocharged petrol engine. It typically reaches 130,000–160,000 miles before needing major repair — an excellent lifespan for a turbo-four. Key failure points (water pump, carbon buildup, timing chain tensioner) are well-understood and repairable, making it one of the more dependable Mercedes engines of its era. The M274 is a significant improvement over the earlier M270.

What are the common signs of M274 failure?

Coolant loss with no visible external leak (water pump failure), cold-start rattle (timing chain tensioner wear), rough idling or misfires (carbon buildup), and overheating (thermostat or water pump). Regular coolant checks and listening for startup noises are the best early detection methods.

How much does an M274 replacement cost?

A reconditioned M274 supply costs £2,500–£4,200 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour for removal and replacement is 8–11 hours at independent specialist rates (£65/hr), adding £520–£715. Total fitted cost ranges from £3,020 to £4,915. Main dealer costs would be significantly higher.

What's the difference between M270 and M274?

Both are 2.0-litre turbocharged four-cylinder petrol engines. The M270 is the earlier version, used in W176 A-Class, W246 B-Class, C117 CLA and X156 GLA from 2012–2018. The M274 is the later, updated version with different engine management, revised timing chain components, improved water pump design, and some internal improvements. The M274 is generally more refined and slightly more reliable, and both share the same basic architecture.

Should I buy a high-mileage M274?

Absolutely — the M274 is a robust engine and high-mileage examples (80,000+ miles) are viable if they have a full service history. Look for evidence of water pump replacement (around 60,000–80,000 miles) — if it hasn't been done, budget for it. Check for cold-start rattle, which suggests timing chain tensioner wear. A well-maintained M274 can easily reach 150,000 miles.

Is the M274 better than the M270?

Yes — the M274 is a clear improvement over the M270. It has better engine management, a revised timing chain and tensioner design, improved water pump reliability, and better overall refinement. However, the M270 is not significantly less reliable; the main differences are age and the specific models they were fitted to. The M274 is the engine to choose if you're looking for a more modern, refined experience.
