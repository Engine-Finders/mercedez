Data Sources

Where every number on this site comes from.

Proprietary CRM Data

24,650 unique engine replacement enquiries from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. Every enquiry includes: engine code, vehicle model, mileage, customer postcode, and the specific fault described where supplied.

Specialist Interviews

100+ UK engine specialists and workshop owners — direct interviews about failure patterns, repair costs, labour times, and common pitfalls. These are the people who actually rebuild these engines every day.

Third-Party Market Data

Vehicle value bands from AutoTrader, Motors.co.uk, and CarGurus UK market data 2025. Manufacturer Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) where available. Used for repair-to-value calculations and validation.

All data is UK-specific. The UK Mercedes market has its own fleet composition, ownership patterns, and repair economics — we don't use US or European data unless explicitly stated and marked as [THIRD-PARTY].

Verification Process

The five layers of verification every figure passes before publication.

1. Internal Data Extraction

Raw CRM data is extracted, cleaned, and checked for anomalies. Duplicate entries are removed. Incomplete or invalid records are flagged but not discarded unless they can't be verified.

2. Specialist Cross-Referencing

All failure pattern claims are cross-referenced with specialist interviews. A claim that appears in the data but isn't corroborated by workshops is marked as provisional or omitted.

3. Third-Party Validation

Key findings are checked against manufacturer TSBs, MOT test data, and market sources. If three independent sources agree, it's published as verified.

4. Market Validation

Cost figures are cross-checked against eBay Motors, PistonHeads classifieds, and actual supplier quotes sent through our CRM. If the market disagrees with our data, we re-investigate.

5. Editorial Review

Every published page is reviewed by an editorial team member with automotive experience. The reviewer checks: data tagging accuracy, logical consistency, and whether the page's verdict is supported by the evidence presented. No page publishes until this review is complete.

We don't use AI to generate or verify data. Every figure on this site is sourced, checked, and signed off by humans. Our Editorial Standards page sets out the full policy.

Data Tags — What Each One Means

Every number on this site carries a tag telling you exactly where it came from.

[EM-VERIFIED]

Exact count or percentage from our internal CRM — enquiry volumes, model requests, condition split. From 24,650 quote requests, Jan–Dec 2025.

[EM-QUOTE]

Pricing ranges derived from actual supplier quotes sent through our CRM, cross-checked with marketplaces. Source: supplier quotes + interviews.

[PATTERN DATA]

Qualitative failure patterns derived from enquiry descriptions, specialist interviews, and TSBs. Not a verified volume claim — but consistently observed.

[THIRD-PARTY]

External data — AutoTrader, Parkers, manufacturer TSBs, MOT data. Named source and date are always provided in the citation.

[PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs cost sheet]

This tag appears only in draft. A page with this tag does not publish. It means the figure needs sourcing before the page can go live. If you see this tag on a published page, it's a build failure — please tell us.

Limitations — What Our Data Cannot Tell You

Honesty about limitations is part of our editorial standard. Here's what our data can't do.

Enquiry Volume ≠ Failure Rate

A high enquiry count doesn't necessarily mean an engine is unreliable — it can mean it's fitted to a large number of vehicles, or that those vehicles are at the age where failures become common. We don't claim to know the total vehicle parc for each engine, so we don't claim per-vehicle failure rates.

Not a Random Sample

Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — not a random sample of all Mercedes owners. It over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. We're transparent about this in every data report.

No Prediction

We don't predict future failures. We report on what has happened in our data. We don't say "Engine X will fail at Y miles" — we say "Engine X has been reported to us at Y miles in our 2025 dataset." The future may be different.

We state these limitations explicitly on every data page — not as a disclaimer, but as a foundation for trust. A number without a limitation is a number without integrity.

Refresh Cycle

How often we update our data and how we handle changes.

Annual refresh: Our core data is refreshed annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. The 2026 edition covers 2025 data. The 2027 edition will cover 2026 data and will include a year-on-year comparison.

Fixed baseline: Figures are locked at time of publication and are not silently revised. If we need to correct an error, it's logged on our Corrections Log rather than edited away.

Supplementary updates: We may publish supplementary reports (e.g., regional breakdowns, new engine families) between annual cycles. These are clearly marked as additional analysis, not replacements for the core annual dataset.

This refresh cycle ensures our data is current enough to be useful, stable enough to be cited, and honest about its vintage. A data point from 2025 is a data point from 2025 — not pretending to be 2026.

How to Cite This Methodology

If you're referencing our data in press coverage, academic work, or forum discussion, please use the following format.

Suggested citation "Mercedes Engine Data Methodology," Mercedes-Benz Ownership Guide, published February 2026, mercedesengines.uk/data/methodology

For individual data points, include the specific page and the date. Example: "The M276 generated 390 enquiries in 2025 (Mercedes-Benz Ownership Guide, February 2026)." If you're using a specific figure with a data tag, please retain the tag in your citation.

For press enquiries or the full underlying dataset, contact us via our About page. We're happy to provide additional context or breakdowns for journalists working on a related story.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Mercedes engine enquiries did you analyse in 2025?

We analysed 5,920 Mercedes-specific enquiries from our total of 24,650 across all brands. This covers every engine code and model that generated at least one enquiry in our CRM data. The full year 2025, Jan–Dec.

Is this data representative of all Mercedes on UK roads?

No, and we're upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — it over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. We pair enquiry data with specialist interviews and third-party sources to build a fuller picture, but we never claim our dataset is a random sample of the total Mercedes parc.

How do you verify cost figures?

Cost figures are cross-checked through three channels: supplier quotes sent through our CRM, direct interviews with 100+ UK specialists, and marketplaces (eBay Motors, PistonHeads). All cost figures are marked [EM-QUOTE] and we disclose the source range. Main dealer costs may be 30–50% higher.

How often is your data updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at publication and not silently revised. Corrections are logged on our Corrections Log. Supplementary reports may be published between annual cycles.

Can I use this data in my own research?

Yes — our data is free to use with attribution. Use the citation format provided on this page. For commercial use or large-scale reproduction, please contact us via our About page.

Why don't you use a larger sample size?

24,650 total enquiries across all brands is a substantial dataset — it represents real customers with real engine problems, not a synthetic or modelled dataset. We prioritise data quality and verification over sample size. A verified figure from 24,650 enquiries is more useful than an unverified figure from 100,000.
