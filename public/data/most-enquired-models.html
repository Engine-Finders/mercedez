How We Compiled This Mercedes Engine Reliability Data

This report draws exclusively from internal CRM quote-request data collected across EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk during the full 2025 calendar year. The dataset comprises 24,650 unique quote requests for Mercedes-Benz engines in the UK [EM-VERIFIED]. Each entry represents a distinct customer enquiry specifying a particular model or engine code; repeat requests from the same individual for the same vehicle are deduplicated to prevent skewing.

What this data captures is replacement intent — people actively seeking a used, reconditioned, or rebuilt engine. It does not measure total vehicles on the road, nor does it capture routine servicing or minor repairs. A high position in this ranking therefore signals either a large surviving population reaching typical failure mileage, a known vulnerability accelerating replacement timelines, or both. We explicitly avoid claiming "failure rates" against total registrations because our sample is self-selecting by definition.

All figures cited below carry the [EM-VERIFIED] tag, meaning they map directly to a row in our proprietary 2025 dataset. Cost ranges are drawn from actual supplier quotes sent through our platform [EM-QUOTE], and vehicle value bands reference Auto Trader, Motors.co.uk and AutoUncle 2025 market data [THIRD-PARTY]. Where we discuss patterns not directly quantifiable from the dataset, we use [PATTERN DATA] and state the limitation plainly.

Enquiry Volume by Model Generation

Aggregating enquiries by generation rather than individual chassis codes reveals where replacement demand concentrates within each Mercedes family. This matters because a single generation may span a decade of production, and enquiry volume typically peaks as the earliest examples reach 10–15 years old and 80,000–120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA].

C-Class Family

The W205 generation (2014–2021) leads all Mercedes models with 368 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. Its predecessor, the W204 (2007–2014), follows with 252 [EM-VERIFIED]. Combined, these two generations represent 620 of the 24,650 total Mercedes enquiries in our dataset — approximately 2.5% of all requests. The newer W206 (2021–present) has already generated 188 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], suggesting early OM654-equipped examples are entering the replacement cycle sooner than some owners anticipated. The older W203 (2000–2007) appears only marginally with 33 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], consistent with very low residual values making replacement economically unviable for most examples.

E-Class Family

The W212 (2009–2016) sits second overall with 338 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], while the W211 (2003–2009) contributes 234 [EM-VERIFIED]. The W213 (2016–2023) has accumulated 214 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] despite being significantly newer, indicating that OM654 and M276 engines in this generation are reaching failure milestones. The W210 (1996–2003) registers 173 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], almost entirely from late OM642 retrofits rather than original petrol units. This generational spread confirms that E-Class engine replacement demand is sustained across three decades of production rather than concentrated in a single problematic year.

SUV Families (GLC, GLE, GLK, ML, GL)

SUV models collectively account for over 1,000 enquiries in our 2025 dataset [EM-VERIFIED]. The GLC X253 leads at 304 [EM-VERIFIED], followed by the GLA X156 at 272 [EM-VERIFIED] and the GLE W166 at 142 [EM-VERIFIED]. Older SUV platforms remain significant: the ML W164 generates 120 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] and the GLK X204 152 [EM-VERIFIED], both predominantly OM642-powered. The higher average transaction value of SUV hosts means replacement remains financially viable even when repair costs approach £5,000–£6,500, which explains why older SUV models sustain enquiry volumes that equivalent-age saloons do not.

Why Enquiry Volume Is Not the Same as Failure Rate

The most important caveat in interpreting this Mercedes engine reliability data is that enquiry volume conflates popularity with vulnerability. The C-Class W205 tops this list not necessarily because it is the least reliable Mercedes ever built, but because it was one of the highest-volume Mercedes models sold in the UK between 2014 and 2021 and its earliest examples are now reaching the 10-year mark when timing chain and injector seal issues on the OM651 typically manifest [PATTERN DATA].

Conversely, models with lower absolute enquiry counts may have higher proportional failure rates if their surviving population is small. The S-Class W221, for example, generated 120 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] — far fewer than the C-Class W205 — but the total number of W221s still on UK roads is also substantially smaller. Without access to DVLA registration data cross-referenced against our enquiry base, we cannot calculate a true failure rate per 1,000 vehicles. What we can say with confidence is that the OM651 and OM642 engines appear disproportionately across multiple model lines, confirming that engine-specific design factors drive replacement demand as much as host vehicle popularity.

For owners evaluating whether their specific model's position on this list should concern them, the more relevant metric is the Repair-to-Value flag in our Repair vs Replace Framework. A high-enquiry model whose replacement cost sits comfortably below 60% of current vehicle value presents a fundamentally different ownership proposition than one approaching the scrap threshold, regardless of how many other owners have faced the same decision.

Regional Distribution of Mercedes Engine Enquiries

Enquiry density tracks Mercedes ownership concentration rather than regional differences in driving conditions or climate. The South East and Greater London account for the highest absolute volumes, consistent with higher urban concentrations of newer C-Class, E-Class and GLC models. The North West and West Midlands show proportionally higher enquiry shares for older E-Class (W211/W212) and ML/GL SUVs, reflecting different demographic ownership patterns and longer retention periods for premium vehicles outside London [PATTERN DATA].

Scotland and Northern Ireland show elevated proportions of OM642-powered SUV enquiries relative to saloon models, consistent with rural use profiles and higher annual mileages accelerating wear on turbocharger and oil cooler components. None of these regional variations change the underlying failure mechanisms described in our per-engine pages — an OM651 timing chain tensioner wears identically regardless of postcode — but they do affect specialist availability and typical diagnostic turnaround times, which our registration lookup tool accounts for when routing enquiries to vetted specialists.

What This Means for Mercedes Owners

The Mercedes C-Class W205, E-Class W212 and GLC X253 dominate UK engine replacement enquiries because they combine large surviving populations with engines (OM651, OM642, OM654) whose known failure modes cluster around the 80,000–120,000 mile mark [PATTERN DATA]. This is neither a crisis nor an indictment of Mercedes engineering; it is the predictable intersection of sales volume, vehicle age and component lifecycle.

For prospective buyers, this data suggests prioritising service history verification and pre-purchase inspection focused on timing chain condition, injector seals and oil cooler integrity for any OM651 or OM642 example, regardless of model badge. For current owners whose vehicles fall within the peak enquiry mileage band, proactive inspection at 60,000–80,000 miles can distinguish a £300 preventative fix from a £5,000 reactive replacement. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a high enquiry count mean my Mercedes is unreliable?

Not necessarily. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles exist and their average age as much as it reflects failure frequency. The C-Class W205 leads partly because Mercedes sold enormous numbers of them, not solely because they fail disproportionately. Cross-reference with our per-engine failure data for proportional context.

Why aren't AMG models higher on this list?

AMG variants (M157, M177, M178) generate significant per-unit repair costs but lower absolute enquiry volumes because far fewer were sold. Their failures are often catastrophic when they occur, but the small surviving population keeps total request counts below mainstream diesel models. See our Most Expensive AMG Repairs guide for cost-specific analysis.

Is this data representative of all Mercedes vehicles in the UK?

No. Our dataset captures people actively seeking replacement engines, which naturally over-represents failing vehicles and under-represents trouble-free ones. Full methodology and limitations are disclosed above. We do not claim this mirrors DVLA registration data or manufacturer warranty claim statistics.

How often is this report updated?

Annually each February, covering the prior full calendar year. The 2026 edition (covering 2025 data) is our baseline. Subsequent editions will enable year-on-year trend analysis currently impossible with a single snapshot.
