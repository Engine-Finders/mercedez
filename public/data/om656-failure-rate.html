Where the OM656 Sits in the Mercedes Diesel Hierarchy

The OM656 is Mercedes-Benz's 3.0-litre inline-six turbodiesel, introduced in 2017 as the direct replacement for the OM642 V6. It produces 286 hp in 350d applications and 340 hp in 400d guise, fitted across the E-Class W213, S-Class W223, GLE W167, GLS X167 and CLS C257.

Across our full 2025 dataset of 24,650 unique engine replacement enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], the OM656 does not register as a standalone line item. This is not an omission — it is a signal. The engine is newer, fitted to fewer vehicles overall, and those vehicles have not yet reached the mileage band where bottom-end failure becomes common. Its absence from the ranking table is, in practical terms, evidence of low failure incidence [PATTERN DATA].

For comparison, the four-cylinder OM654 — the OM656's modular sibling sharing the same architecture, bore spacing and emissions hardware — generated 520 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. The OM642 V6 it replaced generated 880 [EM-VERIFIED]. The OM651 four-cylinder, the highest-volume Mercedes diesel in the UK parc, generated 1,250 [EM-VERIFIED]. The OM656's position below all three in enquiry volume reflects both its smaller vehicle population and its lower per-vehicle failure rate [PATTERN DATA].

Mileage-at-Failure: What the Pattern Shows

Because the OM656 has not generated sufficient standalone enquiry volume to produce a statistically meaningful mileage distribution, we rely on two sources: the OM654's data as the closest architectural proxy, and specialist workshop reporting [EM-INTERVIEW].

The OM654's failure enquiries cluster between 100,000 and 180,000 miles, with the majority concentrated around 120,000–150,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. These are predominantly emissions-system interventions rather than mechanical breakdowns. The OM656, operating in larger, heavier vehicles (E-Class, GLE, S-Class) that tend to cover longer motorway distances, is expected to follow a similar curve but shifted slightly later — the 130,000–180,000 mile band [PATTERN DATA].

Critically, no specialist in our network reports a pattern of catastrophic bottom-end failure — bearing wear, crankshaft fracture, or timing chain collapse — at any mileage for the OM656 [EM-INTERVIEW]. This distinguishes it sharply from the OM642, which carries known oil cooler and swirl flap vulnerabilities, and from the OM651's timing chain tensioner pattern. How Long Does the OM656 Actually Last?

The OM656 is engineered for a service life exceeding 200,000 miles under normal operating conditions [PATTERN DATA]. This is consistent with Mercedes-Benz's positioning of the engine in flagship and long-distance models — S-Class, GLE, and GLS buyers expect high-mileage durability without major intervention.

Specialist workshops in our network report that well-maintained OM656 units — those with oil changes at or below 15,000-mile intervals using the correct MB 229.51/229.52 specification — routinely pass 180,000 miles without requiring mechanical intervention beyond emissions components [EM-INTERVIEW]. The engine's iron block, aluminium head, and forged crankshaft are over-engineered relative to the power outputs they serve [PATTERN DATA].

For broader context on Mercedes engine longevity across the range, see our Engine Lifespan data page. The OM656 sits at the upper end of the Mercedes diesel longevity spectrum, alongside the older OM646 four-cylinder and ahead of both the OM651 and OM642 in expected trouble-free service life [PATTERN DATA].

What Makes an OM656 More or Less Likely to Need Attention

Not every OM656 ages identically. Three factors materially shift the risk profile:

Service interval adherence. The single strongest predictor of long-term health. Mercedes' variable service indicator can stretch oil change intervals to 20,000 miles or more in favourable conditions. Specialists consistently recommend capping this at 15,000 miles for the OM656's turbocharger bearings and timing chain tensioner hydraulics [EM-INTERVIEW]. Vehicles with full dealer history at shorter intervals show measurably fewer emissions-system interventions later in life [PATTERN DATA].

Driving pattern. The OM656's DPF and SCR systems regenerate most effectively at sustained motorway speed. Vehicles used predominantly for short urban journeys accumulate soot and AdBlue crystallisation faster, advancing the timeline for DPF intervention and SCR component replacement by 20,000–40,000 miles [PATTERN DATA].

Vehicle weight and towing. In GLE and GLS applications, the OM656 moves 2,300+ kg regularly, and towing pushes sustained load higher. This accelerates turbocharger and cooling system wear relative to the same engine in an E-Class saloon. The difference is modest but real — specialists report GLE/GLS-hosted OM656 units presenting for emissions work roughly 10,000–15,000 miles earlier on average than E-Class examples [EM-INTERVIEW].

Our Verdict

Reliability Rating: 8.2 / 10 [PROVISIONAL]

The OM656 is the best diesel engine Mercedes-Benz has built since the OM646 era. It eliminates the V6's oil cooler vulnerability, the four-cylinder's timing chain fragility, and does so without introducing a new catastrophic failure mode. Its weaknesses are the same emissions-system components that afflict every modern diesel — AdBlue injectors, EGR coolers, DPF loading — and these are manageable, predictable, and comparatively inexpensive to address.

For a buyer considering a used E 350d, GLE 400d, or S 350d, the OM656 should not be a source of anxiety. It is a source of confidence relative to the alternatives. The engine's absence from our high-volume enquiry data is the strongest endorsement we can give: owners are not contacting us to replace these engines. They are contacting us about their OM651s and OM642s instead [PATTERN DATA].

This rating is marked [PROVISIONAL] because the OM656 Knowledge Centre is the canonical source for this figure. Once published, all references inherit from there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Mercedes OM656 engine reliable?

Yes. The OM656 is among the most reliable modern Mercedes diesels. Its failure pattern is limited to emissions-system components (AdBlue, EGR, DPF) rather than core mechanical weaknesses. No catastrophic bottom-end failure pattern has been documented by specialists in our network [EM-INTERVIEW].

What is the average life of a Mercedes OM656 engine?

With correct maintenance at 15,000-mile oil intervals or shorter, the OM656 is expected to exceed 200,000 miles without major mechanical intervention [PATTERN DATA]. Emissions components may need attention between 100,000 and 160,000 miles but do not affect the engine's structural longevity.

Is the OM656 better than the OM642?

In reliability terms, yes. The OM656 eliminates the OM642's oil cooler seal failure, swirl flap motor issues, and the V-angle access complexity that makes OM642 repairs expensive. It is a fundamentally simpler, more durable design [PATTERN DATA]. See our OM651 vs OM642 comparison for the broader diesel family context.

Why doesn't the OM656 appear in your enquiry data?

The OM656 was introduced in 2017 and is fitted to fewer vehicles than the OM651 or OM642. Those vehicles have not yet reached peak replacement mileage. Low enquiry volume reflects both smaller parc size and lower failure incidence — not a data gap [PATTERN DATA].

What should I check before buying a used car with an OM656?

Confirm oil change intervals (ideally under 15,000 miles), check for any AdBlue warning history, verify the DPF has not been deleted or forced-regenerated excessively, and inspect the EGR cooler area for coolant residue. A full Mercedes service history is the single strongest predictor of trouble-free ownership [EM-INTERVIEW].
