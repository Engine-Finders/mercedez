What Each Model Costs to Replace – In Context

Beyond the numbers, here’s what those costs actually mean for real owners – based on our 2025 enquiry data and feedback from specialists.

C‑Class (W205) – OM651

With 368 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], the W205 C‑Class is the most common Mercedes we see. A recon OM651 at £2,800–£5,000 supply-only is well within the vehicle’s £8,000–£22,000 value band – the 70% threshold is comfortably cleared, making replacement a straightforward decision for most owners. The newer OM654 is even more affordable to source.

E‑Class (W212) – OM642

The Verdict – Which Mercedes Is Cheapest to Replace?

Based on our 2025 data, the Mercedes A‑Class and B‑Class with OM651 are the cheapest Mercedes models to re‑engine – recon costs from £2,800. The AMG GT with M177 is the most expensive at over £8,000 supply‑only. But cost alone doesn’t tell the whole story.

The real surprise: The C‑Class W205 and GLC X253 are both very affordable to replace relative to their high market values, making them ✅ Safe almost without exception. The E‑Class W212, while not expensive in absolute terms, sits on a lower value curve – a £5,000 OM642 recon on a £6,000 W212 is a 🔴 Scrap scenario. Always apply the 70% rule to your own car, not a generic “cheap” or “expensive” label.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average Mercedes engine replacement cost?

Across all models, the average recon supply cost is roughly £3,500–£5,500, but it varies widely. The cheapest are OM651 units at £2,800–£4,800, while AMG V8s can exceed £10,000. Fitted costs add £500–£1,500 for labour.

Which Mercedes model has the cheapest engine replacement?

The A‑Class (W176) and B‑Class (W246) with OM651 have the lowest recon costs at £2,800–£4,800. The C‑Class W205 with OM651 is also very affordable relative to its value.

How much does a Mercedes OM651 engine cost to replace?

Supply‑only, a reconditioned OM651 costs £2,800–£4,800; with fitting, expect £3,500–£5,600. Used units are cheaper at £1,500–£2,800 but carry more risk.

Is a Mercedes V8 engine worth replacing?

It depends on the vehicle value. For an AMG GT worth £40,000+, an £8,000–£14,000 recon is often viable; for an older S‑Class W221 worth £8,000, the same cost would exceed the 70% threshold – the answer is model‑ and condition‑specific.

What is the 70% rule for Mercedes engine replacement?

If the full fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of your Mercedes’ current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts. See the full framework page for details and a worked example.

Mercedes accounted for over 5,000 of our 24,650 total engine enquiries in 2025, with OM651 alone generating 1,250 requests [EM-VERIFIED].
