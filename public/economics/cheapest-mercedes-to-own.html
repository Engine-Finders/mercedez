The 70% repair‑to‑value threshold is the key to understanding which Mercedes is genuinely cheapest to own. Here's how the top contenders measure up.

C‑Class W205

The Verdict – Which Mercedes Is Actually Cheapest?

The answer depends on how you define "cheapest". Here's the honest verdict.

🏆 Cheapest to Repair: A‑Class W176 (OM651)

With recon supply from £2,800 and fitted costs from £3,500, the W176 A‑Class is the lowest‑cost Mercedes to re‑engine. If your priority is the absolute lowest repair cost, this is the car.

💰 Best Value for Money: C‑Class W205 (OM651)

The W205 C‑Class combines affordable OM651 replacement costs (£3,500–£5,800 fitted) with significantly stronger retained values (£8,000–£22,000). The 70% threshold is easily cleared, and the car offers more space, better refinement and higher residuals than the A‑Class – making it the smarter long‑term ownership choice.

🚗 Cheapest SUV: GLC X253 (OM651 / OM654)

The GLC is the cheapest Mercedes SUV to own. Recon costs are £2,800–£5,000, and high SUV values (£14,000–£38,000) mean the 70% threshold is almost never tested. For families wanting premium SUV ownership without premium repair bills, this is the one.

⚠️ Watch Zone: E‑Class W212 (OM642)

The W212 E‑Class is the cautionary tale. While OM651 variants are fine, OM642 V6 recon costs can reach £5,500, and on a £5,000–£6,000 example the 70% threshold is easily breached. If buying a W212, choose the OM651 diesel or ensure the car is worth more than £8,000 before committing to a V6 replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Mercedes is the cheapest to own overall?

The A‑Class W176 with the OM651 diesel is the cheapest to own based on engine replacement costs (£2,800–£4,800 recon). However, the C‑Class W205 offers better value for money due to stronger residuals – it's the smarter long‑term purchase for most buyers.

Is the A‑Class cheaper to run than the C‑Class?

Yes and no. The A‑Class has a slightly lower engine replacement cost (£2,800–£4,800 vs £2,800–£5,000) and lower labour hours. But the C‑Class holds its value significantly better, making total cost of ownership over 3–5 years often lower for the C‑Class despite a marginally higher repair cost.

Which Mercedes SUV is cheapest to own?

The GLC X253 is the cheapest Mercedes SUV to own. Recon costs for OM651/OM654 are £2,800–£5,000, and strong SUV values (£14,000–£38,000) mean the 70% threshold is easily cleared. The GLA is slightly cheaper to repair but has lower values overall.

Is an E‑Class W212 worth owning on a budget?

Yes – but choose the OM651 diesel variant. OM651 recon costs £2,800–£5,000 and fit well within the 70% threshold on most W212 examples. Avoid the OM642 V6 on sub‑£8k cars; the repair cost can exceed the vehicle value.

How does the 70% rule affect which Mercedes is cheapest?

The 70% rule means the repair cost must be less than 70% of the car's value. A car with a low repair cost but very low value (e.g., a £3,000 A‑Class with a £4,000 repair) fails the threshold. The cheapest cars to own are those with both low repair costs and strong values – the C‑Class W205 is the best example.

Which Mercedes engine is cheapest to replace?

The OM651 2.1‑litre diesel is the cheapest Mercedes engine to replace, with recon supply costs of £2,800–£4,800 and fitted costs from £3,500. It's used across A‑Class, B‑Class, C‑Class, GLC, GLA and E‑Class, making parts and specialist knowledge widely available.
