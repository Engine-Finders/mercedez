The short answer: a Mercedes M272 V6 failure is not automatically a write‑off - it depends entirely on your car's current value. A reconditioned M272 supply‑only costs £3,800–£6,500; fitted, you're looking at £4,500–£7,400 [EM-QUOTE]. Applying the 70% rule, if your car is worth more than £8,000, the repair is usually viable; below that, it's a write‑off.

  • M272 timing chain stretch typically appears at 60,000–120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]
  • Chain replacement alone is £1,500–£2,500, but a full engine replacement is often the smarter fix if the chain has snapped [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • In our 2025 data, M272 hosts like the W204 C‑Class and W212 E‑Class have typical values of £8,000–£30,000, making a recon engine a safe decision on most examples
  • If your car is worth under £6,000, the honest advice is usually to scrap it – the repair will exceed 70% of the car's value

When the Honest Answer Is "Write It Off"

A site that always recommends repair is not serving you. Here's when an M272 failure is genuinely a financial write‑off.

🚫

Vehicle Value Below £6,000

With recon fitted costs from £4,500, a £6,000 car hits 75% – above the 70% threshold. On a £4,000 car, the same repair exceeds 100% of the value. In these cases, the honest advice is to sell for parts or scrap.

🚫

High Mileage + Other Issues

If your M272 has covered over 120,000 miles and also needs suspension, brakes or gearbox work, the repair cost stacks up. Our data shows owners who stack multiple repairs on tired cars are the most likely to regret it – see When Should You Scrap a Mercedes?

🚫

Chain Already Snapped

If the timing chain has snapped, the engine is usually terminal. A bottom‑end rebuild can cost £6,000–£9,500, and the risk of hidden damage makes a replacement engine the only sensible option – at which point the 70% rule almost always fails on older cars.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an M272 timing chain failure always a write‑off?

No. If caught early (rattle on cold start) and repaired with a recon engine, it's often financially viable – provided your car is worth more than £8,000. Once the chain snaps, the repair cost typically exceeds the vehicle value, making it a write‑off.

How much does an M272 recon engine cost fitted?

Supply‑only a reconditioned M272 costs £3,800–£6,500; with fitting at an independent specialist, expect £4,580–£7,540. Labour is 12–16 hours at £65/hr.

Which Mercedes models have the M272 engine?

The M272 3.5‑litre V6 was fitted to the C‑Class (W204), E‑Class (W212), CLS (C218), SL (R230), GLK (X204), S‑Class (W221) and others. It was replaced by the M276 from around 2010–2012.

What is the 70% rule for M272 repairs?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your car's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For the M272, a £5,800 repair on a £8,500 car is 68% – safe. On a £6,000 car, it's 97% – scrap.

Can I replace just the timing chain on an M272?

Yes – the chain replacement cost is £1,500–£2,500. However, if the chain has stretched significantly, the tensioner and guides are also worn, and many specialists recommend a full recon engine instead, as the labour is similar and you get a warranty.

Is the M272 reliable overall?

The M272 is generally a robust V6, but the timing chain is a known weak point – especially on pre‑2010 examples. Regular oil changes and early diagnosis of cold‑start rattle can prevent catastrophic failure. Our M272 Knowledge Centre covers this in detail.
