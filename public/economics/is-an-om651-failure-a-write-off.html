The short answer: an OM651 failure is not automatically a write‑off – in fact, on most examples it's financially viable. A reconditioned OM651 supply‑only costs £2,800–£4,800; fitted, you're looking at £3,500–£5,600 [EM-QUOTE]. With typical OM651‑equipped Mercedes worth £5,000–£22,000, the 70% rule is comfortably cleared on the vast majority of cases.

  • The OM651 is the most‑enquired Mercedes engine in our 2025 data – 1,250 requests [EM-VERIFIED] – meaning parts and specialist knowledge are widely available
  • Common failures include timing chain tensioner wear and injector seal failure (Black Death) [PATTERN DATA]
  • A full engine replacement typically costs £3,500–£5,600 fitted – well below the 70% threshold on any Mercedes worth more than £5,000
  • If your car is worth under £4,000, the honest advice is usually to scrap it – the repair exceeds 70% of the car's value

When the Honest Answer Is "Write It Off"

The OM651 is usually a safe repair – but there are clear exceptions. Here's when an OM651 failure is genuinely a financial write‑off.

🚫

Vehicle Value Below £4,000

With recon fitted costs from £3,300, a £4,000 car hits 83% – above the 70% threshold. On a £2,500 car, the same repair exceeds 130% of the value. In these cases, the honest advice is to sell for parts or scrap.

🚫

Injector Seal Failure (Black Death) + Other Issues

If the injector seals have failed and caused carbon buildup, the repair can escalate quickly. When combined with a tired gearbox or suspension, the total bill often exceeds the 70% threshold. See When Should You Scrap a Mercedes?

🚫

E‑Class W211 or C‑Class W204 on Low Values

These older chassis can be worth as little as £2,500–£4,000. Even a £3,500 recon OM651 on a £3,000 W211 is 117% – a write‑off. If your W211 or W204 is worth under £5,000, the repair is usually not worth it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an OM651 timing chain failure always a write‑off?

No. The OM651 is one of the cheapest Mercedes engines to replace – recon costs from £2,800 supply‑only. On most C‑Class, GLC or GLA examples, the repair is comfortably within the 70% threshold. It's only a write‑off on very low‑value cars under £4,000.

How much does an OM651 recon engine cost fitted?

Supply‑only a reconditioned OM651 costs £2,800–£4,800; with fitting at an independent specialist, expect £3,320–£5,580. Labour is 8–12 hours at £65/hr.

Which Mercedes models have the OM651 engine?

The OM651 2.1‑litre diesel was fitted to the C‑Class (W204, W205), E‑Class (W211, W212), GLC (X253), GLA (X156), GLK (X204), A‑Class (W176), B‑Class (W246), Sprinter and Vito. It is Mercedes' most widely‑used modern diesel.

What is the 70% rule for OM651 repairs?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your car's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For the OM651, a £4,200 repair on a £9,500 car is 44% – safe. On a £3,500 car, it's 120% – scrap.

Can I replace just the timing chain on an OM651?

Yes – the chain replacement cost is £600–£1,500. However, if the tensioner has failed, the guides are also worn, and many specialists recommend a full recon engine instead, as the labour is similar and you get a warranty. [EM-INTERVIEW]

Is the OM651 reliable overall?

The OM651 is generally a reliable engine, but it has two common weak points: timing chain tensioner wear (TSB LI80.20-P-050001) and injector seal failure (Black Death). Regular oil changes and early diagnosis of cold‑start rattle can prevent catastrophic failure. Our OM651 Knowledge Centre covers this in detail.
