The Verdict – Is It Worth Replacing a GLC Engine?

The answer is overwhelmingly yes – the GLC is one of the safest engine replacement decisions in the Mercedes range. Here's the honest verdict.

With values from £14,000 upwards, the repair cost is typically 10–30% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold. Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth replacing a GLC engine?

Yes – on almost every GLC. The X253 and X254 have strong values (£14,000–£55,000) and engine replacement costs from £3,500, making the repair just 7–30% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold.

How much does a GLC engine replacement cost in the UK?

Costs vary by engine: OM651 diesel from £2,800 supply (£3,500 fitted), OM654 diesel from £3,000 supply (£3,700 fitted), and M276 V6 petrol from £3,800 supply (£4,600 fitted). Labour adds £520–£1,040 depending on the engine.

Which GLC generation is cheapest to replace?

Both generations are very affordable. The X253 with OM651 is slightly cheaper to source, but the X254 has higher values, making the repair an even smaller percentage of the car's total value.

What is the 70% rule for GLC engine replacement?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your GLC's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For a GLC worth £16,000 with a £4,200 repair, the ratio is 26% – safe. For an exceptional sub‑£8,000 example, it could approach 70% – but this is rare.

Is a GLC more expensive to replace than a C‑Class?

No – the GLC and C‑Class share the same engine families (OM651, OM654, M276) and have virtually identical replacement costs. The GLC's higher SUV values actually make the repair a smaller percentage of the car's value, making it an even safer decision.

Which GLC engine is most reliable?

The OM651 is generally reliable but has timing chain tensioner wear as a known weak point. The OM654 is more modern and generally more robust. Both are widely supported by specialists. See our OM651 and OM654 Knowledge Centres for full detail.
