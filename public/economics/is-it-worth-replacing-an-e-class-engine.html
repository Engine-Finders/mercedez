E‑Class Engine Replacement – By Generation

Each E‑Class generation has different economics. Here's how the W211, W212, W213 and W214 stack up against the 70% threshold.

W211 (2003–2009) – 🔴 Scrap (usually)

With values of £2,500–£9,000, the W211 is the marginal E‑Class. A £3,500 OM651 repair on a £3,000 car is 117% – a write‑off. Even on a £7,000 example, a £5,200 OM642 repair is 74% – borderline. Only the best W211s are worth repairing.

The Verdict – Is It Worth Replacing an E‑Class Engine?

The answer depends entirely on your generation and engine. Here's the honest verdict.

✅ Yes – W213 and W214 E‑Class

With values from £14,000 upwards, the repair cost is typically 10–40% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold. The OM654 is affordable and reliable, and the W213/W214's strong residual values make replacement a financially sensible decision. Replacement is almost always the right decision.

⚠️ It Depends – W212 E‑Class

The W212 is the borderline generation. On a £7,500+ example, replacement is usually safe. On a £5,000–£7,000 car, the repair often approaches or exceeds the 70% threshold – especially with the OM642 V6. Check your car's value carefully before committing.

🔴 No – W211 E‑Class (usually)

If your E‑Class is pre‑2009 (W211), an engine replacement is often not worth it. The car's value is typically £2,500–£9,000, and even a £3,500 OM651 repair can exceed the 70% threshold on cheaper examples. Only the best W211s – low‑mileage, well‑specified examples – are worth repairing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth replacing an E‑Class engine?

On modern E‑Classes (W213, W214) – yes. On the W212, it depends on the car's value and engine. On the W211, it's usually not worth it. Always apply the 70% rule to your individual car.

How much does an E‑Class engine replacement cost in the UK?

Costs vary by engine: OM651 diesel from £2,800 supply (£3,500 fitted), OM642 V6 diesel from £3,200 supply (£4,000 fitted), OM654 diesel from £3,000 supply (£3,700 fitted), and M276 V6 petrol from £3,800 supply (£4,600 fitted). Labour adds £520–£1,040 depending on the engine.

Which E‑Class generation is cheapest to replace?

The W213 and W214 with OM654 are the cheapest to source and have the highest values, making them the most financially sensible to replace. The W212 has similar costs but lower values, making it more marginal.

What is the 70% rule for E‑Class engine replacement?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your E‑Class's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For a W212 worth £7,500 with a £5,200 OM642 repair, the ratio is 69% – borderline. For a £5,500 car with the same repair, it's 95% – scrap.

Is a W212 E‑Class worth repairing?

It depends. On a £7,500+ W212 with the OM651, yes – the repair is under 70% of the car's value. On a sub‑£6,000 car with the OM642, no – the repair exceeds 70%. Always check your car's current value before deciding.

Which E‑Class engine is most reliable?

The OM651 is generally reliable but has timing chain tensioner wear as a known weak point. The OM642 has swirl flap and oil cooler seal issues. The OM654 is more modern and generally more robust. See our OM651, OM642 and OM654 Knowledge Centres for full detail.
