S‑Class Engine Replacement – By Generation

Each S‑Class generation has different economics. Here's how the W221, W222, W223 and C217 stack up against the 70% threshold.

W221 (2005–2013) – ⚠️ Watch

With values of £5,000–£18,000, the W221 is the marginal S‑Class. A £4,000 OM642 repair is 22–80% – safe on £6,000+ examples, marginal on cheaper cars. The M278 V8 (£6,000–£9,200 fitted) pushes the ratio higher – check your car's value carefully.

The Verdict – Is It Worth Replacing an S‑Class Engine?

The S‑Class is a flagship – and flagship values usually make engine replacement worthwhile. Here's the honest verdict.

✅ Yes – W222 and W223 S‑Class

With values from £15,000 upwards, the repair cost is typically 10–40% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold. The OM642, M276 and even M177 are affordable relative to the car's value. Replacement is almost always the right decision.

⚠️ It Depends – W221 S‑Class

The W221 is the marginal generation. On a £7,500+ example, replacement is usually safe. On a £5,000–£7,000 car, the repair often approaches or exceeds the 70% threshold – especially with V8 engines. Check your car's value carefully before committing.

🔴 No – W221 with V12 (usually)

A V12 (M275/M279) replacement can cost £12,000–£22,000 fitted. On a W221 worth £5,000–£10,000, this is 120–440% of the car's value – a definite write‑off. Unless the car has exceptional value or sentimental significance, a V12 W221 is almost never worth repairing.

🏆 Flagship Value – The S‑Class Advantage

The S‑Class's flagship status means strong residual values. Even a £9,000 OM642 repair on a £25,000 W222 is just 36% – well under the threshold. For W222 and W223 owners, replacement is one of the easiest decisions in the Mercedes range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth replacing an S‑Class engine?

On modern S‑Classes (W222, W223) – yes. The W221 is marginal – check your car's value. V12 W221s are almost never worth it due to the enormous replacement cost.

How much does an S‑Class engine replacement cost in the UK?

Costs vary by engine: OM642 diesel from £3,200 supply (£4,000 fitted), M276 petrol from £3,800 supply (£4,600 fitted), M278 V8 from £5,000 supply (£6,000 fitted), M177 AMG V8 from £8,000 supply (£9,000 fitted), and V12 (M275/M279) from £10,000–£20,000+ supply (£12,000–£22,000+ fitted). Labour adds £650–£1,950 depending on the engine.

Which S‑Class generation is cheapest to replace?

The W222 and W223 with OM642 or M276 are the most financially sensible – the engines are affordable and the car's value is high. The W221 has similar costs but lower values, making it more marginal.

What is the 70% rule for S‑Class engine replacement?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your S‑Class's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For a W222 worth £25,000 with a £5,200 OM642 repair, the ratio is 21% – safe. For a W221 worth £6,500 with the same repair, it's 80% – scrap.

Is a V12 S‑Class worth repairing?

Almost never. V12 replacement costs £12,000–£22,000 fitted – even on a £20,000 W222, this is 60–110% of the car's value. On a £8,000 W221, it's 150–275% – a clear write‑off. V12s are for owners with very strong sentimental attachment or exceptional car values.

Which S‑Class engine is most reliable?

The OM642 is generally reliable but has swirl flap and oil cooler seal issues. The M276 petrol is well‑regarded. The M278 V8 has timing chain concerns. V12s are complex and expensive to maintain. See our OM642, M276 and M278 Knowledge Centres for full detail.
