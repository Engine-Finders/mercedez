#1 – M177 Cylinder Scoring – The AMG GT's Achilles Heel

The M177 4.0‑litre V8 is a masterpiece of engineering – but cylinder scoring on the AMG GT, C63 and E63 can lead to repair bills that exceed £15,000.

What Goes Wrong

Cylinder scoring (vertical scratches on the cylinder wall) is caused by piston ring failure, often linked to fuel dilution or overheating. Symptoms include high oil consumption, blue smoke on startup, and a misfire under load. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 40,000–80,000 miles

The Cost

Recon engine: £8,000–£14,000 supply [EM-QUOTE]
Labour: 16–22 hrs (£1,040–£1,430)
Fitted total: £9,000–£15,400
Dealer cost: £11,000–£18,000+
The verdict: On an AMG GT worth £40,000+, a £12,000 repair is just 30% – a clear yes. On a C63 worth £20,000, the same repair is 60% – borderline. On a sub‑£15,000 C63, it's a write‑off.

#2 – M157 Turbo Failure – The High‑Mileage Nightmare

The M157 5.5‑litre V8 in the E63, S63, CLS63 and G63 is prone to turbocharger failure – a repair that can cost over £11,000.

What Goes Wrong

Hot‑vee turbo design leads to heat‑related failures – oil coking, bearing wear, and turbine shaft failure. Symptoms include loss of power, excessive turbo whistle, and blue smoke. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 60,000–120,000 miles

The Cost

Recon engine: £6,000–£10,000 supply [EM-QUOTE]
Labour: 16–22 hrs (£1,040–£1,430)
Fitted total: £7,000–£11,400
Dealer cost: £9,000–£14,000+
The verdict: The M157 is common in higher‑mileage AMGs. On a £25,000 E63, a £9,000 repair is 36% – safe. On a £12,000 S63, it's 75% – scrap. With 410 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], this is the most common AMG engine failure.

#3 – M156 Head Bolt Failure – The Classic AMG Pitfall

The naturally aspirated 6.2‑litre M156 is one of AMG's greatest engines – but pre‑2013 examples suffer from head bolt failure, a repair that can cost over £8,000.

What Goes Wrong

Head bolts stretch and fail due to poor metallurgy on pre‑2013 engines. Coolant loss, overheating and eventual head gasket failure are the result. The fix is a full head bolt replacement with updated bolts – or a complete engine rebuild. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 40,000–80,000 miles

The Cost

Head bolt repair: £2,500–£4,500
Recon engine: £4,000–£7,000 supply
Fitted total: £5,500–£8,500
Full rebuild: £8,000–£12,500
The verdict: The M156 is found in C63, E63, S63 and SL63. On a £25,000 C63, a £6,000 repair is 24% – safe. On a £10,000 C63, the same repair is 60% – borderline. Pre‑2013 cars are at risk – factor this into any purchase decision.

The Verdict – Is AMG Ownership Worth the Risk?

AMG engines are expensive to repair – but on high‑value cars, they're almost always worth it. Here's the honest verdict.

✅ Yes – AMG GT, Newer C63/E63 (W205/W213)

With values from £30,000 upwards, even a £12,000 M177 repair is just 20–40% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold. Replacement is almost always the right decision.

⚠️ It Depends – W204 C63, W212 E63, M156/M157

These older AMGs have values of £10,000–£25,000. An M156 head bolt repair at £4,000–£6,000 is often safe, but an M157 turbo failure at £7,000–£11,400 is marginal. Check your car's value carefully.

🔴 No – Very Low‑Value AMGs (Under £15,000)

A £10,000 W204 C63 with a £7,000 M157 repair is 70% – borderline. The same car with an M177 cylinder scoring repair at £12,000 is 120% – a clear write‑off. If your AMG is worth under £15,000, a major engine repair is often not worth it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most expensive AMG repair?

The M177 cylinder scoring repair on the AMG GT, C63 and E63 is the most expensive – fitted costs range from £9,000 to £15,400. M157 turbo failure is a close second at £7,000–£11,400.

How much does an AMG engine replacement cost?

An M177 recon engine costs £8,000–£14,000 supply; fitted, £9,000–£15,400. An M157 recon costs £6,000–£10,000 supply; fitted, £7,000–£11,400. M156 head bolt repair is cheaper at £2,500–£4,500.

Is it worth replacing an AMG engine?

On high‑value AMGs (AMG GT, newer C63/E63) – yes. On older W204 C63s or W212 E63s worth under £20,000, a major repair can exceed the 70% threshold – check your car's value carefully.

What is the 70% rule for AMG repairs?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your AMG's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For a £40,000 AMG GT with a £12,000 repair, the ratio is 30% – safe. For a £12,000 C63 with the same repair, it's 100% – scrap.

Which AMG engine is most reliable?

The M156 is reliable after the head bolt issue is fixed (post‑2013). The M177 is generally robust but has cylinder scoring on some examples. The M157 is prone to turbo failure – 410 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED] make it the most common AMG failure. See our M177, M157 and M156 Knowledge Centres.

Should I buy an AMG with high mileage?

High‑mileage AMGs are risky – especially pre‑2013 M156s (head bolts) and M157s (turbo failures). Budget £5,000–£10,000 for potential repairs, or buy a post‑2013 car with a proven service history. Our Most Expensive AMG Repairs guide has full details.
