#1 – M177 Cylinder Scoring – The AMG Achilles Heel

The M177 4.0‑litre V8 is a masterpiece of engineering – but cylinder scoring on the AMG GT, C63 and E63 can lead to repair bills that exceed £15,000.

What Goes Wrong

Cylinder scoring (vertical scratches on the cylinder wall) is caused by piston ring failure, often linked to fuel dilution or overheating. Symptoms include high oil consumption, blue smoke on startup, and a misfire under load. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 40,000–80,000 miles

The Cost

Recon engine: £8,000–£14,000 supply [EM-QUOTE]
Labour: 16–22 hrs (£1,040–£1,430)
Fitted total: £9,000–£15,400
Dealer cost: £11,000–£18,000+
The verdict: On an AMG GT worth £40,000+, a £12,000 repair is just 30% – a clear yes. On a C63 worth £20,000, the same repair is 60% – borderline. On a sub‑£15,000 C63, it's a write‑off.

#2 – M278 Timing Chain Failure – The S‑Class V8 Issue

The M278 4.7‑litre V8 in the S500, CLS500 and GLE500 is prone to timing chain failure – a repair that can cost over £9,000.

What Goes Wrong

Timing chain stretch and tensioner failure are well‑documented on the M278. Symptoms include cold‑start rattle, engine warning light, and in severe cases, catastrophic engine failure. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 60,000–120,000 miles

The Cost

Chain repair: £2,500–£4,000
Recon engine: £5,000–£8,000 supply
Fitted total: £6,000–£9,200
Dealer cost: £8,000–£12,000+
The verdict: On a £30,000 S500, a £7,500 repair is 25% – safe. On a £12,000 CLS500, the same repair is 63% – borderline. Always check your car's value – these V8s depreciate faster than the V12s.

#3 – V12 Repairs – The Flagship Tax

The M275 and M279 V12 engines in the S600, S65, SL65 and G65 are the most expensive Mercedes engines to repair – and often the least financially sensible.

What Goes Wrong

V12 engines suffer from ignition coil failure (12 coils, each expensive), oil leaks from the valley, and timing chain issues. Full engine replacement is the only option for catastrophic failures. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 40,000–100,000 miles

The Cost

Ignition coils (12): £2,000–£4,000
Recon engine: £10,000–£20,000+ supply
Fitted total: £12,000–£22,000+
Dealer cost: £15,000–£28,000+
The verdict: V12 repairs are almost never worth it on older cars. A £15,000 repair on a £10,000 W221 S600 is 150% – a definite write‑off. Only consider if your car is worth £30,000+ or has exceptional sentimental value.

#4 – OM642 Oil Cooler Seal – The V6 Diesel Issue

The OM642 V6 diesel is common across the Mercedes range – and its oil cooler seal is a well‑known weak point. While the repair itself is manageable, it can lead to full engine failure if ignored.

What Goes Wrong

The oil cooler O‑ring fails, causing oil and coolant to mix. Symptoms include coolant contamination, oil in the expansion tank, and overheating. If caught early, the repair is £800–£1,500. If ignored, the engine can suffer terminal damage. [PATTERN DATA]

Typical mileage: 70,000–150,000 miles

The Cost

Oil cooler seal: £800–£1,500 [EM-QUOTE]
Labour: 10–14 hrs
Recon engine: £3,200–£5,500 supply
Fitted total: £4,000–£6,400
The verdict: The oil cooler seal repair is a no‑brainer – £1,000 is well worth it on any Mercedes. But if the engine fails, the full replacement cost is significant – especially on older E‑Classes and GLs worth under £7,000.

The Verdict – Is Mercedes Ownership Worth the Risk?

Mercedes repairs can be expensive – but on high‑value cars, they're almost always worth it. Here's the honest verdict.

✅ Yes – Newer S‑Class, AMG GT, GLE, GLS

With values from £25,000 upwards, even a £9,000 M278 repair is just 36% of the car's value – well under the 70% threshold. Replacement is almost always the right decision.

⚠️ It Depends – W221 S‑Class, W212 E‑Class, Older AMGs

These cars have values of £7,000–£25,000. An OM642 oil cooler seal is always worth it. But a £9,000 M278 repair on a £12,000 CLS500 is 75% – scrap territory. Check your car's value carefully.

🔴 No – V12 W221/W220, Very Low‑Value Mercedes

A £15,000 V12 repair on a £8,000 S600 is 188% – a clear write‑off. If your Mercedes is worth under £7,000 and the repair quote exceeds that figure, the honest advice is usually to scrap or sell for parts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most expensive Mercedes repair?

The M177 cylinder scoring repair on the AMG GT, C63 and E63 is the most expensive – fitted costs range from £9,000 to £15,400. V12 engine replacement (M275/M279) is even higher at £12,000–£22,000+.

How much does a Mercedes engine replacement cost?

Costs vary by engine: OM651 4‑cyl from £3,500 fitted, OM642 V6 from £4,000 fitted, M278 V8 from £6,000 fitted, M177 AMG V8 from £9,000 fitted, and V12 M275/M279 from £12,000–£22,000+ fitted.

Is it worth replacing a Mercedes engine?

On newer, high‑value Mercedes (S‑Class, AMG GT, GLE) – yes. On older cars (W221, W212) with values under £10,000 – often no. Apply the 70% rule to your individual car.

What is the 70% rule for Mercedes repairs?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your Mercedes's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. For a £30,000 S500 with a £7,500 M278 repair, the ratio is 25% – safe. For a £10,000 S600 with a £15,000 V12 repair, it's 150% – scrap.

Which Mercedes model has the most expensive repairs?

The S‑Class, AMG GT and GLE with V8 or V12 engines have the most expensive repairs. The M177 AMG V8 and V12 M275/M279 are the costliest to replace, with fitted costs often exceeding £10,000.

Should I buy an older S‑Class with high mileage?

Be very careful – the OM642 V6 is generally reliable, but the M278 V8 and V12 M275/M279 can be ruinously expensive to repair.
