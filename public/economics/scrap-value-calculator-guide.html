What Is Scrap Value?

Scrap value is what a breaker or recycler will pay for your Mercedes – typically based on the weight of metal, the value of reusable parts, and the current market for scrap materials.

💰 Typical Scrap Values (2025 UK)

Small car (A‑Class)£150–£400 Saloon (C‑Class/E‑Class)£200–£600 Large saloon (S‑Class)£300–£800 SUV (GLC/GLE)£250–£700 Van (Sprinter)£400–£1,000

Figures are [INDUSTRY ESTIMATE] – actual prices vary by condition and current metal prices.

🔧 Parts That Affect Scrap Value

  • Engine – still runs? higher value
  • Gearbox – working? adds value
  • Catalytic converter – often £100–£300 alone
  • Alloy wheels – sell separately for more
  • Body panels – demand varies by model

Key point: Scrap value is lower than part-exchange or private sale value. A £500 scrap quote might mean £1,000–£2,000 if you sell parts individually or trade the car in. Always compare options.

How to Calculate Your Mercedes Scrap Value

A step-by-step guide to estimating the scrap value of your Mercedes – and comparing it to repair or replacement costs.

1

Get a Repair Quote

First, know what the repair or replacement would cost. Use our calculator or get a quote from a specialist. This is the cost you're comparing against.

2

Check Your Car's Market Value

Get a current trade valuation – we recommend Auto Trader, Parker's, or a dealer valuation. This is the "vehicle value" in the 70% rule. Don't use what you paid or think it's worth – use today's figure.

3

Get a Scrap Quote

Contact 3–5 local scrap yards or specialist breakers. Give them the reg, model, condition and whether the engine runs. Prices vary by up to 200% – always get multiple quotes.

4

Apply the 70% Rule

Calculate the repair-to-value ratio: Repair Cost ÷ Vehicle Value. If the ratio is above 70%, scrapping is usually the honest answer. If below 70%, repair is often the better option.

5

Compare Scrap vs Part-Ex

A part-exchange deal might give you £1,000–£2,000 more than scrap. It's often worth spending an afternoon on comparison sites to see the difference.

6

Make the Decision

If scrap > repair value or repair-to-value ratio > 70%, scrap. If the car is valuable or the repair is affordable, repair. Use our calculator to run both scenarios.

The 70% Rule – When Scrapping Is the Answer

The 70% rule is the simplest way to decide whether to repair or scrap your Mercedes. Here's how to use it.

The Formula

Repair Cost ÷ Current Vehicle Value = Ratio

If the ratio is above 70%, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts. If below 70%, repair is typically the better financial decision.

Worked Example: W211 E‑Class OM642

Vehicle: 2008 E‑Class W211 E320 CDI (OM642)
Repair quote: £5,200 (fitted recon)
Current trade value: £3,500
Scrap value: £400

Calculation: £5,200 ÷ £3,500 = 149% – well above 70%.

Verdict: Scrap or sell for parts. The repair cost far exceeds the car's value. Even with the scrap value offset (£5,200 – £400 = £4,800 loss), the repair is not financially sensible.

Always check your car's current value before deciding – valuations can change quickly. Use our calculator to run your own numbers.

How to Scrap Your Mercedes – Step by Step

If you've decided to scrap your Mercedes, here's what to do – including how to get the best price.

1. Get Multiple Scrap Quotes

Contact 3–5 local scrap yards or specialist breakers. Prices vary wildly – a £200 quote in one place might be £500 elsewhere.

2. Remove Valuable Parts (if you can)

If you have the skills and tools, selling parts separately (alloys, cat, interior) can yield more than the scrap value. But factor in your time.

3. Prepare the Paperwork

You need the V5C logbook and your driving licence. The scrappie will issue a Certificate of Destruction (CoD) – you'll need this to notify the DVLA.

4. Notify the DVLA

Once the car is scrapped, notify the DVLA to avoid fines and tax liability. Most scrappies will do this for you.

5. Cancel Insurance & Tax

Cancel your insurance and claim a refund on any remaining road tax – this can add £100–£200 to your overall recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is scrap value for a Mercedes in the UK?

Typical scrap values range from £150 for a small A‑Class to £1,000 for a Sprinter van. Most Mercedes saloons (C‑Class/E‑Class) fetch £200–£600. These figures are [INDUSTRY ESTIMATE] – always get multiple quotes.

How do I calculate my Mercedes scrap value?

Get quotes from 3–5 local scrap yards or breakers. Give them the make, model, year, condition, and whether the engine runs. You can also use online scrap value calculators – but quotes vary significantly, so always get real offers.

Should I scrap my Mercedes if the engine has failed?

Apply the 70% rule. If the engine repair cost is more than 70% of your car's current market value, scrapping is usually the best financial decision. For a W211 E‑Class worth £3,500, a £5,200 repair is 149% – scrap. For a W205 C‑Class worth £15,000, the same repair is 35% – repair.

Is part-exchange better than scrapping?

Often yes. A dealer will usually offer more than a scrap yard – sometimes £1,000–£2,000 more, especially for a car that still runs or has valuable parts. Always get a part-exchange quote before deciding to scrap.

What parts increase a Mercedes scrap value?

Working engine and gearbox add the most value. Catalytic converters (often £100–£300), alloy wheels, and body panels in good condition also increase the price. A broken engine car is worth much less than one that still runs.

Is it worth scrapping a Mercedes that needs an engine?

It depends on the model. A W205 C‑Class with a failed OM651 is usually worth repairing – the car's value justifies the cost. A W211 E‑Class with a failed OM642 is often not worth it – the repair cost exceeds the car's value. Always apply the 70% rule to your individual car.
