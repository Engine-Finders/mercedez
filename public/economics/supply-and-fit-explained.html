What Does Supply and Fit Mean?

In plain terms, supply and fit is the total cost to replace your engine – the engine itself, the labour, and all the bits needed to make it work. But it's not always as simple as it sounds.

📦 Supply

The engine itself – purchased from a supplier. It can be:

  • Reconditioned – cleaned and fitted with new wearing parts
  • Used – as‑removed, with unknown history
  • Rebuilt – full strip‑down and rebuild to factory spec

Supply costs vary wildly – see our comparison guide.

🔧 Fit

The labour to remove the old engine and install the new one, plus:

  • Labour hours – typically 8–30 hours depending on engine
  • Fluids – oil, coolant, air‑con gas
  • Gaskets & seals – often supplied but not always
  • Ancillaries – belts, hoses, sensors that need replacing

Labour rates vary by region – see Labour Rates & Fitting Times.

Key point: Some quotes include ancillaries and fluids; others don't. Always ask for a detailed breakdown – a cheap quote might be missing essentials.

What's Included in a "Supply and Fit" Quote?

A typical Mercedes engine replacement quote is made up of four main cost categories. Here's what each includes – and what often gets overlooked.

1

Supply Cost (Engine)

The core cost – reconditioned, used or rebuilt. Typical supply costs from our 2025 data:

  • OM651: £2,800–£4,800 [EM-QUOTE]
  • OM642: £3,200–£5,500 [EM-QUOTE]
  • M177: £8,000–£14,000 [EM-QUOTE]

Often includes a 6–24 month warranty.

2

Labour

Based on hours × hourly rate. Our 2025 figures:

  • OM651: 8–12 hrs (£520–£780)
  • OM642: 10–14 hrs (£650–£910)
  • M177: 16–22 hrs (£1,040–£1,430)

Labour rates: indie £65–£85/hr; main dealer £130–£180/hr.

3

Ancillaries & Fluids

Items often sold separately:

  • Engine oil & filter: £80–£150
  • Coolant: £50–£100
  • Gaskets & seals: £100–£300
  • Belts, hoses, sensors: £100–£400

This is the most common hidden cost – ask if it's included.

4

Incidentals

Items that might be needed depending on your car:

  • Turbo replacement: £800–£2,000
  • Injectors: £300–£800 each
  • DPF cleaning/replacement: £400–£1,200
  • Clutch (manual): £500–£1,000

These can double the final bill – ask about them upfront.

What to Ask Before Accepting a Quote

A supply-and-fit quote is only as good as what it includes. Here are the questions to ask every specialist before you commit.

1. What's included in the supply?

Is it reconditioned, used or rebuilt? What warranty is included? Are gaskets, seals and timing chain included?

2. What's the labour rate and estimated hours?

Ask for the hourly rate and the number of hours estimated. Compare this to our labour guide to see if it's fair.

3. What ancillaries are extra?

Turbo, injectors, DPF, clutch – these are often not included. Ask upfront to avoid a surprise bill mid‑job.

4. Is the fitting guaranteed?

Most specialists offer a warranty on the fitted work – typically 6–24 months. Make sure it's in writing.

5. Can I have an itemised quote?

Never accept a single lump-sum figure. An itemised quote allows you to compare like-for-like and spot missing items.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does supply and fit mean for Mercedes engines?

Supply and fit means the cost of the engine itself (supply) plus the labour to remove the old engine and install the new one (fit). It usually also includes fluids and basic gaskets, but may not include ancillaries like turbo, injectors or clutch.

How much does a Mercedes engine supply and fit cost?

Costs vary by engine: OM651 £3,500–£5,600 fitted, OM642 £4,000–£6,400, M276 £4,600–£7,500, M177 £9,000–£15,400, V12 M275/M279 £12,000–£23,000+ fitted. These figures include supply, labour and basic ancillaries [EM-QUOTE].

What's usually not included in a supply and fit quote?

Turbochargers, injectors, DPF, clutch, and major cooling system components are often not included. These can add £500–£3,000 to the final bill – always ask if they're included or extra.

Why is the fitted cost higher than the supply cost?

Because labour, fluids, gaskets, seals and ancillaries all add to the total. For an OM651, supply is £2,800–£4,800, but fitted cost is £3,500–£5,600 – labour and ancillaries add £700–£1,000.

Should I choose an independent specialist or a main dealer?

Independents are typically 40–60% cheaper and often have more Mercedes-specific experience. Main dealers are more expensive but may be required for warranty or service history reasons. See our labour rates guide for full comparison.

Can I buy my own engine and get it fitted?

Yes – some specialists will fit an engine you supply, but many won't warranty the work. You also lose the supply warranty. It's usually better to let the specialist source the engine so they stand behind the whole job.
