The 70% Rule – The Simple Way to Decide

The 70% rule is the simplest and most reliable way to decide whether to scrap or repair your Mercedes. Here's how it works.

The Formula

Repair Cost ÷ Current Vehicle Value = Ratio

If the ratio is above 70%, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts. If below 70%, repair is typically the better financial decision.

Why 70%?

70% reflects the typical parts and labour baseline for Mercedes and BMW engines in our enquiry data – higher than Land Rover and Jaguar's 60% threshold. It's reviewed against real enquiry outcomes and is deliberately conservative to account for uncertainty in vehicle valuations.

When Should You Scrap Your Mercedes?

Beyond the 70% rule, there are specific scenarios where scrapping is the clear answer. Here are the five most common situations.

🔴

Repair Cost > 70% of Vehicle Value

This is the primary trigger. If a £5,200 OM642 repair on a £3,500 W211 E‑Class gives a 149% ratio – scrap. Always calculate the ratio before deciding.

🔴

Vehicle Value Under £4,000

Below this line, almost no engine fault clears the 70% threshold. Even a £3,500 OM651 repair on a £4,000 car is 88% – scrap. See our scrap value guide.

🔴

V12 Engine Failure

M275/M279 V12 replacement costs £12,000–£22,000+ fitted. On a W221 S600 worth £8,000, the ratio is 150–275% – almost always a write‑off.

🔴

Multiple Major Failures

If the engine has failed AND the gearbox, suspension or bodywork needs serious work, the repair bill stacks up. Often better to scrap and start fresh.

🔴

Ageing W211 E‑Class or W204 C‑Class

These models are at the bottom of their value curve. A £5,000 repair on a £3,500 car is 143% – scrap. Always check the current value before committing.

How to Scrap Your Mercedes – Step by Step

If you've decided to scrap your Mercedes, here's what to do – including how to get the best price.

1. Get Multiple Quotes

Contact 3–5 local scrap yards or specialist breakers. Prices vary by up to 200% – always shop around.

2. Remove Valuable Parts (if you can)

Selling parts separately (alloys, cat, interior) can yield more than scrap value. Factor in your time and skills.

3. Prepare the Paperwork

You need the V5C logbook and driving licence. The scrappie will issue a Certificate of Destruction (CoD).

4. Notify the DVLA

Once the car is scrapped, notify the DVLA to avoid fines and tax liability. Most scrappies do this for you.

5. Cancel Insurance & Tax

Cancel your insurance and claim a refund on any remaining road tax – this can add £100–£200 to your recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I scrap my Mercedes?

When the repair cost exceeds 70% of the car's current market value. For a W211 E‑Class worth £3,500, a £5,200 repair is 149% – scrap. For a W205 C‑Class worth £15,000, the same repair is 35% – repair.

Is a W211 E‑Class worth repairing?

Usually not. With typical values of £2,500–£9,000, a £4,000–£6,400 OM642 repair can easily exceed 70% of the car's value. Always check your car's value before deciding.

Is a W221 S‑Class V12 worth repairing?

Almost never. V12 replacement costs £12,000–£22,000+ – on a W221 worth £8,000, the ratio is 150–275% – a clear write‑off. Only consider if the car has exceptional sentimental value or is worth over £30,000.

Is a W205 C‑Class worth repairing?

Yes – almost always. With values of £8,000–£22,000, a £3,500–£5,600 OM651 repair is just 16–70% – comfortably under the threshold. The W205 is one of the safest Mercedes to repair.

What is the 70% rule for Mercedes?

The 70% rule: if the full fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your Mercedes's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts. It's a starting guide – always get a current valuation before deciding.

How much is my Mercedes worth for scrap?

Typical scrap values range from £150 for an A‑Class to £1,000 for a Sprinter van. Most saloons fetch £200–£600. Always get 3–5 quotes – prices vary significantly. See our scrap value guide for details.
