The short answer: Mercedes engine failure can result in one of four write-off categories. Category N (non-structural damage) and Category S (structural damage) are the most common for engine failures – and both can be repaired. Category A and B mean the car cannot be repaired and must be scrapped. The deciding factor is whether the repair cost exceeds 70% of the car's value – which is also the basis of our 70% rule.

  • Category A: Scrap only – no parts can be reused
  • Category B: Scrap – but some parts can be salvaged
  • Category S: Structural damage – repairable, but needs inspection
  • Category N: Non-structural damage – repairable (most engine failures)
  • 70% rule: If repair cost > 70% of vehicle value, even a Category S or N car is often not worth repairing

Write-Off Categories Explained

When a Mercedes is declared a write-off, it's assigned one of four categories. Here's what each means – and whether the car can be repaired.

A

Category A

Scrap only. The car is too badly damaged to be repaired. No parts can be salvaged – the entire vehicle must be crushed. Category A is rare for engine failure alone – it usually requires catastrophic structural damage or fire.

B

Category B

Scrap, but parts can be salvaged. The bodyshell cannot be reused, but mechanical parts (engine, gearbox, interior) can be removed and sold. Some engine failures on high-value models can result in Category B if the shell is damaged.

S

Category S

Structural damage – repairable. The car has suffered structural damage (chassis, subframe) but can be repaired. An engine failure with ancillary structural damage can fall into Category S. Requires a structural inspection after repair.

N

Category N

Non-structural damage – repairable. The most common write-off category for engine failure. The engine has failed, but the shell is sound. Repair is possible – and often the right decision if the car's value justifies it. No structural inspection required.

Category S and N cars can be repaired and returned to the road. However, they carry a write-off marker on the V5C, which can affect future sale value. Always factor this into your decision.

Engine Failure and Write-Off Categories

Most Mercedes engine failures result in a Category N write-off – the engine is damaged, but the shell is sound. Here's how it plays out in practice.

Common Scenarios

🔧 OM651 Timing Chain Failure

Engine needs replacement. Shell is sound. Category N – repairable.

🔧 OM642 Swirl Flap Failure

Engine failure. Shell is sound. Category N – repairable.

🔧 M177 AMG V8 Cylinder Scoring

Engine failure. Shell is sound. Category N – repairable (but expensive).

🚗 Engine Failure + Accident Damage

If the car has also been in an accident, it may be Category S – structural damage.

The Write-Off Threshold

An insurer declares a car a write-off when the repair cost exceeds 50–70% of the car's pre-accident value. This is similar to our 70% rule – if the repair cost is too high relative to the car's value, the insurer will write it off instead of repairing it.

The 70% Rule – Write-Off or Repair?

Our 70% repair-to-value threshold is the best way to decide whether a Category N or S Mercedes is worth repairing – even if the insurer has written it off.

The Formula

Repair Cost ÷ Current Vehicle Value = Ratio

If the ratio is above 70%, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts – even if the insurer has assigned a Category N or S. If below 70%, repair is typically the better financial decision.

Worked Example: W211 E‑Class OM642

Vehicle: 2008 E‑Class W211 E320 CDI
Repair quote: £5,200
Current trade value: £3,500
Write-off category: Category N (engine failure)

Calculation: £5,200 ÷ £3,500 = 149% – well above 70%.

Verdict: Even though the car is Category N (repairable), the repair cost far exceeds the car's value. The honest answer is to scrap or sell for parts – the Category N marker doesn't change the economics.

Buying a Write-Off Mercedes – What to Know

If you're considering buying a Category S or N Mercedes, here's what you need to know – and how the 70% rule applies.

1. Check the Write-Off Category

Category N (non-structural) is safer than Category S (structural). Category S requires a structural inspection after repair – and can affect insurance and resale value.

2. Get a Full History

Ask for the repair invoice, photos of the damage, and the structural inspection report (for Category S). A well-repaired write-off can be a bargain – but a poorly repaired one is a money pit.

3. Apply the 70% Rule

If the car needs an engine, apply the 70% rule – even a Category N car isn't worth repairing if the cost exceeds 70% of the post-repair value.

4. Insurance and Resale

Category S and N cars are harder to insure and sell. Some insurers refuse to cover Category S cars. Factor this into your purchase price – you should pay 20–40% less than a non-write-off car.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Category N write-off?

Category N means the car has suffered non-structural damage – such as an engine failure – and can be repaired. It's the most common write-off category for engine failures.

What is a Category S write-off?

Category S means the car has suffered structural damage (chassis, subframe) and can be repaired – but requires a structural inspection after repair. It's less common for engine failures unless the car has also been in an accident.

Can a Category N Mercedes be repaired?

Yes – Category N cars can be repaired and returned to the road. The V5C will carry a marker, but the car is roadworthy once repaired. Always check the repair quality and apply the 70% rule.

Is a Category S Mercedes worth repairing?

It depends. If the structural damage is minor and the repair is done properly, a Category S car can be a bargain. But it's harder to insure and sell – and the repair must be inspected. Always get the structural inspection report.

How does the 70% rule apply to write-offs?

The 70% rule applies to the repair cost vs the car's current value – not the insurer's write-off decision. A Category N car with a £5,000 repair on a £3,500 car is 149% – scrap. The Category N marker doesn't change the economics.

Should I buy a write-off Mercedes?

Only if the repair is documented, the category is N (not S), and the price reflects the write-off marker – typically 20–40% less than a non-write-off car. Always apply the 70% rule before committing.
