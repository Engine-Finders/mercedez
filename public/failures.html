The Short Answer

Mercedes engine failures are often predictable and well-documented. The most common issues include timing chain stretch (OM651, M276), injector seal leaks (OM651, OM642), oil cooler seal leaks (OM642), and balance shaft wear (M272). In 2025, the OM651 was the most enquired‑about engine, with 1,250 requests [EM‑VERIFIED].

Failures by Symptom

Diagnose your issue by the symptom you're experiencing. Each symptom links to the most likely failure pages.

  • Cold‑start rattle – often timing chain tensioner wear (OM651, M276, M272).
  • Black smoke from exhaust – injector seal failure (OM651), EGR issues, or overfuelling.
  • Blue smoke – oil cooler seal leak (OM642), valve stem seals.
  • Engine knocking – severe timing chain stretch, bearing wear.
  • Oil in coolant – oil cooler seal failure (OM642).
  • Overheating – coolant leaks, water pump failure, blocked EGR cooler.
  • Limp mode – often linked to DPF/AdBlue issues (OM654, OM651) or transmission faults.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common Mercedes engine failure?

Based on our 2025 enquiry data, the OM651 timing chain tensioner wear is the most frequently reported failure, with 1,250 requests. It affects C‑Class, E‑Class, GLC, and Sprinter models. Early detection is critical.

How do I know if my Mercedes engine is about to fail?

Listen for cold‑start rattle, look for blue or black smoke, check for oil in coolant, and watch for warning lights. Use our Symptom Hub to narrow down the issue.

Is it worth repairing a Mercedes engine failure?

It depends on the vehicle value and the repair cost. Use the 70% scrap threshold as a guideline: if the repair cost exceeds 70% of the car's value, consider scrapping. See the repair/replace/scrap framework for worked examples.

How much does a Mercedes engine replacement cost?

Costs vary widely. A reconditioned OM651 supply‑only is around £2,800–£4,800, while an OM642 is £3,200–£5,500, and an M276 is £3,800–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. See the replacement cost guide for a full breakdown.

Can I prevent Mercedes engine failures?

Regular maintenance is key. Use the correct oil, change it on time, and follow the manufacturer's service schedule. For specific engines, see the preventative advice on each failure page.
