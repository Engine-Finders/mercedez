M272 Balance Shaft Chain — Symptoms, Cost & Diagnosis

The Mercedes M272 V6 is a refined and widely used engine, but a design flaw in the balance shaft chain can lead to catastrophic failure. This guide explains the symptoms, affected models, repair costs, and whether it’s worth fixing.

M272 balance shaft chain wear is a well‑known weakness. If caught early, a new timing chain and adjuster can save the engine. Left unattended, it destroys the engine and often makes the car a write‑off.

  • Repair cost: £1,200–£2,500 for chain and adjuster; full engine replacement £3,800–£6,500+ [EM-QUOTE]

Symptom Progression — What M272 Owners Notice, In Order

The M272 balance shaft chain does not fail silently. The symptoms develop over time, giving you a crucial window to act before catastrophic engine damage occurs.

  1. Stage 1 – Cold‑start rattle. A brief rattling noise from the front of the engine, lasting 1–2 seconds on first start of the day. This is the balance shaft chain slapping against the timing cover due to a worn tensioner or a stretched chain.
  2. Stage 2 – Rattle extends. The rattle lasts longer and may be heard on warm starts as well. The timing is now shifting, and the engine management light may begin to flicker.
  3. Stage 3 – Check Engine Light (CEL) appears. Fault codes related to camshaft/crankshaft correlation (P0016, P0017, P0021) appear. The engine may feel slightly rough at idle.
  4. Stage 4 – Drivability suffers. Hesitation on acceleration, reduced fuel economy, and a noticeable loss of low‑end torque. The chain is now stretching and the engine is out of time.
  5. Stage 5 – Catastrophic failure. The chain snaps or the tensioner fails completely. The engine may stall, make a loud rattling/knocking noise, and suffer bent valves or damaged pistons. At this point, a full engine replacement is almost always required.

If you hear a cold‑start rattle on your M272, do not delay. A simple diagnostic scan can confirm the condition and give you a repair cost that is still manageable. For a broader look at Mercedes engine issues, see our engine knocking noise guide.

Root Cause — Why M272 Balance Shaft Chains Fail

The M272 is a 60‑degree V6 with a balance shaft located at the front of the engine, driven by a small chain from the crankshaft. The balance shaft chain is held under constant tension by a hydraulic tensioner, which is itself supplied with oil pressure from the engine. Over time, two things happen:

  • Chain stretch. The balance shaft chain stretches, increasing the distance between the crankshaft sprocket and the balance shaft sprocket. This causes the chain to run loose and slap against the timing cover, producing the characteristic cold‑start rattle.
  • Tensioner wear. The hydraulic tensioner loses its ability to maintain proper pressure, allowing the chain to slacken further. This is accelerated by low oil pressure, poor oil quality, or extended oil change intervals.

The design flaw is that the balance shaft chain tensioner is oil‑dependant, meaning it relies on the engine being started to build pressure. That first second of cranking is when the chain is at its slackest and the tensioner is at its weakest — exactly when the damage is done. Early M272 engines (up to mid‑2007) had a soft sprocket on the balance shaft itself, which would wear prematurely and accelerate chain stretch. This was revised in later engines with a hardened sprocket [EM-INTERVIEW].

What Makes M272 Chain Failure More or Less Likely

🛢️

Extended Oil Intervals

Ignoring the 7,500‑mile or 1‑year service interval is the single biggest contributor to tensioner and chain wear. Old oil loses its viscosity and contaminates the tensioner.

❄️

Cold Starts & Short Trips

The tensioner relies on oil pressure. Cars used predominantly for short trips in cold weather suffer the most wear, because the chain slaps more often before the oil reaches pressure.

🔧

Pre‑2007 Build Date

Engines built before mid‑2007 have the soft sprocket. This is the highest‑risk group. Post‑2007 engines are significantly more durable.

How a Specialist Confirms M272 Balance Shaft Chain Wear

1

Diagnostic Scan

Using STAR Diagnosis or a Mercedes‑compatible scanner, a specialist will read fault codes from the engine control unit. Look for camshaft/crankshaft correlation faults (P0016, P0017).

2

Live Data & Timing Deviation

The actual camshaft timing deviation is measured using the scanner. If the deviation exceeds ±3° on either bank, the chain is stretched and should be replaced.

3

Mechanical Inspection

In some cases, the timing cover may be removed to inspect the chain, tensioner and sprocket condition. This is usually only done when the scanner suggests advanced wear.

Prevention & Early Detection — M272 Chain Survival Guide

The M272 is a robust engine, but it demands discipline. These steps will greatly improve your chances of avoiding a catastrophic failure.

  • Change the oil every 7,500 miles or 1 year — never stretch it. Use a high‑quality 5W‑40 or 5W‑30 that meets Mercedes‑Benz 229.5 or 229.51 specification. The tensioner is oil‑dependant; clean, fresh oil is your best defence.
  • Listen for cold‑start rattle. If you hear even a second of rattle on first start, book a diagnostic immediately. Do not wait for the engine management light to appear.
  • Use genuine or OEM parts. Cheap aftermarket chains and tensioners fail quickly. Use INA, Febi, or genuine Mercedes components.
  • Check the engine number. If your engine number starts with 2729.. up to 2729..30 468? (pre‑mid‑2007), you are in the soft‑sprocket group. Consider preventative chain replacement at 70,000 miles if you plan to keep the car.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of a failing M272 balance shaft chain?

The first sign is a brief rattling noise on cold start, lasting 1–2 seconds. If ignored, the rattle extends to warm starts, and eventually the engine management light appears with camshaft/crankshaft correlation fault codes (P0016, P0017).

How much does an M272 balance shaft chain repair cost in the UK in 2025?

For a supply‑and‑fit of the chain, tensioner, and guide rails, expect £1,200–£2,500 at an independent specialist. If the sprockets and adjusters also need replacing, the cost rises to £2,200–£4,000. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £2,500–£6,500.

Is it worth fixing an M272 balance shaft chain on an older Mercedes?

It depends on the vehicle's value and condition. On a W204, GLK, or well‑maintained E‑Class worth over £5,000, the repair is almost always worthwhile. On a W203 or early W211 worth £2,500–£3,500, the repair may exceed 70% of the car's value. In that case, evaluate the car's condition and scrap value before committing. A full engine replacement is rarely cost‑effective on older cars.

Can I drive with an M272 balance shaft chain rattle?

It is not advisable. The rattle indicates the chain is stretching and the timing is shifting. Continued driving risks the chain snapping or the tensioner failing, which will cause severe engine damage and a significantly higher repair bill. If you hear the rattle, arrange a diagnostic immediately.

Is there a recall or extended warranty for M272 balance shaft chains?

There is no general recall, but Mercedes‑Benz did issue a technical bulletin for early M272 engines (up to mid‑2007) regarding the balance shaft sprocket. Some cars may have been covered under goodwill or extended warranty, but this is now expired. Always check with your dealer using your VIN.
