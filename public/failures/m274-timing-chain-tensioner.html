M274 Timing Chain Tensioner Failure - Symptoms, Cost & Diagnosis

Timing chain tensioner failure on the Mercedes M274 2.0L turbo four-cylinder engine is a known issue that can lead to catastrophic engine damage if ignored. This guide covers the symptoms, repair costs for 2025, and whether it's worth fixing.

Timing chain tensioner failure on the M274 2.0L turbo engine is a serious issue that can cause timing chain slack, rough running, and engine destruction. Early diagnosis is critical to avoid a full engine rebuild.

Symptom Progression - What Owners Notice First

Timing chain tensioner failure on the M274 engine rarely happens without warning. The symptoms develop over time, and catching them early can save your engine.

  1. Stage 1 – Cold-start rattle. A brief rattling noise from the front of the engine on first start-up, lasting 1–3 seconds. This is the earliest warning sign of a failing tensioner.
  2. Stage 2 – Rough idle. The engine runs unevenly at idle, with occasional misfires. The check engine light may flash briefly.
  3. Stage 3 – Loss of power. The engine feels sluggish, particularly at low RPM. Acceleration is poor, and the car may hesitate.
  4. Stage 4 – Check Engine Light. Fault codes P0011, P0012, P0021, or P0022 (camshaft position timing) appear. The engine may enter limp mode.
  5. Stage 5 – Timing chain jump. If the tensioner fails completely, the timing chain can skip teeth, causing valves to hit pistons. This leads to catastrophic engine damage.

If you notice any of these signs, do not drive the car. Have it inspected immediately. For a broader look at timing chain issues, see our M271 timing chain guide.

Root Cause - Why M274 Timing Chain Tensioners Fail

The M274 2.0L turbocharged four-cylinder engine uses a timing chain that drives the camshafts. The chain is tensioned by a hydraulic tensioner that relies on engine oil pressure. Over time, the tensioner can wear out or fail due to oil pressure issues, causing the chain to slacken. The most common failure point is the hydraulic tensioner itself, which can lose its ability to maintain tension, especially on cold starts when oil pressure is low. This is particularly common on vehicles with high mileage or those that have suffered from oil starvation. The problem is exacerbated by the chain's location at the front of the engine, making replacement labour-intensive but accessible. The M274 is a high-performance engine, and the timing chain is under significant stress, especially when driven hard [PATTERN DATA].

What Makes Timing Chain Tensioner Failure More or Less Likely

🛢️

Neglected Oil Changes

The hydraulic tensioner relies on clean, high‑quality oil. Extended intervals or the wrong oil can cause the tensioner to fail, leading to chain slack.

🔧

Incorrect Oil Viscosity

Using oil that's too thick or too thin can prevent the tensioner from functioning correctly. Always use Mercedes‑approved oil.

⚠️

Age and Mileage

Failure typically occurs after 60,000–100,000 miles. The chain stretches and the tensioner wears out over time.

🏎️

Hard Driving

Frequent high‑revving and aggressive driving put extra stress on the timing chain, accelerating wear.

Prevention & Early Detection

You can reduce the risk of timing chain tensioner failure and catch issues early.

  • Stick to strict oil change intervals. Use only Mercedes‑approved oil (MB 229.5) and change it every 10,000 miles or annually. Clean oil is critical for the hydraulic tensioner.
  • Use the correct oil viscosity. For the M274, 5W‑40 is typically recommended. Check your owner's manual.
  • Listen for timing chain rattle. A brief rattle on cold start is a warning sign – have it inspected before it causes damage.
  • Avoid short trips. Longer drives allow the engine to reach operating temperature, reducing sludge formation.
  • Address check engine lights promptly. If the camshaft position codes appear, don't delay – it could save your engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of timing chain tensioner failure on an M274 engine?

The earliest signs are a brief rattling noise on cold start, a rough idle, and a check engine light with camshaft position codes.

Can I drive with a faulty timing chain tensioner?

No. If the tensioner fails completely, the timing chain can jump, causing valves to hit pistons. This leads to catastrophic engine damage. Stop driving and have it inspected immediately.

How much does it cost to replace an M274 timing chain tensioner?

At an independent specialist, expect £1,000–£1,800 for the tensioner alone, and £1,800–£2,800 if the timing chain and guides also need replacement. Main dealer prices are significantly higher.

Is it worth replacing the timing chain tensioner on an older M274?

It depends on the vehicle's value. On a car worth over £8,000, the repair is usually worthwhile. On a sub‑£5,000 car, evaluate whether the car is otherwise in good condition.

Can timing chain tensioner failure be prevented?

Regular oil changes with the correct oil are the best prevention. Using Mercedes‑approved oil and changing it every 10,000 miles significantly reduces the risk.

What is the difference between the M274 and M271 engines?

The M274 is a 2.0L turbocharged four‑cylinder engine used from 2013–present. The M271 is a 1.8L supercharged four‑cylinder used from 2000–2011. Both can have timing system issues, but the failure modes differ.
