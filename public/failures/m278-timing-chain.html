M278 Timing Chain Failure — Symptoms, Cost & Repair Verdict

The M278 twin‑turbo V8 is a performance icon, but timing chain stretch is one of its most expensive weaknesses. This guide covers what it sounds like, what it costs to fix in 2026, and whether it's a write‑off.

M278 timing chain stretch is a serious, expensive fault that typically strikes between 70,000 and 120,000 miles. On most modern Mercedes, a repair is still financially sound, but catch it early.

What Owners Notice First — Symptom Progression

The M278 timing chain wears progressively. These are the stages owners consistently report — if you're at stage 2, act quickly.

  1. Stage 1 – Rattling on cold start. A brief, metallic rattle that lasts 1–2 seconds when starting from cold. This is the most important early warning sign.
  2. Stage 2 – Check Engine Light. The engine management light appears, often with stored codes for camshaft position correlation or timing.
  3. Stage 3 – Persistent rattling. The noise lasts longer and may become audible at idle or under light load. Fuel economy may worsen.
  4. Stage 4 – Rough running and misfires. The engine runs unevenly, may misfire, and produces a noticeable loss of power.
  5. Stage 5 – Timing chain failure. If ignored, the chain can snap or jump teeth, leading to catastrophic valve and piston damage. At this point, a full engine rebuild is almost always required.

If you hear that cold‑start rattle, do not ignore it. A diagnostic scan can confirm the issue before it becomes a £5,000+ engine rebuild. See our cold start rattle guide for more context.

Root Cause — Why M278 Timing Chains Fail

The M278 engine uses a complex timing chain system with multiple chains, tensioners, and guides. The primary weakness is the timing chain tensioner, which relies on oil pressure to maintain correct chain tension. Over time, the tensioner's internal seals can degrade, or the oil pressure can drop due to sludge buildup from poor maintenance. When the tensioner fails to maintain proper tension, the chain can stretch or become loose, leading to the characteristic rattle on startup and, eventually, timing errors.

Additionally, the chain itself can stretch over time, particularly if the engine has been subjected to high mileage or frequent high‑load driving. The M278 is a powerful twin‑turbo V8, and the added torque places significant stress on the timing system. Unlike some other engines, the M278 does not have a belt; it uses a chain that is generally durable but not immune to wear. Many specialists report that the tensioner is the primary failure point, and replacing it early can prevent chain damage [EM-INTERVIEW].

What Makes Timing Chain Failure More Likely

🛢️

Missed Oil Changes

Neglecting oil changes is the number one contributor to timing chain wear. Old, degraded oil doesn't provide the pressure or cleanliness the tensioner needs, accelerating wear.

⏱️

High Mileage & Hard Use

Engines used in heavy traffic or with frequent high‑load acceleration see more chain stress. Higher mileage (over 80,000) increases the risk of tensioner failure.

📉

Low Oil Pressure

Oil pressure issues — often caused by a failing oil pump or blocked pickup — can prevent the tensioner from maintaining proper chain tension.

How a Specialist Confirms M278 Timing Chain Failure

1

Diagnostic Scan

Using a Mercedes‑compatible diagnostic tool, a specialist reads the fault codes. Common codes include P0016, P0017, P0021 and P0022 — all relating to crankshaft/camshaft correlation errors.

2

Cold Start Listening Check

A specialist will listen to the engine start from cold. The characteristic metallic rattle is often enough to confirm the issue, but a scope is used to confirm.

3

Camshaft Timing Inspection

In some cases, the engine cover is removed and the camshaft timing is checked visually or using a dial gauge to confirm the chain has stretched.

Prevention & Early Detection

The M278 timing chain is a wear item, but you can significantly delay or even avoid failure with good maintenance.

  • Stick to the service schedule. Use MB‑approved oil (MB‑Approval 229.5 or later) and change it every 10,000 miles or annually, whichever comes first.
  • Listen on startup. A brief rattle when starting from cold is your earliest warning sign. Get it checked immediately.
  • Maintain oil pressure. Keep the oil level between the marks and address any oil leaks or pressure warnings immediately.
  • Use genuine parts. If you do need replacement, use genuine Mercedes or OE‑supplied tensioners and chains. Aftermarket parts have a poor reputation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a failing M278 timing chain sound like?

The classic symptom is a metallic rattle on cold start that lasts 1–2 seconds. It may also rattle briefly when accelerating from low RPM or when the engine is hot and has been left to cool for a short period.

Can I drive with a faulty M278 timing chain?

In the early stages (rattle on cold start only), you can drive the car to a specialist for diagnosis. However, if you are experiencing persistent rattling, rough running or a check engine light, stop driving immediately. Driving with a worn chain can cause the chain to jump or snap, destroying the engine.

How much does it cost to replace an M278 timing chain in the UK in 2026?

For a supply‑and‑fit of a reconditioned chain and tensioner, expect £2,500–£4,000 at an independent specialist. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £5,000–£7,000. If the chain guides and other components also need replacing, add £1,000–£2,000 to the bill.

Is it worth fixing an M278 timing chain on an older Mercedes?

It depends on the vehicle's value. On most S‑Class, CLS and GLE models, the repair is worthwhile. However, on a very low‑value car (sub‑£8,000), a full top‑end rebuild may approach or exceed 70% of the car's value. Use our repair‑vs‑replace calculator to help decide.

Is there a recall for Mercedes M278 timing chains?

Mercedes has issued several technical service bulletins (TSBs) related to M278 timing and chain issues, but there has been no full recall. Some early M278 engines (2010–2012) had timing chain tensioner issues that were covered under warranty extensions in certain markets. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any campaigns apply to your vehicle.

What is the typical mileage for M278 timing chain failure?

Failure typically occurs between 70,000 and 120,000 miles. Engines with poor oil maintenance can fail much earlier, while well‑maintained engines may exceed 120,000 miles without issue.
