OM611 Injector Failure — Symptoms, Cost & Repair Verdict

The OM611 2.2‑litre CDI engine is a workhorse, but injector failure is a common and expensive issue. This guide covers what to look for, what it costs to fix in 2026, and whether it's a write‑off.

OM611 injector failure is a common diesel issue that typically appears between 80,000 and 120,000 miles. Repair is usually cost‑effective, but catching it early is key.

What Owners Notice First — Symptom Progression

OM611 injector failure rarely happens overnight. These are the stages owners consistently report — if you're at stage 2, act quickly.

  1. Stage 1 – Rough idle and black smoke. The engine idles unevenly, and you may notice black smoke from the exhaust, especially under acceleration.
  2. Stage 2 – Loss of power and hesitation. The car feels sluggish, and there's a noticeable delay when you press the accelerator.
  3. Stage 3 – Increased fuel consumption. You're filling up more often, and the trip computer shows worse mpg than usual.
  4. Stage 4 – Check Engine Light. The engine management light appears, often with codes related to injector circuit or fuel quantity.
  5. Stage 5 – Engine misfire and potential damage. If ignored, a stuck-open injector can wash the cylinder walls, leading to bore scoring and catastrophic engine failure.

If you notice rough running or black smoke, do not ignore it. A simple injector leak‑off test can confirm the issue before it becomes a £2,000+ engine rebuild. See our black smoke guide for more context.

Root Cause — Why OM611 Injectors Fail

The OM611 uses a common‑rail fuel injection system with solenoid‑controlled injectors. Each injector contains a precision‑machined nozzle, a solenoid, and a control valve. Over time, several things can go wrong.

The most common failure is wear on the injector nozzle, which can cause poor atomisation, leading to incomplete combustion, black smoke, and rough running. The solenoid can also fail electrically, causing the injector to stick open or closed. Another common issue is contamination from poor‑quality diesel or water in the fuel, which can damage the delicate internal components. Finally, the injector seals can leak, allowing combustion gases to escape and causing a "chuffing" noise and loss of compression [PATTERN DATA].

Unlike some later engines, the OM611 injectors are relatively simple to replace, but precise calibration is required. For a deeper dive into the OM611 platform, see our OM611/OM613 Knowledge Centre.

What Makes Injector Failure More Likely

⛽

Poor Fuel Quality

Low‑quality diesel or contaminated fuel can clog injector nozzles and damage the solenoid, leading to premature failure.

🛢️

Neglected Fuel Filter Changes

A clogged fuel filter restricts flow and can allow debris to reach the injectors, causing wear and blockages.

📉

High Mileage & Age

Injectors are wear items. After 100,000 miles, the internal components are more likely to suffer from wear and tear.

How a Specialist Confirms OM611 Injector Failure

1

Diagnostic Scan

Using a Mercedes‑compatible diagnostic tool, a specialist reads fault codes from the engine ECU. Common codes include P0200 (injector circuit) and P1200 (injector calibration).

2

Leak‑off Test

This measures the amount of fuel returning from each injector. Excessive leak‑off indicates a faulty injector that needs replacement.

3

Injector Testing

In some cases, injectors are removed and tested on a bench to check spray pattern, flow rate, and electrical response.

Prevention & Early Detection

OM611 injectors are durable, but you can significantly extend their life with good maintenance.

  • Use quality diesel. Stick to reputable fuel brands and avoid supermarket fuel if possible, as it may have lower additive content.
  • Change the fuel filter regularly. Every 20,000 miles or as per the service schedule. A clean filter ensures clean fuel reaches the injectors.
  • Use injector cleaner occasionally. A high‑quality diesel injector cleaner added to the fuel tank every 10,000 miles can help keep nozzles clean.
  • Address warning signs early. If you notice rough idle or black smoke, get it checked immediately to prevent more expensive damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of OM611 injector failure?

The earliest signs are rough idle, black smoke from the exhaust, and a noticeable loss of power. You may also experience increased fuel consumption and a check engine light.

Can I drive with a faulty OM611 injector?

In the early stages, you can drive the car to a specialist for diagnosis. However, if the injector is stuck open, it can cause cylinder wash and severe engine damage. If you notice persistent rough running or excessive smoke, stop driving immediately.

How much does it cost to replace an OM611 injector in the UK in 2026?

For a single reconditioned injector supply and fit, expect £200–£400 at an independent specialist. A full set of four typically costs £600–£1,200. Main dealer prices are significantly higher, often £500–£800 per injector.

Is it worth fixing an OM611 injector on an older Mercedes?

It depends on the vehicle's value. On most C‑Class, E‑Class and Sprinter models, a single injector replacement is well worth it. However, on a very low‑value car (sub‑£1,500), a full set replacement may approach or exceed 70% of the car's value. Use our repair‑vs‑replace calculator to help decide.

Is there a recall for Mercedes OM611 injectors?

Mercedes has issued several technical service bulletins (TSBs) related to injector issues on OM611 engines, but there has been no full recall. Some early OM611 engines (1999–2001) had injector wiring harness issues that were covered under warranty extensions in certain markets. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any campaigns apply to your vehicle.

What is the typical mileage for OM611 injector failure?

Failure typically occurs between 80,000 and 120,000 miles. Engines with poor fuel quality or neglected maintenance can fail much earlier, while well‑maintained engines may exceed 150,000 miles without issue.
