Engine Fault Codes

Engine-related codes covering fuel system, sensors, timing, and more.

P0011 — Camshaft Position Timing Over-Advanced

Timing chain or adjuster issue. Common on M272 and M276 engines.

P0016 — Crankshaft/Camshaft Position Correlation

Timing chain stretch or sensor misalignment. See M272 balance shaft chain.

P0087 — Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

Common on OM651 and OM642 diesels. Often a clogged filter or failing sensor.

P0088 — Fuel Rail Pressure Too High

Pressure regulator or fuel return issue. See OM651 balance shaft for related issues.

P0090 — Fuel Pressure Regulator Circuit

Electrical fault in the pressure regulator circuit. Often a wiring issue.

P0128 — Coolant Thermostat

Thermostat stuck open. Common on OM642 and M276 engines.

P0193 — Fuel Rail Pressure Sensor High Input

Failing fuel rail sensor. Can trigger P0087/P0088.

P0217 — Engine Overheat Condition

Cooling system failure. See engine overheating guide.

P0234 — Turbocharger Overboost Condition

Turbo control issue. Common on OM651 and OM642. See turbocharger failure.

P0263 — Cylinder 1 Contribution/Balance

Injector or compression issue. See OM646 injector seal.

P0299 — Turbocharger Underboost

Common on OM651 and OM642. Vacuum leak or turbo actuator failure.

P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

Fuel, spark, or compression issue. See misfire guide.

P0303 — Cylinder 3 Misfire

Specific cylinder misfire. Often injector or coil pack related.

P0562 — System Voltage Low

Battery or alternator issue. See battery warning guide.

P0670 — Glow Plug Module Control Circuit

Common on OM651 and OM642. Faulty glow plug control module.

Emissions & EGR Fault Codes

Codes related to EGR, DPF, AdBlue, and emissions control systems.

P0401 — EGR Flow Insufficient

Clogged EGR valve or cooler. Common on OM651 and OM642. See EGR valve cooler.

P0402 — EGR Flow Excessive

EGR valve stuck open. Often carbon buildup.

P0403 — EGR Control Circuit

Electrical fault in EGR circuit. Check wiring and connectors.

P0420 — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold

Failing catalytic converter. Often triggered by underlying engine issues.

P132B — Turbocharger Boost Pressure Too Low

Related to turbocharger failure or boost leak.

P164B — Glow Plug Module Communication Fault

Common on OM651. Communication error with glow plug module.

P2002 — DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

Clogged diesel particulate filter. See DPF blockage guide.

P204F — Reductant System Performance

AdBlue system issue. Common on OM654.

P20E8 — Reductant Pressure Too Low

AdBlue pump or line issue. See AdBlue/SCR failure.

P244A — DPF Differential Pressure Too Low

DPF sensor or exhaust leak issue.

P244B — DPF Differential Pressure Too High

Clogged DPF or blocked pressure lines.

P2463 — DPF Soot Accumulation

Soot overload. Often requires forced regeneration or cleaning.

P246C — DPF Pressure Sensor Circuit

Electrical fault in DPF pressure sensor circuit.

P0717 — Turbine Speed Sensor No Signal

P0730 — Incorrect Gear Ratio

Internal mechanical fault. Often requires valve body or clutch pack repair.

P0748 — Pressure Control Solenoid Circuit

Electrical fault in transmission solenoid. See 7G-Tronic gearbox faults.

P0750 — Shift Solenoid A

Common solenoid failure. Can cause delayed or harsh shifting.

Similar to P0750. Check transmission fluid and electrical connectors.

P0760 — Shift Solenoid C

Common on older Mercedes transmissions.

P0765 — Shift Solenoid D

Check for stuck solenoid or wiring fault.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find my Mercedes fault code?

You'll need a Mercedes-compatible OBD-II scanner. Plug it into the diagnostic port (usually under the dashboard) and read the codes. Some codes are manufacturer-specific and may require a Mercedes STAR diagnostic tool.

What does "P" mean in a Mercedes fault code?

"P" stands for Powertrain — these codes relate to the engine, transmission, and emissions systems. Other prefixes include "C" (Chassis), "B" (Body) and "U" (Network/Communication).

Can I clear my Mercedes fault code without fixing the problem?

Yes, using a code reader or disconnecting the battery can clear codes. However, the code will return if the underlying fault isn't fixed. Clearing codes also erases freeze-frame data that a specialist could use to diagnose the issue.

What are the most common Mercedes fault codes?

P0087, P0088, P0299, P0401, P2002, and U0100 are among the most common on Mercedes diesels. Each has a dedicated page in this index with detailed repair guidance.

Is it safe to drive with a Mercedes fault code?

It depends on the code. Some codes (like P0562 for low voltage) are safe for short journeys, while others (like P0217 for overheating) require immediate attention. Each code page includes specific drivability guidance.
