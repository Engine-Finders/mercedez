P0087 Mercedes - Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

One of the most common diesel codes on Mercedes OM651 and OM642 engines, and one of the most over-quoted. What it actually means, the causes ranked cheapest-first, and how to tell whether you're facing a £90 sensor or a £1,500 pump.

P0087 means the ECU is seeing lower fuel pressure in the rail than it commanded. On most Mercedes diesel engines this is a fuel supply or sensor problem long before it's a failed high-pressure pump - and it should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common real cause: a clogged fuel filter or a failing rail pressure sensor - typically £90–£380 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: high-pressure fuel pump failure, £1,200–£1,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0088 or P0193 - the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a fuel pump before the filter and sensor have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0087 Actually Means

In a common-rail diesel, the ECU commands a target fuel pressure and monitors the actual pressure via a rail sensor. P0087 is logged when actual pressure falls meaningfully below target for long enough to matter. Crucially, the code describes a measurement, not a diagnosis - the pressure could be genuinely low (a supply restriction or a worn pump), or it could be being reported incorrectly by a failing sensor. That distinction is the entire difference between a £90 fix and a £1,500 one, and it's why the diagnostic order below matters more than the code itself. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM651 2.1 to the OM642 V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration or load
  • Hard starting, or extended cranking before the engine catches - see cranks but won't start
  • Occasional rough idle or misfire when pressure drops at low RPM
  • Black smoke from exhaust if the ECU is over-fuelling to compensate

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check fuel filter history

If it's overdue or unknown, replace it before anything else. This alone resolves a meaningful share of P0087 cases.

2

Live-data the rail sensor

A specialist compares commanded vs actual pressure on live data. A sensor reading implausibly low at idle points to the sensor, not the pump.

3

Test supply pressure

Low-pressure side tested before condemning anything on the high-pressure side.

4

Check for return-flow leak-off

Excessive injector leak-off mimics pump failure. Cheap to test, expensive to misdiagnose.

5

Only then consider the pump

If steps 1–4 are clear and pressure is genuinely low, the HP pump becomes the likely answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes - short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0087 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault the way an oil pressure warning is. But if the vehicle has dropped into limp mode, motorway driving becomes genuinely unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally to merge or overtake. And if low rail pressure is being caused by fuel starvation, prolonged running in that state can shorten injector and pump life - turning a cheap fix into an expensive one. Get it looked at within days, not weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0087 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0088 - Rail Pressure Too High

The mirror image. If both are logged intermittently, suspect the sensor or regulator rather than supply.

P0193 - Rail Sensor High Input

Strong supporting evidence that the sensor itself is at fault.

P0090 - Pressure Regulator Circuit

Points toward the metering valve rather than the pump.

P0299 - Turbocharger Underboost

Often co-occurs where limp mode has been triggered. See turbocharger failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0087 on a Mercedes?

For short journeys to reach a garage, generally yes - but not for normal use, and not on motorways if the vehicle has entered limp mode, because you won't be able to accelerate safely. Prolonged running with genuinely low rail pressure can also damage injectors and the pump.

Does P0087 always mean the fuel pump has failed?

No. Across the five ranked causes, high-pressure pump failure is the least common. A clogged filter or a failing rail pressure sensor are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU sees the pressure discrepancy again, usually within a drive cycle or two. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would have used to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
