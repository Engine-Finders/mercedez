One of the most common fuel system fault codes on Mercedes diesel engines. It rarely means the entire fuel system has failed — and the cheapest fix is often the right one.

P0090 means the ECU is reporting an electrical fault in the fuel pressure regulator circuit — usually on the rail-mounted pressure control valve. On most Mercedes diesels (OM651, OM642, OM646) this is a wiring or solenoid fault long before it's a failed fuel pump.

  • Most common cause: a failed fuel pressure regulator solenoid — typically £200–£450 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: high-pressure fuel pump replacement, £800–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0087 or P0088 — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a fuel pump before the regulator and its wiring have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0090 Actually Means

In a common-rail diesel, the ECU uses a fuel pressure regulator (often called a metering valve or pressure control valve) to maintain the correct fuel pressure in the rail. P0090 is logged when the ECU detects an electrical fault in that regulator circuit — an open circuit, a short, or an implausible resistance reading. Crucially, the code describes a circuit fault, not a fuel pressure fault. The regulator might be fine but its wiring or connector could have failed. That distinction is the entire difference between a £200 fix and a £1,200 one, and it's why the diagnostic order below matters more than the code itself. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM646 to the OM651 and OM642, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration or load
  • Hard starting, or extended cranking before the engine catches — see cranks but won't start
  • Rough idle or misfire when pressure control is erratic
  • Occasional stalling, particularly when coming to a stop

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check the wiring and connector

Inspect the regulator connector and wiring harness for chafing, corrosion or bad pins. This alone resolves a meaningful share of P0090 cases.

2

Measure regulator resistance

A specialist will check the resistance across the regulator solenoid against factory spec. Out-of-spec readings point directly to the regulator.

3

Test the ECU output

With a known-good regulator, or by back-probing the connector, they'll confirm the ECU is sending the correct PWM signal.

4

Check supply pressure

Low-pressure side tested before condemning anything on the high-pressure side.

5

Evaluate the relief valve

A sticking relief valve can cause the regulator to overwork; this is cheaper to test than the HP pump.

6

Only then consider the pump

If steps 1–5 are clear and the regulator still reads bad, the HP pump becomes the likely answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0090 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault the way an oil pressure warning is. But if the vehicle has dropped into limp mode, motorway driving becomes genuinely unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally to merge or overtake. And if the regulator is failing, prolonged running can cause the fuel pressure to oscillate, which can overwork the fuel pump and injectors — turning a cheap fix into an expensive one. Get it looked at within days, not weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0090 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0087 — Rail Pressure Too Low

If both are logged, the regulator may be stuck closed, starving the rail. Focus on the regulator and supply.

P0088 — Rail Pressure Too High

The regulator may be stuck open, over-pressurising the rail. A clear indicator of regulator failure.

P0091 — Regulator Circuit Low

Short circuit. Often a wiring fault rather than the regulator itself.

P0193 — Rail Sensor High Input

Co-occurring with P0090 suggests the ECU is seeing conflicting data; sensor or ECU diagnostics.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0090 on a Mercedes?

For short journeys to reach a garage, generally yes — but not for normal use, and not on motorways if the vehicle has entered limp mode, because you won't be able to accelerate safely. Prolonged running with a faulty regulator can also damage injectors and the pump.

Does P0090 always mean the fuel pump has failed?

No. Across the six ranked causes, high-pressure pump failure is the least common. A wiring fault or a failed regulator solenoid are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the circuit fault again, usually within a drive cycle or two. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would have used to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
