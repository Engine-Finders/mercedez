One of the most common fuel system fault codes on Mercedes diesel engines. It rarely means the entire fuel system has failed - and the cheapest fix is often the right one.

P0263 means the ECU has detected that Cylinder 1 is not contributing equally to engine power - it's running weaker than the other cylinders. On most Mercedes diesels, this is an injector or compression issue, not a catastrophic engine failure. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: a faulty or contaminated fuel injector - typically £300–£600 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: cylinder compression loss (rings, valves, or head gasket), £1,200–£1,800 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and rough idle
  • Frequently appears with P0264, P0265 or P0266 - the cylinder number identifies which cylinder is affected

Do not let anyone sell you a full engine rebuild before the injector and its wiring have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0263 Actually Means

In a common-rail diesel, the ECU monitors the contribution of each cylinder by analysing the crankshaft acceleration during each firing event. P0263 is logged when Cylinder 1 is not providing its expected share of power - it's running weaker than the other cylinders. This could be due to insufficient fuel delivery (injector, fuel pressure, or wiring) or poor compression (rings, valves, head gasket). Crucially, the code describes a mechanical or fuel imbalance, not an electrical fault. That distinction is the entire difference between a £300 injector and a £1,800 head gasket. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM646 to the OM651 and OM642, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Rough idle or noticeable misfire, especially at low RPM
  • Loss of power under acceleration, often accompanied by limp mode
  • Increased fuel consumption and black smoke from exhaust
  • Engine vibration or shaking, particularly when cold

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check injector wiring and connector

Inspect the wiring to Cylinder 1 injector. A loose or corroded connection is a cheap fix and often overlooked.

2

Perform a leak-off test (return flow)

Compare the return flow from each injector. Excessive return flow from Cylinder 1 points to a faulty injector.

3

Swap injectors and test

Move the Cylinder 1 injector to another cylinder. If the fault moves with it, the injector is the problem.

4

Check fuel rail pressure and supply

Ensure the low-pressure and high-pressure fuel systems are delivering adequate pressure to all cylinders.

5

Perform a cylinder compression test

If all fuel-related checks pass, a compression test will confirm whether the issue is mechanical (rings, valves, head gasket).

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes - short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. P0263 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but prolonged driving with a misfiring cylinder can damage the catalytic converter or DPF, and can cause further engine damage if the cause is mechanical. If the vehicle has dropped into limp mode, motorway driving is unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally. Get it looked at within days, not weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0263 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0264 - Cylinder 2 Contribution/Balance

If you have P0263 and P0264 together, it's more likely a fuel supply issue rather than a single injector.

P0265 - Cylinder 3 Contribution/Balance

Multiple cylinder imbalance points toward fuel pressure or ECU issues.

P0201 - Injector Circuit Open (Cylinder 1)

If you have P0201 alongside P0263, the injector wiring or solenoid is likely at fault.

P0087 - Fuel Rail Pressure Too Low

Low rail pressure can cause all cylinders to misfire. See injector failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0263 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. Prolonged driving with a misfiring cylinder can damage the catalytic converter or DPF. Get it checked within a few days.

Does P0263 always mean the injector has failed?

No. While injector failure is the most common cause, wiring issues, fuel supply problems, and even compression loss can also trigger this code.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the imbalance again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
