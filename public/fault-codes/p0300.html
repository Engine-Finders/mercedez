P0300 Mercedes - Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire Detected

One of the most common engine fault codes on Mercedes petrol engines. It means the ECU has detected misfires across multiple cylinders - and it can cause serious damage if ignored.

P0300 means the ECU has detected misfires on multiple cylinders - the engine is not firing properly in more than one cylinder. On most Mercedes petrol engines, this is an ignition or fuel problem, not a catastrophic engine failure. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: faulty ignition coils or spark plugs - typically £150–£400 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: fuel injector failure or internal engine damage, £800–£1,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and rough idle
  • Frequently appears with P0301, P0302, P0303, etc. - the specific cylinder code identifies the affected cylinder

Do not let anyone sell you a full engine rebuild before the ignition coils and spark plugs have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0300 Actually Means

The ECU monitors each cylinder's firing by analysing the crankshaft acceleration during each power stroke. When a cylinder misfires, the crankshaft slows down slightly. P0300 is set when the ECU detects misfires on multiple cylinders (or random misfires that shift between cylinders). Crucially, the code describes a symptom, not a cause - the misfire could be due to ignition (spark plugs, coils, leads), fuel delivery (injectors, fuel pressure), or mechanical issues (compression, valves, timing). That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 coil pack and a £1,500 engine repair. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes petrol engines, from the M270 four-cylinder to the M276 V6 and the M177 V8, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Rough idle or noticeable misfire, especially at idle or under load
  • Loss of power under acceleration, often accompanied by limp mode
  • Increased fuel consumption and black smoke from exhaust
  • Engine vibration or shaking, particularly when cold

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check fault memory for specific cylinder codes

P0300 is a general code; check for P0301–P0308 to identify which cylinders are misfiring.

2

Inspect spark plugs and ignition coils

Remove and inspect plugs for wear. Swap coils between cylinders - if the misfire moves, the coil is faulty.

3

Check for vacuum leaks

Inspect intake hoses, PCV system, and brake booster lines for cracks or disconnections.

4

Test fuel pressure and injector balance

Ensure the fuel pump is delivering adequate pressure, and test injector flow and leak-off.

5

Perform a compression test

If all ignition and fuel checks pass, a compression test will reveal mechanical issues like worn rings or valve problems.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes - short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. P0300 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but prolonged driving with misfires can damage the catalytic converter, overheat the engine, and cause further mechanical wear. If the vehicle has dropped into limp mode, motorway driving is unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally. Get it looked at within a few days.

Related Fault Codes

P0300 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0301 - Cylinder 1 Misfire

Specific cylinder codes help isolate the problem. If only one cylinder is misfiring, focus on that cylinder's components.

P0302 - Cylinder 2 Misfire

Same as above - specific cylinder detection.

P0303 - Cylinder 3 Misfire

Check for patterns - e.g., if all cylinders on one bank misfire, suspect a common issue like a vacuum leak or fuel pressure.

P0171 - System Too Lean (Bank 1)

Often accompanies P0300 when a vacuum leak or fuel delivery issue causes lean misfire. See vacuum leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0300 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. Prolonged driving with misfires can damage the catalytic converter. Get it checked within a few days.

Does P0300 always mean the engine is damaged?

No. In most cases, it's a simple ignition or vacuum issue. Compression problems are the least common cause.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects misfires again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
