P0303 Mercedes - Cylinder 3 Misfire Detected

One of the most common engine fault codes on Mercedes petrol engines. It means the ECU has detected that Cylinder 3 is not firing properly - and it can cause serious damage if ignored.

P0303 means the ECU has detected a misfire on Cylinder 3 - the cylinder is not firing properly. On most Mercedes petrol engines, this is an ignition or fuel problem, not a catastrophic engine failure. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: a faulty ignition coil or spark plug on Cylinder 3 - typically £150–£250 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: fuel injector failure or internal engine damage, £800–£1,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and rough idle
  • Frequently appears with P0300 (random misfire) or other cylinder-specific codes - the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a full engine rebuild before the ignition components have been ruled out. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0303 Actually Means

The ECU monitors each cylinder's firing by analysing the crankshaft acceleration during each power stroke. When a cylinder misfires, the crankshaft slows down slightly. P0303 is set when the ECU detects a misfire specifically on Cylinder 3. Crucially, the code describes a symptom, not a cause - the misfire could be due to ignition (spark plug, coil, lead), fuel delivery (injector, fuel pressure), or mechanical issues (compression, valves, timing). That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 coil pack and a £1,500 engine repair. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes petrol engines, from the M270 four-cylinder to the M276 V6 and the M177 V8, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Rough idle or noticeable misfire, especially at idle or under load
  • Loss of power under acceleration, often accompanied by limp mode
  • Increased fuel consumption and black smoke from exhaust
  • Engine vibration or shaking, particularly when cold

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Swap the ignition coil

Swap the Cylinder 3 coil with another cylinder. If the misfire moves to that cylinder, the coil is faulty.

2

Swap the spark plug

If the coil swap doesn't move the misfire, swap the spark plug. A worn plug is a cheap fix.

3

Check for vacuum leaks

Inspect intake hoses, PCV system, and brake booster lines for cracks or disconnections.

4

Test fuel pressure and injector on Cylinder 3

Ensure the fuel pump is delivering adequate pressure, and test the Cylinder 3 injector for flow and leak-off.

5

Perform a compression test

If all ignition and fuel checks pass, a compression test will reveal mechanical issues like worn rings or valve problems.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes - short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. P0303 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but prolonged driving with a misfiring cylinder can damage the catalytic converter, overheat the engine, and cause further mechanical wear. If the vehicle has dropped into limp mode, motorway driving is unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally. Get it looked at within a few days.

Related Fault Codes

P0303 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0300 - Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

If you have P0300 alongside P0303, the misfire may be shifting between cylinders, which points toward a common cause like a vacuum leak or fuel pressure issue.

P0301 - Cylinder 1 Misfire

If multiple cylinder-specific codes are present, suspect a fuel or mechanical issue rather than an ignition problem.

P0302 - Cylinder 2 Misfire

Check for patterns - if all cylinders on one bank misfire, suspect a common issue like a vacuum leak or fuel pressure.

P0171 - System Too Lean (Bank 1)

Often accompanies P0303 when a vacuum leak or fuel delivery issue causes lean misfire. See vacuum leak.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0303 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. Prolonged driving with a misfire can damage the catalytic converter. Get it checked within a few days.

Does P0303 always mean the engine is damaged?

No. In most cases, it's a simple ignition issue like a faulty coil or spark plug. Compression problems are the least common cause.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects misfires again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
