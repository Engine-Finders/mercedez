P0402 Mercedes - Exhaust Gas Recirculation Flow Excessive

One of the most common diesel engine fault codes on Mercedes. It means the EGR system is flowing too much exhaust gas - which can lead to increased emissions, rough running, and possible limp mode.

P0402 means the ECU has detected that the EGR (Exhaust Gas Recirculation) system is flowing too much exhaust gas into the intake manifold. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a stuck-open EGR valve or a faulty sensor, not a catastrophic failure. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: EGR valve stuck open due to carbon buildup - typically £150–£350 for cleaning or replacement [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: EGR cooler failure or replacement of the entire EGR system, £800–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P0401 (insufficient flow) or P0403 (circuit) - the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a new EGR valve before the differential pressure sensor and the valve operation have been inspected. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0402 Actually Means

The EGR system recirculates a portion of exhaust gas back into the intake manifold to reduce NOx emissions. The ECU monitors the flow using a differential pressure sensor across the EGR valve or a MAF sensor comparison. P0402 is set when the measured flow is significantly higher than the commanded flow for a set period. Crucially, the code describes a flow problem - the valve could be stuck open, the differential pressure sensor could be faulty, or the control circuit could be shorted. That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 cleaning and a £1,200 cooler replacement. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes diesel engines, from the OM646 to the OM651 and the OM642, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Rough idle or hesitation - see rough idle
  • Loss of power and limp mode in some cases
  • Increased fuel consumption and possible black smoke
  • Reduced engine braking or surging at steady throttle

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check fault memory for related codes

Look for P0401, P0403, or sensor codes to narrow the issue.

2

Test the EGR differential pressure sensor

Compare live data readings with actual pressure - a faulty sensor is a cheap fix.

3

Inspect the EGR valve for carbon buildup or sticking

Remove and visually inspect. Often, cleaning with a solvent restores function.

4

Check electrical connections and wiring

Ensure the valve receives proper signal and that wiring is intact - shorts can cause the valve to stay open.

5

Assess EGR cooler blockage

If all else passes, the cooler may be blocked; this is the most expensive fix.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but not for long periods. P0402 on its own is not a critical engine‑destroying fault, but it can cause rough running, increased emissions, and reduced fuel economy. If the vehicle goes into limp mode, motorway driving becomes unsafe. Also, a stuck-open EGR can lead to carbon buildup in the intake manifold over time. Get it looked at within a week to avoid secondary issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0402 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but avoid heavy acceleration. Prolonged driving with an EGR fault can cause rough running and may lead to carbon buildup. Get it checked within a week.

Does P0402 always mean the EGR valve is broken?

No. It often just needs cleaning. Carbon buildup can cause the valve to stick open, and cleaning the valve resolves many cases.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects excessive flow again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
