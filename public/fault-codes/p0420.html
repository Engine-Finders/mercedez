P0420 Mercedes — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold

One of the most common catalyst-related fault codes on Mercedes petrol engines. It means the catalytic converter is not cleaning exhaust gases as efficiently as it should — but it's often caused by a faulty sensor, not a failed catalytic converter.

P0420 means the ECU has detected that the catalytic converter (Bank 1) is not reducing emissions efficiently — usually because the oxygen sensor readings before and after the catalyst are too similar. On most Mercedes, this is a sensor or exhaust leak issue, not a failed catalytic converter. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: a faulty or lazy rear oxygen sensor — typically £150–£250 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: catalytic converter replacement, £800–£1,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light and black smoke
  • Frequently appears with P0430 (Bank 2) or P0300 (misfire) — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a new catalytic converter before the oxygen sensors and the exhaust system have been inspected. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0420 Actually Means

The catalytic converter's job is to convert harmful exhaust gases (HC, CO, NOx) into less harmful substances. The ECU monitors the converter's efficiency by comparing the signals from the upstream (before the cat) and downstream (after the cat) oxygen sensors. When the converter is working, the downstream sensor should show a much slower, flatter signal than the upstream sensor. P0420 is set when the downstream sensor's signal is too similar to the upstream sensor's — indicating the catalyst is not storing oxygen effectively. Crucially, the code describes an inefficiency condition, not a broken converter. The cause could be a faulty downstream sensor, an exhaust leak before or after the cat, a misfire that has contaminated the cat, or indeed a failed cat. That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 sensor and a £1,500 cat replacement. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes petrol engines, from the M270 four-cylinder to the M276 V6 and the M177 V8, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Often no drivability issues — the car may run normally
  • Reduced fuel economy in some cases
  • Possible black smoke from exhaust if mixture is rich
  • May fail an MOT emissions test

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check live data of both oxygen sensors

Compare the waveforms of the front and rear sensors. If the rear sensor mirrors the front, it's likely a sensor issue or exhaust leak.

2

Inspect the exhaust system for leaks

Look for cracks, loose joints, or holes before and after the catalytic converter. Even a tiny leak can cause P0420.

3

Test the rear oxygen sensor

Swap the rear sensor with a known-good one (if available) or test its response time. A slow sensor is a cheap fix.

4

Check for misfire codes or fuel trim issues

If the engine has been running rich or misfiring, the cat may be contaminated. Address the root cause first.

5

Perform a catalytic converter efficiency test

If all sensors and leaks are fine, the cat may be degraded. This is the most expensive repair.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but not indefinitely. P0420 on its own is not a critical engine‑destroying fault, but it will cause the vehicle to fail an emissions test (MOT). Prolonged driving with a failing catalytic converter can lead to further damage and a more expensive repair. If the car is misfiring or running poorly, address that immediately. Otherwise, you can continue driving while you diagnose the cause, but get it checked within a few weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0420 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0430 — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 2)

If both P0420 and P0430 appear, suspect a common issue like an exhaust leak or a fuel system problem affecting both banks.

P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

Misfires can dump unburnt fuel into the catalytic converter, damaging it. Fix the misfire first.

P0171 — System Too Lean (Bank 1)

A lean condition can affect oxygen sensor readings and cause false P0420 codes.

Black smoke from exhaust

Often accompanies catalyst issues. See catalytic converter failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0420 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short to medium journeys, but it will fail an MOT emissions test. Get it checked within a few weeks to avoid further damage.

Does P0420 always mean the catalytic converter is broken?

No. In many cases, it's a faulty oxygen sensor or an exhaust leak. Always diagnose cheapest-first.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU completes the catalyst monitor cycle and detects the inefficiency again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
