P0562 Mercedes — System Voltage Low

One of the most common electrical system fault codes on Mercedes. It means the ECU is detecting low system voltage — and it can cause a cascade of other issues if ignored.

P0562 means the ECU is detecting system voltage below the minimum threshold — typically below 10V while the engine is running. On most Mercedes, this is a battery or charging system issue, not a catastrophic failure. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

What P0562 Actually Means

The ECU monitors the vehicle's system voltage via the battery voltage input. When the engine is running, the alternator should maintain system voltage between approximately 13.5V and 14.5V. P0562 is set when the ECU detects voltage below a critical threshold (typically around 10V) for a set period. Crucially, the code describes a voltage problem, not a specific component failure. The voltage could be genuinely low due to a weak battery, a failing alternator, or a wiring issue. That distinction is the entire difference between a £100 battery and a £800 alternator replacement. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes petrol and diesel engines, from the OM651 diesel to the M276 petrol V6, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Battery warning light on the dashboard (red or amber)
  • Slow cranking when starting the engine
  • Dim lights or flickering dash lights
  • Loss of power or limp mode in some cases

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check battery voltage and condition

With the engine off, measure battery voltage. A healthy battery should read 12.5V or more. If it's below 12V, it needs charging or replacement.

2

Inspect battery terminals and ground connections

Look for corrosion, loose connections, or damaged cables. A simple clean and tighten may fix the issue.

3

Test the alternator output

With the engine running, measure voltage at the battery. It should be 13.5–14.5V. If it's below 13V, the alternator may be faulty.

4

Check the drive belt

Inspect the belt for cracks, glazing, or looseness. A worn belt can slip and reduce alternator output.

5

Consider ECU or wiring

If battery and alternator are both good, the ECU's voltage sensing circuit or wiring may be at fault.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — but you should get it checked soon. P0562 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but if the battery is not being charged, you risk being stranded. If the voltage drops too low, the ECU may shut down non-essential systems and the engine may eventually stall. If the battery warning light is on and the engine is running, get the alternator tested as soon as possible. Short journeys are okay, but avoid long trips until the cause is identified.

Related Fault Codes

P0562 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0560 — System Voltage Malfunction

General voltage code. If both appear, suspect a widespread charging system issue.

P0563 — System Voltage High

The mirror image. If both P0562 and P0563 appear, suspect an intermittent wiring or regulator issue.

P0620 — Generator Control Circuit

Indicates a problem with the alternator control circuit.

Battery warning light

Often accompanies P0562. See alternator failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0562 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but get it checked soon. If the battery is not being charged, you risk being stranded. Avoid long trips until the cause is identified.

Does P0562 always mean the alternator is broken?

No. In many cases, it's a weak battery or a loose connection. Always diagnose cheapest-first.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects low voltage again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
