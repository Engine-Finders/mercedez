P0670 Mercedes — Glow Plug Control Module Circuit

One of the most common diesel engine fault codes on Mercedes. It means the ECU has detected a problem with the glow plug control module — and it can cause cold start issues if ignored.

P0670 means the ECU has detected an electrical fault in the glow plug control module circuit. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a wiring or module issue, not a problem with the glow plugs themselves. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: a faulty glow plug control module — typically £150–£350 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: wiring harness damage or ECU failure, £400–£600 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside glow plug warning light and cold start issues
  • Frequently appears with P0671 through P0676 (specific cylinder glow plug codes) — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a full set of glow plugs before the control module and its wiring have been inspected. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0670 Actually Means

The glow plug system is essential for starting a diesel engine in cold conditions. The glow plug control module (also called the glow plug relay or glow plug controller) supplies power to the glow plugs for a set time before and during starting. P0670 is set when the ECU detects an electrical fault in the control module circuit — typically an open circuit, short circuit, or an implausible current reading. Crucially, the code describes a circuit fault, not a glow plug failure. The module itself could be faulty, the wiring to the module could be damaged, or the ECU's control signal could be missing. That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 module and a £600 wiring repair. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes diesel engines, from the OM646 to the OM651 and the OM642, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Glow plug warning light flashing or staying on
  • Hard starting in cold weather — see cranks but won't start
  • Excessive white smoke from exhaust when starting — see white smoke
  • Rough running after a cold start — see rough idle

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check the glow plug control module fuse

Locate the fuse for the glow plug module (often 40–60A). A blown fuse can cause P0670.

2

Inspect the module wiring and connector

Look for chafing, corrosion, or loose pins. A simple repair can resolve P0670.

3

Test the control module input/output

Check for power supply and ground at the module. A specialist will test the module's output with a multimeter.

4

Check glow plug resistance and current draw

Test each glow plug's resistance (typically 0.5–2.0 ohms). A shorted plug can overload the module.

5

Consider ECU or wiring

If module and glow plugs are good, the ECU's control signal or wiring may be faulty.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but expect difficult cold starts. P0670 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but the glow plugs may not be heating properly. In mild weather, the engine may still start, though with more cranking and white smoke. In freezing conditions, you may be unable to start the car. It's best to get it checked soon, especially before winter.

Related Fault Codes

P0670 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0671 — Cylinder 1 Glow Plug Circuit

If you have P0670 and P0671 together, the module may be faulty or a specific cylinder's circuit is shorting.

P0672 — Cylinder 2 Glow Plug Circuit

Multiple cylinder codes suggest a module or wiring problem, not individual glow plugs.

P0673 — Cylinder 3 Glow Plug Circuit

If all cylinder codes are present, the module is the most likely cause.

Glow plug warning light

Often accompanies P0670. See glow plug system failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0670 on a Mercedes?

Yes, but expect difficult cold starts. In cold weather, the car may not start at all. Get it checked soon, especially before winter.

Does P0670 always mean the glow plug module is broken?

No. It often just needs wiring repair or a new fuse. Always diagnose cheapest-first.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the circuit fault again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
