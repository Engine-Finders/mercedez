P2002 Mercedes — Diesel Particulate Filter Efficiency Below Threshold

One of the most common diesel engine fault codes on Mercedes. It means the ECU has detected that the DPF is not filtering particulates as efficiently as it should — but it's often caused by a sensor issue, not a failed DPF.

P2002 means the ECU has detected that the Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) is not trapping particulates as efficiently as expected — often due to a faulty differential pressure sensor or a failed regeneration cycle. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a sensor or driving pattern issue, not a failed DPF. It should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common cause: a faulty DPF differential pressure sensor — typically £200–£400 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: DPF replacement, £1,200–£2,000 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside DPF warning light and limp mode
  • Frequently appears with P2458 or P2459 — the combination narrows the diagnosis considerably

Do not let anyone sell you a new DPF before the differential pressure sensor and regeneration system have been inspected. Full diagnostic order below.

What P2002 Actually Means

The Diesel Particulate Filter traps soot particles from the exhaust gases. The ECU monitors the filter's efficiency using a differential pressure sensor that measures the pressure drop across the DPF. When the filter is clean, the pressure drop is low. As soot accumulates, the pressure drop increases. P2002 is set when the pressure drop is lower than expected for a given soot load — indicating that the filter is not trapping particles effectively. Crucially, this code describes an efficiency issue, not a clogged filter. The sensor could be faulty, the pressure lines could be blocked, the DPF could be cracked, or the regeneration system could be failing. That distinction is the entire difference between a £200 sensor and a £2,000 DPF replacement. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes diesel engines, from the OM651 to the OM642, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • DPF warning light on the dashboard
  • Reduced power and limp mode in some cases
  • Increased fuel consumption and occasional black smoke from exhaust
  • Failed or interrupted DPF regeneration cycles

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check differential pressure sensor readings

With the engine off, the sensor should read 0kPa. If it reads a value, the sensor or lines are faulty.

2

Inspect pressure sensor lines

Remove and inspect the lines from the DPF to the sensor for blockages or damage.

3

Check DPF regeneration history

Using a diagnostic tool, check the last regeneration time and distance. If it hasn't regenerated recently, a forced regen may resolve the issue.

4

Test DPF integrity (pressure test)

A specialist can perform a pressure test to check for cracks or damage in the DPF.

5

Consider DPF replacement

If the DPF is cracked or damaged beyond cleaning, replacement is the only option.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but not for long periods. P2002 on its own is not a critical engine‑destroying fault, but it can cause the DPF to clog further, leading to limp mode and expensive repairs. If the DPF warning light is flashing, the filter may be dangerously full and require immediate attention. Short journeys are okay, but get it checked within a week to avoid secondary issues.

Related Fault Codes

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P2002 on a Mercedes?

Yes, for short journeys, but get it checked soon. If the DPF warning light is flashing, the filter may be dangerously full. Avoid long trips until the cause is identified.

Does P2002 always mean the DPF is broken?

No. In many cases, it's a faulty differential pressure sensor or blocked sensor lines. Always diagnose cheapest-first.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the efficiency issue again. Clearing it also destroys the freeze-frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one hour of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.
