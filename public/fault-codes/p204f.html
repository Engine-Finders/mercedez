P204F Mercedes - Reductant Injection Valve Circuit Performance

P204F is one of the most common AdBlue/SCR fault codes on Mercedes diesel models. It means the ECU has detected a problem with the reductant (AdBlue) injection system – but that can be caused by a clogged injector, a faulty NOx sensor, a frozen AdBlue line, or a software issue. This page breaks down the causes ranked by cost, what to check first, and whether you should keep driving.

P204F means the ECU is seeing a problem with the AdBlue injection system – typically because the injector is clogged, the NOx sensor is misreading, or the AdBlue has crystallised in the lines. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a maintenance or sensor issue long before it needs an expensive SCR replacement.

  • Most common real cause: a clogged AdBlue injector or crystallised lines – typically £150–£350 for cleaning or replacement [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: a failed NOx sensor or SCR catalyst, £800–£1,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by AdBlue warning lights and limp mode
  • Frequently appears alongside P20E8 (AdBlue pressure too low) or P244A – the combination narrows the diagnosis

What P204F Actually Means

In a Mercedes diesel equipped with an SCR (Selective Catalytic Reduction) system, the ECU monitors the performance of the reductant injection valve – the component that sprays AdBlue (DEF) into the exhaust stream. P204F is logged when the ECU detects that the injection valve is not performing as expected – this could be due to a mechanical blockage, an electrical fault, or a reading from the NOx sensors that doesn't match expected values. The code is a diagnostic flag, not a death sentence – it tells you the system has detected a performance issue, but it doesn't tell you whether the injector is clogged, the sensor is faulty, or the AdBlue has crystallised. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM651 four‑cylinder to the OM642 V6 and the newer OM654, and the diagnostic approach is similar regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • AdBlue warning light or message on the dash
  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber
  • Noticeable loss of power or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may be overfuelling to compensate
  • Occasional rough idle or hesitation when the SCR system attempts to dose
  • In some cases, a "Check AdBlue System" message with a countdown to no-start

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check AdBlue quality and tank level

Low-quality AdBlue or a low tank level can trigger P204F. A simple top-up with correct-spec AdBlue or a tank flush is the cheapest first step.

2

Inspect AdBlue injector and lines for crystallisation

Crystallised AdBlue can block the injector or lines. A specialist can clean the injector and flush the lines – often around £150–£250.

3

Read live data: NOx sensor values and dosing rate

A specialist compares upstream and downstream NOx readings. If the readings are implausible, the sensor is often at fault.

4

Test the dosing control module

If the injector and sensors are fine, the module that controls the dosing may have failed.

5

Only then consider SCR catalyst replacement

If steps 1–4 are clear and the system still reports a performance issue, the catalyst itself may need replacing.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes – but get it diagnosed within days. P204F on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but if the system is unable to dose AdBlue correctly, the vehicle will eventually enter a limited-performance mode and may eventually prevent restarting. Many Mercedes models display a "Check AdBlue System" warning with a countdown (e.g., 500 miles to no-start). Short journeys to a specialist are acceptable, but prolonged driving with P204F – especially with a countdown active – is not recommended.

Related Fault Codes

P204F rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P20E8 - AdBlue Pressure Too Low

Points to a supply issue – a leak, a blocked line, or a failing pump.

P244A - DPF Temperature Sensor Circuit

If the temperature sensor is faulty, the SCR system may not operate correctly.

P203F - Reductant Level Sensor Circuit

Indicates a problem with the AdBlue level sensor – cheap to replace.

P2200 - NOx Sensor Circuit Bank 1

If the NOx sensor has failed, it will trigger both P2200 and P204F.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P204F on a Mercedes diesel?

Short journeys to a garage are generally fine, but if the vehicle displays a countdown warning (e.g., "Check AdBlue System – 500 miles to no-start"), you must get it diagnosed before the countdown expires. Prolonged driving with an active AdBlue fault can also cause NOx sensors to fail prematurely.

Does P204F always mean the SCR catalyst needs replacing?

No – a clogged injector or a faulty NOx sensor is far more common. The SCR catalyst itself rarely fails unless the system has been neglected for a long time.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only if the underlying problem is temporary (e.g., a one-off quality issue). The code will return if the fault persists, and clearing it may destroy freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

How much does an AdBlue injector cleaning cost?

Expect £150–£250 at an independent Mercedes specialist – often much less than a replacement. Many specialists will include a cleaning as part of a diagnostic check.

What is the 70% rule for scrap vs repair?

For Mercedes, the repair‑to‑value threshold is 70% – if the cost of the repair (including diagnostics and parts) exceeds 70% of the car's current market value, scrapping may be the more sensible financial decision. Always get a current valuation before committing to major work.
