P20E8 Mercedes - AdBlue Pressure Too Low

P20E8 is a common AdBlue/SCR fault code on Mercedes diesel models. It means the ECU has detected that the pressure in the AdBlue dosing system is below the expected level – but that can be caused by a frozen line, a clogged filter, a faulty pump, or a simple leak. P20E8 means the ECU is seeing low pressure in the AdBlue dosing system – typically because the pump is failing, the filter is clogged, or the lines are leaking or frozen. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a pump or line issue long before it requires a full SCR replacement.

  • Most common real cause: a clogged AdBlue filter or crystallised lines – typically £100–£250 for cleaning or filter replacement [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: a failed AdBlue dosing pump, £600–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by AdBlue warning lights and limp mode
  • Frequently appears alongside P204F (injector performance) or P203F (level sensor) – the combination narrows the diagnosis

What P20E8 Actually Means

In a Mercedes diesel equipped with an SCR (Selective Catalytic Reduction) system, the AdBlue dosing module includes a pump that pressurises the fluid and delivers it to the injection valve. P20E8 is logged when the ECU detects that the pressure in the system is below a calibrated threshold – this could be due to a weak pump, a blocked filter, a leak, crystallised lines, or even frozen AdBlue in cold weather. The code is a diagnostic flag, not a death sentence – it tells you the system has a pressure issue, but it doesn't tell you whether the pump is worn, the filter is clogged, or the lines are leaking. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM651 four‑cylinder to the OM642 V6 and the newer OM654, and the diagnostic approach is similar regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • AdBlue warning light or message on the dash
  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber
  • Noticeable loss of power or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may be overfuelling to compensate
  • Occasional rough idle or hesitation when the SCR system attempts to dose
  • In some cases, a "Check AdBlue System" message with a countdown to no-start

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check AdBlue level and quality

Low level or poor-quality AdBlue can cause pressure issues. Top up with correct fluid and clear codes – sometimes that's all it takes.

2

Inspect lines and filter for crystallisation/blockage

Crystallised AdBlue can block the filter or lines. A specialist can flush the system and replace the filter – often under £250.

3

Check for leaks and frozen lines

Visual inspection of lines and connections; if freezing is suspected, thaw the vehicle in a warm garage and test again.

4

Test the dosing pump pressure

A specialist can run a pressure test to determine if the pump is delivering the correct pressure.

5

Only then consider pump or sensor replacement

If steps 1–4 are clear and pressure is still low, the pump or pressure sensor may need replacing.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes – but get it diagnosed within days. P20E8 on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but if the system is unable to maintain pressure, the vehicle will eventually enter a limited-performance mode and may eventually prevent restarting. Many Mercedes models display a "Check AdBlue System" warning with a countdown (e.g., 500 miles to no-start). Short journeys to a specialist are acceptable, but prolonged driving with P20E8 – especially with a countdown active – is not recommended.

Related Fault Codes

P20E8 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P204F - Reductant Injection Valve Performance

If you have both, suspect a clogged injector or crystallised lines affecting both pressure and delivery.

P203F - Reductant Level Sensor Circuit

A faulty level sensor can cause the system to misread the tank level, leading to pressure issues.

P2200 - NOx Sensor Circuit Bank 1

A failed NOx sensor can cause the system to over-dose, affecting pressure and catalyst health.

P244A - DPF Temperature Sensor Circuit

Temperature sensor issues can affect SCR operation and regeneration, indirectly causing pressure faults.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P20E8 on a Mercedes diesel?

Short journeys to a garage are generally fine, but if the vehicle displays a countdown warning (e.g., "Check AdBlue System – 500 miles to no-start"), you must get it diagnosed before the countdown expires. Prolonged driving with a low-pressure fault can also cause the pump to run dry and fail.

Does P20E8 always mean the AdBlue pump has failed?

No – a clogged filter or frozen lines are far more common, especially in winter. The pump itself rarely fails unless it has been run dry or the system has been neglected.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only if the underlying problem is temporary (e.g., a one-off freezing event). The code will return if the fault persists, and clearing it may destroy freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

How much does an AdBlue filter replacement cost?

Expect £100–£250 at an independent Mercedes specialist – often much less than a pump replacement. Many specialists will include a filter change as part of a diagnostic check.

What is the 70% rule for scrap vs repair?

For Mercedes, the repair‑to‑value threshold is 70% – if the cost of the repair (including diagnostics and parts) exceeds 70% of the car's current market value, scrapping may be the more sensible financial decision. Always get a current valuation before committing to major work.
