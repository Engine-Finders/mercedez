P244A Mercedes - DPF Temperature Sensor Circuit Range/Performance

P244A is a common DPF temperature sensor fault code on Mercedes diesel models. It means the ECU has detected that the temperature sensor signal is outside the expected range – but that can be caused by a faulty sensor, a wiring issue, or a genuine temperature problem. P244A means the ECU is seeing an implausible signal from the DPF temperature sensor – typically because the sensor has failed, the wiring is damaged, or the sensor is reading a temperature that doesn't match expected values. On most Mercedes diesels, this is a sensor or wiring issue long before it indicates a DPF problem.

What P244A Actually Means

In a Mercedes diesel equipped with a DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter), the ECU monitors exhaust gas temperature using one or more temperature sensors located before and after the DPF. P244A is logged when the signal from the DPF temperature sensor is outside the expected range – this could be due to a faulty sensor, a short or open circuit in the wiring, a poor connection, or a genuine temperature reading that is abnormally high or low. The code is a diagnostic flag, not a death sentence – it tells you the system has a temperature signal problem, but it doesn't tell you whether the sensor is faulty, the wiring is damaged, or the DPF is genuinely overheating. This code appears across the full range of Mercedes common-rail diesels, from the OM651 four‑cylinder to the OM642 V6 and the newer OM654, and the diagnostic approach is similar regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • DPF warning light or message on the dash
  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber
  • Noticeable loss of power or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • DPF regeneration may fail to start or complete because the ECU doesn't have accurate temperature data
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine may be unable to regenerate correctly
  • Occasional rough idle or hesitation when the DPF is attempting to regenerate

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest First

1

Check the sensor connector and wiring

Inspect the connector for corrosion, bent pins, or loose connections. Clean with contact cleaner – often resolves the issue.

2

Read live data: temperature readings

A specialist can compare the DPF temperature sensor reading against other sensors (e.g., EGT sensor) to see if it's reporting plausibly.

3

Check resistance of the sensor

Using a multimeter, test the sensor resistance against manufacturer specifications – often the quickest way to confirm a faulty sensor.

4

Inspect for wiring continuity

Test for continuity from the sensor connector to the ECU – a wiring break can cause the fault.

5

Only then consider DPF inspection

If the sensor and wiring are fine but temperature readings are still abnormal, the DPF itself may be blocked or damaged.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes – but get it diagnosed within days. P244A on its own is not an engine-destroying fault, but if the ECU cannot read the temperature correctly, DPF regeneration may fail. Over time, this can lead to a blocked DPF and expensive repairs. Short journeys to a specialist are acceptable, but if the DPF warning light is also flashing or if the vehicle is in limp mode, prolonged driving is not recommended.

Related Fault Codes

P244A rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P2002 - DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

If you have both, the temperature sensor may be preventing proper regeneration, causing the DPF to clog.

P2463 - DPF Soot Accumulation

A blocked DPF can cause overheating, which may trigger P244A.

P244B - DPF Temperature Sensor Circuit High

Similar to P244A but specifically indicating a high voltage condition.

P204F - SCR System Performance

Temperature sensor issues can affect the SCR system, particularly regeneration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P244A on a Mercedes diesel?

Short journeys to a garage are generally fine, but if the vehicle displays a DPF warning or has entered limp mode, you should get it checked promptly. Prolonged driving with an inaccurate temperature reading can prevent DPF regeneration and cause the filter to block.

Does P244A always mean the DPF temperature sensor has failed?

No – wiring issues, corrosion, or poor connections are also common. A simple connector clean or a £150 sensor replacement often fixes it.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only if the underlying problem is temporary (e.g., a one-off connection glitch). The code will return if the fault persists, and clearing it may destroy freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

How much does a DPF temperature sensor replacement cost?

Expect £100–£250 at an independent Mercedes specialist – parts are inexpensive and labour is typically under 1 hour.

What is the 70% rule for scrap vs repair?

For Mercedes, the repair‑to‑value threshold is 70% – if the cost of the repair (including diagnostics and parts) exceeds 70% of the car's current market value, scrapping may be the more sensible financial decision. Always get a current valuation before committing to major work.
