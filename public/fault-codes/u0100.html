U0100 Mercedes — Lost Communication With Engine Control Module

U0100 is a network communication fault code on Mercedes vehicles. It means the ECU is not receiving data from the engine control module – but that can be caused by a blown fuse, a wiring fault, a dead battery, or a module failure. This page breaks down the causes ranked by cost, what to check first, and whether you should keep driving.

U0100 means the ECU has lost communication with the engine control module (ECM) – typically because of a wiring fault, a blown fuse, a dead battery, or a module failure. On most Mercedes, this is an electrical or network issue long before it requires module replacement.

  • Most common real cause: a blown fuse or wiring fault – typically £50–£200 for diagnosis and repair [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: a failed ECM or CAN bus module, £600–£1,200 fitted and programmed [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by engine management light, non-start, or limp mode
  • Frequently appears alongside U0101 (lost comms with TCM) or P0562 (low voltage) – the combination narrows the diagnosis

Do not assume the ECM has failed – a simple fuse or a £50 wiring repair often fixes it. Full diagnostic order below.

What U0100 Actually Means

In a Mercedes vehicle, the engine control module (ECM) communicates with other modules over the CAN (Controller Area Network) bus – a high-speed network that carries data between components. U0100 is logged when the ECU detects that it cannot communicate with the engine control module. This could be because the ECM has lost power (blown fuse, dead battery), because the wiring between modules is damaged, or because the ECM itself has failed. The code is a diagnostic flag, not a death sentence – it tells you there's a communication problem, but it doesn't tell you why. It's important to note that U0100 is a generic code that appears across all Mercedes models, from the OM651 diesel to the high-performance M157 V8, and the diagnostic approach is consistent regardless of which engine or model is affected.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber or red
  • Vehicle may not start at all, or may crank but not fire
  • Noticeable loss of power or limp mode
  • Dash cluster may show no RPM reading or erratic gauge behaviour
  • In some cases, multiple warning lights (ABS, traction control) may appear together – a sign of CAN bus issues
  • Diagnostic tools may not be able to connect to the ECM

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check fuses and relays for ECM power

A blown fuse or relay is the simplest and cheapest fix. Check the ECM fuse in the main fuse box – often a 5A, 10A, or 15A fuse.

2

Test battery voltage and connections

Low voltage can cause communication faults. Check battery voltage (should be 12.6V+), clean terminals, and ensure earth connections are secure.

3

Inspect CAN bus wiring

Check the CAN bus wires for damage, corrosion, or loose connections. Common problem areas are under the fuse box or near the ECM connector.

4

Check for other CAN bus modules

A faulty module on the CAN bus can take down the entire network. Disconnect modules one by one to isolate the fault.

5

Only then consider ECM replacement

If steps 1–4 are clear and communication is still lost, the ECM itself may have failed.

Can You Still Drive It?

No – if the vehicle starts at all, get it towed to a specialist. U0100 indicates a critical communication failure. If the ECM is not communicating, the engine management system cannot control engine functions properly. Driving with U0100 can be dangerous – the vehicle may enter limp mode, stall unexpectedly, or lose power at speed. If the vehicle starts and runs, it may be safe to drive slowly to a nearby specialist (under 5 miles). However, if the vehicle is in limp mode or showing other warning lights, do not drive it – call for recovery.

Related Fault Codes

U0100 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

U0101 — Lost Communication With TCM

If you have both, suspect a CAN bus network fault rather than a single module failure.

P0562 — System Voltage Low

Low battery voltage is a common cause of U0100 – a £0 fix if it's just a loose connection.

P0600 — Serial Communication Link

Indicates a broader communication fault, often linked to U0100.

P0606 — ECM Processor Fault

If you have both, the ECM itself may be faulty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with U0100 on a Mercedes?

No – this is a critical communication failure. If the vehicle starts, it may be safe to drive very slowly to a nearby garage (under 5 miles), but if it's in limp mode or showing multiple warning lights, call for recovery.

Does U0100 always mean the ECM has failed?

No – a blown fuse, low battery, or wiring fault is far more common. The ECM itself is the least likely cause.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only if the underlying problem is temporary (e.g., a one-off voltage drop). The code will return if the fault persists, and clearing it may destroy freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

How much does ECM diagnosis cost?

Expect 1-2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent Mercedes specialist – typically £100–£200. Many specialists will offset this against the repair if you proceed with them.

What is the 70% rule for scrap vs repair?

For Mercedes, the repair‑to‑value threshold is 70% – if the cost of the repair (including diagnostics and parts) exceeds 70% of the car's current market value, scrapping may be the more sensible financial decision. Always get a current valuation before committing to major work.
