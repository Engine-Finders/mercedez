How to Use This Index

Whether you're replacing a failed engine, upgrading, or confirming what's in your car, this index helps you narrow it down in three steps.

  1. Find your engine code.

    Check the label on the timing cover or cylinder head. For M-class petrol, look for M272 or M276. For OM-series diesel, OM651 or OM642. If you're unsure, use our Mercedes VIN decoder — the 8th character of your VIN often tells you the engine family.

  2. Match engine to chassis.

    Look up your engine code in the table above. The chassis codes column tells you which models and generations it was factory-fitted to. For example, OM651 appears in W204, W205, W212, W213, X253 — that's C-Class, E-Class, and GLC across multiple generations.

  3. Check compatibility and cost.

    Once you know the engine and chassis, check the fitment matrix for detailed variants (e.g., OM651 DE22 LA vs. OM651 DE22 LA EVO). Then see the replacement cost guide to understand what a reconditioned unit will cost you.

If you need a visual guide, the engine number location page shows where to find the code on the engine block.

How to Identify Your Mercedes Engine Code

Getting the engine code right is the single most important step before ordering a replacement or checking compatibility. Here's how to find it.

  • On the engine block.

    The engine code is stamped on a metal plate or machined surface on the cylinder block. For OM651, it's on the left side of the block near the oil filter housing. For M276, it's on the front of the block behind the intake manifold. The engine number location page has diagrams for every family.

  • On the timing cover label.

    Most Mercedes engines have a sticker on the timing cover or camshaft cover with the engine code and other details. This is the easiest place to check without lifting the car.

  • Using your VIN.

    The 8th character of your VIN often indicates the engine family. For example, a VIN with the 8th character "9" usually means an OM651 diesel. Use our Mercedes VIN decoder for a full breakdown.

  • Check the vehicle registration document.

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number. Cross-reference that with the engine code list in our Engine Code Guide.

If you're still unsure, contact a vetted Mercedes specialist — they can confirm the engine code from the vehicle's service history or on a lift.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a used Mercedes engine will fit my car?

Check the engine code against the chassis code in the index above. For example, an OM651 from a W204 C-Class will also fit a W212 E-Class or X253 GLC, but you must confirm the variant (DE22 LA vs. DE22 LA EVO) and ECU compatibility. Always consult a specialist before buying.

What is the most reliable Mercedes diesel engine?

Based on our data, the OM654 is significantly more reliable than the OM651. The OM642 V6 is also robust, but swirl flap and oil cooler seal failures are common at higher mileage. See the OM654 Knowledge Centre for the full reliability verdict.

Can I fit a petrol engine into a diesel Mercedes chassis?

Theoretically yes, but practically no. The engine mounts, ECU, wiring loom, fuel system, and exhaust are all different. It is almost always cheaper and simpler to replace the engine with the same type. The fitment matrix shows factory-fit options only.

How much does a replacement Mercedes engine cost?

Supply-only costs vary widely. An OM651 reconditioned unit is around £2,800–£4,800 [EM-QUOTE]. An OM642 V6 diesel is £3,200–£5,500. An M276 petrol V6 is £3,800–£6,500. For a full breakdown, see replacement cost by engine code.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for Mercedes?

If the cost of a replacement engine (supply + fitting) exceeds approximately 70% of your car's current market value, it may be more economical to scrap the car and buy a replacement. This threshold is based on our proprietary data and is a genuine guideline, not a hard rule. See the repair/replace/scrap framework for worked examples.
