The Short Answer

The M272 and M276 are both Mercedes V6 petrol engines, but they are not interchangeable. The M272 is a 3.5‑litre unit (2004–2011) with a known balance‑shaft issue, while the M276 is a 3.0‑litre unit (2011+) with a different timing chain design and improved reliability. To identify which you have, check the engine code label on the timing cover or decode the 8th character of your VIN – for M272 it's typically "M" (for early models) and for M276 it's "276" in the engine number.

  • M272 fitment: C‑Class W204/W203, E‑Class W211, ML W164, GLK X204, CLS C219 (2004–2011).
  • M276 fitment: E‑Class W212/W213, CLS C218, GLE W166, GLS X166, S‑Class W221 (2011+).

How to Identify Which Engine Is in Your Mercedes

There are three reliable methods to confirm whether you have the M272 or M276. Use the one that suits your situation.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the sticker on the timing cover or camshaft cover. For an M272, the code will be clearly marked as M272 (e.g., M272 E35). For the M276, you'll see M276 (e.g., M276 DE30). This is the most direct method.

  2. Use the VIN decoder.

    The 8th character of your VIN can indicate the engine family. For M272, it's often a letter like "M" (early) or "9" (later). For M276, the engine number itself contains "276". Use our free Mercedes VIN decoder for a full breakdown.

  3. Check the registration document.

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which typically starts with the engine code (e.g., 272... or 276...). Cross‑reference with our Engine Code Guide.

If you cannot locate the code, a vetted Mercedes specialist can identify it during a service.

Why Correct Identification Matters

Installing the wrong engine is one of the most expensive mistakes you can make. Here's why getting the code right is critical:

  • ECU compatibility: Each engine has a dedicated engine control unit with specific fuel mapping, cam timing, and emission parameters. An M272 ECU cannot run an M276.
  • Physical fitment: Engine mounts, exhaust manifolds, cooling hoses, and wiring looms differ significantly. Even the bellhousing pattern may vary.
  • Legal compliance: Fitting an engine not homologated for your chassis can cause MOT failure, insurance invalidation, or DVLA issues.
  • Warranty & specialist support: Most vetted specialists will refuse to work on a mismatched combination. Always check the fitment matrix and consult a specialist before purchasing.

Common Misidentifications – What They Are Not

The M272 and M276 are often confused with other Mercedes engines. Avoid these pitfalls:

  • M272 vs. M276: Both are V6 petrols, but the M272 is 3.5‑litre and the M276 is 3.0‑litre. They have different timing chain designs – the M272 has a balance‑shaft that can wear, while the M276 uses a revised camshaft adjuster. Check the engine code label.
  • M276 vs. M278: M278 is a 4.7‑litre V8 biturbo petrol, not a V6. They are completely different families – M276 is the V6, M278 is the V8. Do not confuse them.
  • M272 vs. M273: M273 is a 5.5‑litre V8 petrol. While they share the V8/V6 architecture, they are not interchangeable. M273 appears in S‑Class and ML models, but the fitment is different.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if my Mercedes has an M272 or M276 without lifting the bonnet?

The easiest way is to check the 8th character of your VIN. Use our VIN decoder to get a full breakdown. You can also look at the V5C logbook – the engine number will start with "272" or "276".

Which engine is more reliable – M272 or M276?

The M276 is generally considered more reliable. The M272 has a well‑known balance‑shaft issue (timing chain sprocket wear) that can be catastrophic. The M276 revised the design and is less prone to this failure. However, the M276 can suffer from camshaft adjuster wear and oil leaks. See the M272 Knowledge Centre and M276 Knowledge Centre for full reliability verdicts.

Can I fit an M276 engine into a car that originally had an M272?

No – the engine mounts, ECU, wiring, and ancillaries are different. It is not a practical DIY swap and would cost more than simply replacing with the correct engine. Always replace like‑for‑like.

How much does it cost to replace an M272 or M276 engine?

Supply‑only costs are similar: a reconditioned M272 is around £3,800–£6,500, and an M276 is also £3,800–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. Labour for R&R is typically 12–16 hours, adding £780–£1,040 at independent rates. See the replacement cost guide for full details.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for these engines?

If the cost of a replacement engine (supply + fitting) exceeds approximately 70% of your car's current market value, it may be more economical to scrap the car. For an older E‑Class W211 with an M272, the threshold is often reached; for a newer W213 with an M276, replacement is usually viable. See the repair/replace/scrap framework for worked examples.
