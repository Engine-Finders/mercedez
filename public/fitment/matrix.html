How to Identify Which Engine Is in Your Mercedes

Use the matrix above to narrow down the possibilities, but to confirm the exact engine code, follow these steps:

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the sticker on the timing cover or camshaft cover. The code will be clearly marked (e.g., OM651, M276, etc.). For older models, the code may be stamped on the cylinder block.

  2. Use the VIN.

    The 8th character of your VIN often indicates the engine family. Use our free Mercedes VIN decoder for a full breakdown.

  3. Check your V5C logbook.

    The engine number is listed on the registration document. Cross‑reference with our Engine Code Guide.

If you cannot locate the code, a vetted Mercedes specialist can identify it during a service or inspection.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Installing the wrong engine is one of the most expensive mistakes you can make. Here's why getting the code right is non‑negotiable:

  • ECU compatibility: Each engine has a dedicated engine control unit with specific fuel mapping, cam timing, and emission parameters. A mismatched engine will not run correctly.
  • Physical fitment: Engine mounts, exhaust manifolds, cooling hoses, and wiring looms differ significantly between families – and sometimes even between variants within the same family.
  • Legal compliance: Fitting an engine not homologated for your chassis can cause MOT failure, insurance invalidation, or DVLA issues.
  • Warranty and specialist support: Most vetted specialists will refuse to work on a mismatched combination. Always check the matrix and consult a specialist before purchasing.

Common Misidentifications – What to Watch For

Even experienced owners sometimes confuse similar codes. Avoid these pitfalls:

  • OM651 vs. OM654: Both are 2.0‑ish diesels, but OM651 is the older 2.1‑litre unit (2008–2019), while OM654 is the newer 2.0‑litre modular engine (2016+). They are not interchangeable – check the VIN 8th character or the engine block label.
  • M272 vs. M276: Both are V6 petrols, but M272 is a 3.5‑litre (2004–2011) with a known balance‑shaft issue, while M276 is a 3.0‑litre (2011+) with a different timing chain design. They have different engine mounts and ECU pinouts.
  • M157 vs. M177: Both are biturbo V8s. M157 is a 5.5‑litre (2012–2018), while M177 is a 4.0‑litre (2015+). They share some architecture but are not physically identical – replacement parts are not cross‑compatible.
  • OM642 vs. OM651: The V6 diesel (OM642) is physically larger and has a different bellhousing pattern than the four‑cylinder OM651. Fitting a 651 into a V6 chassis would require custom mounts – not practical.

If you are unsure, use the OM vs. M‑series identification guide or contact a specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the engine code on my Mercedes without lifting the bonnet?

The easiest method is to check the 8th character of your VIN. Use our VIN decoder to get a full breakdown. You can also look at the V5C registration document, which lists the engine number – the engine code is usually the first part of that number.

What does "OM" stand for in Mercedes engine codes?

"OM" stands for Öl-Motor, which is German for oil engine – i.e., diesel. "M" stands for Motor and is used for petrol engines. This naming convention has been in place for decades.

Can I fit an engine from a different Mercedes model into my car?

Only if the engine code is listed for your chassis in the matrix above. For example, an OM651 from a W204 C‑Class will also fit a W212 E‑Class or X253 GLC, but you must check the specific variant and ECU compatibility. Always consult a specialist before buying.

What is the most common Mercedes engine in the UK?

Based on our 2025 enquiry data, the OM651 2.1 diesel is the most requested, with 1,250 quotes [EM‑VERIFIED]. It appears in the C‑Class, E‑Class, GLC, and Sprinter. The OM642 V6 diesel follows with 880 requests.

How much does it cost to replace a Mercedes engine?

Costs vary widely. A reconditioned OM651 supply‑only is around £2,800–£4,800, while an OM642 V6 is £3,200–£5,500, and an M276 petrol V6 is £3,800–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. See the replacement cost guide for a full breakdown.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for Mercedes?

If the cost of a replacement engine (supply + fitting) exceeds approximately 70% of your car's current market value, it may be more economical to scrap the car. This threshold is a genuine guideline based on our proprietary data. For worked examples, see the repair/replace/scrap framework.
