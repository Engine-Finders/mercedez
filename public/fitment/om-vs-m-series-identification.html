The Short Answer

Mercedes engine codes starting with OM are diesel engines; those starting with M are petrol. OM stands for Öl-Motor (oil engine) and M for Motor. The most common OM diesel is the OM651 (2.1‑litre, 1,250 enquiries in 2025 [EM‑VERIFIED]), while the most common M petrol is the M276 (3.0‑litre V6, 390 enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED]).

  • OM engines: OM651, OM642, OM654, OM646, OM656 – fitted to C‑Class, E‑Class, GLC, Sprinter, etc.
  • M engines: M276, M274, M272, M270, M177, M157 – fitted to C‑Class, E‑Class, CLS, GLE, AMG models.
  • Identification: Check the engine code label on the timing cover, decode your VIN (8th character), or look at the V5C logbook.
  • Replacement cost (supply‑only): OM651 ~£2,800–£4,800; OM642 ~£3,200–£5,500; M276 ~£3,800–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE].

How to Identify Which Engine Is in Your Mercedes

Use these three methods to confirm whether you have an OM diesel or an M petrol engine.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the sticker on the timing cover or camshaft cover. The code will start with OM or M – for example, OM651 or M276. This is the most direct method.

  2. Use the VIN (8th character).

    The 8th character of your VIN often indicates the engine family. Use our free Mercedes VIN decoder for a full breakdown, including the exact engine code.

  3. Check your V5C logbook.

    The engine number is listed on the registration document; the code is usually the first part of that number. Cross‑reference with our Engine Code Guide.

If you cannot locate the code, a vetted Mercedes specialist can identify it during a service.

Why Correct Identification Matters

Installing the wrong engine type (OM diesel in an M petrol chassis or vice versa) is one of the most expensive mistakes you can make. Here's why getting the fuel type right is critical:

  • ECU compatibility: Diesel and petrol ECUs have completely different fuel mapping, injection timing, and emission controls. An OM diesel ECU cannot run an M petrol engine.
  • Physical fitment: Engine mounts, exhaust systems, cooling hoses, and wiring looms differ significantly between diesel and petrol families. Even within OM or M, there are variations.
  • Legal compliance: Fitting an engine not homologated for your chassis can cause MOT failure, insurance invalidation, or DVLA issues.
  • Warranty and specialist support: Most vetted specialists will refuse to work on a mismatched combination. Always check the matrix and consult a specialist before purchasing.

Common Misidentifications – OM vs M Pitfalls

Even experienced owners sometimes confuse diesel and petrol codes. Avoid these common mistakes:

  • OM651 vs. M274: Both are 2.0‑ish four‑cylinder engines, but OM651 is a diesel and M274 is a petrol. They look similar from the top but have different fuel rails, injectors, and compression ratios. Check the label – if it starts with OM, it's diesel.
  • OM642 vs. M276: Both are V6 engines, but OM642 is a 3.0‑litre diesel and M276 is a 3.0‑litre petrol. The diesel has a high‑pressure fuel pump and common‑rail injectors; the petrol has spark plugs and ignition coils. They are not interchangeable – the engine mounts and bellhousing may also differ.
  • M157 vs. OM642: M157 is a 5.5‑litre V8 petrol biturbo, while OM642 is a 3.0‑litre V6 diesel. They are completely different families – never confuse them.
  • OM654 vs. OM651: Both are diesel, but OM654 is the newer 2.0‑litre modular engine (2016+) while OM651 is the older 2.1‑litre unit (2008–2019). They are not interchangeable due to different mounting points and ECU protocols.

If you are still unsure, use the full fitment matrix or contact a specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if my Mercedes engine is diesel (OM) or petrol (M) without opening the bonnet?

The easiest way is to check the 8th character of your VIN. Use our VIN decoder to get a full breakdown. You can also look at the fuel filler cap – diesel will have a "Diesel" label, petrol will be "Super" or "Premium". But for the exact engine code, the VIN or logbook is most reliable.

What does "OM" stand for in Mercedes engine codes?

"OM" stands for Öl-Motor, which is German for oil engine – i.e., diesel. "M" stands for Motor and is used for petrol engines. This naming convention has been in place for decades.

Can I fit a petrol M engine into a car that originally had a diesel OM engine?

No – the engine mounts, ECU, wiring, fuel system, and exhaust are all different. It is not a practical DIY swap and would cost more than simply replacing with the correct engine. Always replace like‑for‑like.

Which is more reliable – OM diesel or M petrol?

It depends on the specific engine. Generally, the newer OM654 diesel is considered more reliable than the older OM651. Among petrol, the M276 is more reliable than the M272 (which has a balance‑shaft issue). For detailed verdicts, see each engine's Knowledge Centre.

How much does it cost to replace an OM or M engine?

Supply‑only costs vary. A reconditioned OM651 is around £2,800–£4,800, an OM642 is £3,200–£5,500, and an M276 is £3,800–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. Labour for R&R is typically 12–16 hours, adding £780–£1,040 at independent rates. See the replacement cost guide for full details.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for these engines?

If the cost of a replacement engine (supply + fitting) exceeds approximately 70% of your car's current market value, it may be more economical to scrap the car. For an older OM651 in a W204 C‑Class, the threshold is often reached; for a newer M276 in a W213 E‑Class, replacement is usually viable. See the repair/replace/scrap framework for worked examples.
