The Short Answer

The OM642 and OM651 are both Mercedes diesel engines, but they are fundamentally different – the OM642 is a 3.0‑litre V6, while the OM651 is a 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder. They are not interchangeable due to different engine mounts, ECU programming, and physical dimensions. In 2025, the OM651 generated 1,250 quote requests, and the OM642 generated 880 [EM‑VERIFIED].

How to Identify Which Engine Is in Your Mercedes

Use these three reliable methods to confirm whether you have an OM642 or OM651.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the sticker on the timing cover or camshaft cover. For an OM642, you'll see OM642 (e.g., OM642 DE30 LA). For the OM651, it will be OM651 (e.g., OM651 DE22 LA). This is the most direct method.

  2. Use the VIN decoder.

    The 8th character of your VIN often indicates the engine family. For OM651, it's commonly a "2" or "9" (depending on variant); for OM642, it may be "6" or "7". Use our free Mercedes VIN decoder for a full breakdown, including the exact engine code.

  3. Check your V5C logbook.

    The registration document lists the engine number, which typically starts with the engine code (e.g., 642... or 651...). Cross‑reference with our Engine Code Guide.

If you cannot locate the code, a vetted Mercedes specialist can identify it during a service.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Installing the wrong engine is one of the most expensive mistakes you can make. Here's why getting the code right is critical:

  • ECU compatibility: The OM642 and OM651 have different fuel injection systems, turbocharger configurations, and emission controls. Their ECUs are not interchangeable – fitting an OM651 into an OM642 chassis will result in a non‑start or severe running issues.
  • Physical fitment: Engine mounts, exhaust manifolds, cooling pipes, and wiring looms differ significantly. The OM642 is a V6 and is wider, requiring different mounting points than the four‑cylinder OM651.
  • Legal compliance: Fitting an engine not homologated for your chassis can cause MOT failure, insurance invalidation, or DVLA issues.
  • Warranty and specialist support: Most vetted specialists will refuse to work on a mismatched combination. Always check the fitment matrix and consult a specialist before purchasing.

Common Misidentifications – OM642 vs OM651 Pitfalls

Even experienced owners sometimes confuse these two engines. Avoid these common mistakes:

  • OM642 vs. OM651: Both are diesel, but one is a V6 and the other is a four‑cylinder. The OM642 has six injectors and is physically larger; the OM651 has four injectors and is more compact. Check the engine code label – if it starts with "642" it's the V6; "651" is the four‑cylinder.
  • OM642 vs. M276: M276 is a 3.0‑litre V6 petrol, not diesel. The fuel type is different – the M276 has spark plugs and ignition coils; the OM642 has glow plugs and a high‑pressure fuel pump. They are not interchangeable.
  • OM651 vs. OM654: OM654 is the newer 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel (2016+), while OM651 is the older 2.1‑litre unit (2008–2019). They are not interchangeable due to different mounting points and ECU protocols. Check the label – OM654 is clearly marked.
  • OM642 variants: Within the OM642 family, there are different power outputs (e.g., 190 PS, 224 PS, 258 PS). While the block is the same, the turbocharger and injectors differ. Always verify the full engine number to ensure correct replacement parts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if my Mercedes has an OM642 or OM651 without opening the bonnet?

The easiest way is to check the 8th character of your VIN. Use our VIN decoder to get a full breakdown. You can also look at the V5C logbook – the engine number will start with "642" or "651".

Which engine is more reliable – OM642 or OM651?

The OM651 is generally more reliable in terms of oil cooler seal issues (the OM642 is notorious for oil cooler seal leaks in the V of the engine). However, the OM651 can suffer from timing chain tensioner wear and injector seal failure (Black Death). Both have their weaknesses. For detailed reliability verdicts, see the OM642 Knowledge Centre and OM651 Knowledge Centre.

Can I fit an OM651 engine into a car that originally had an OM642?

No – the engine mounts, ECU, wiring, exhaust, and cooling systems are all different. It is not a practical DIY swap and would cost more than simply replacing with the correct engine. Always replace like‑for‑like.

What is the typical replacement cost for an OM642 vs OM651?

Supply‑only costs: OM642 reconditioned is around £3,200–£5,500, while OM651 is £2,800–£4,800 [EM‑QUOTE]. Labour for R&R is typically 10–14 hours for both, adding £650–£910 at independent rates. See the replacement cost guide for full details.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for these engines?

If the cost of a replacement engine (supply + fitting) exceeds approximately 70% of your car's current market value, it may be more economical to scrap the car. For an older OM642 in a W211 E‑Class, the threshold is often reached; for a newer OM651 in a W205 C‑Class, replacement is usually viable. See the repair/replace/scrap framework for worked examples.

How do I know which OM642 variant I have (e.g., 190 PS vs 258 PS)?

Check the engine number on the block or the VIN decoder. The power output is often indicated by the suffix in the engine code (e.g., OM642 DE30 LA with different calibration codes). Your specialist can also read the ECU data to confirm the exact variant.
