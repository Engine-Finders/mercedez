How to check whether your Mercedes has been recalled

A recall attaches to a specific vehicle, not to a model as a whole - two identical-looking C-Class cars can have different recall status. That's why the check always starts with your VIN, not your badge.

  1. Find your VIN and engine code. The VIN is stamped on the vehicle and printed in the logbook; the engine code confirms which unit is actually fitted - important because the same badge can span different engines. Our engine code guide and VIN decoder both help here.
  2. Check the official channels. The GOV.UK vehicle recall lookup covers UK-registered vehicles [THIRD-PARTY], and any Mercedes franchised dealer will check your VIN against the manufacturer's recall register free of charge.
  3. Cross-reference your model page. The matching guide in the index above shows which campaigns affect your model and what the remedy involves, so you know what to expect before you book.
  4. Book the work. Recall repairs are completed at no cost to the owner, regardless of the car's age, mileage or how many times it has changed hands.

Bought your Mercedes used? Check anyway - recall entitlement follows the vehicle, not the keeper, and there is no time limit on completing an open recall.

What Mercedes recalls do - and don't - cover

Owners regularly ask us whether a known fault on their engine "counts as" a recall. In most cases it doesn't, and confusing the three mechanisms leads to the wrong expectation at the dealer counter.

  • A recall is a safety-related defect formally notified to the authorities. The manufacturer contacts registered keepers and fixes the car free of charge.
  • A technical service bulletin (TSB) is internal guidance to workshops about a known issue - it informs repairs but creates no obligation to fix cars free, and many owners never hear about them.
  • A known fault is a pattern documented from real-world failure experience but never escalated to a campaign at all.

That distinction matters because the Mercedes engine problems UK owners most often search for - injector seal leaks, timing chain wear, oil cooler seepage - are overwhelmingly TSB and known-fault territory rather than recalled items. Our failure database covers that ground in full, engine by engine, and the E-Class and C-Class recall pages note where a known fault and an official campaign overlap.

If a fault on your car isn't a recall, the question becomes whether repair is still worth it - which is exactly what our repair, replace or scrap framework answers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if my Mercedes has been recalled?

Use your VIN, not your badge. The GOV.UK recall lookup covers UK-registered vehicles [THIRD-PARTY], and any Mercedes dealer will check your VIN against the manufacturer register free of charge. Then cross-reference your model page in the index above for context on what the remedy involves.

Do Mercedes recalls cost anything?

No. Recall work is completed free of charge, whatever the vehicle's age or mileage, and whoever owns it at the time. If you're quoted for a repair that's described as a recall, question it - genuine recall remedies are always at the manufacturer's expense.

I bought my Mercedes used - do recalls still apply?

Yes. Recall entitlement follows the vehicle, not the keeper, and there is no expiry date on an open recall. Check your VIN as soon as you take ownership; previous keepers may never have had the work done.

Is a recall the same as a TSB or a known fault?

No. A recall is a formal safety campaign with a legal notification duty. A TSB is internal workshop guidance, and a known fault is a documented failure pattern with no campaign behind it at all. Most widely discussed Mercedes engine issues sit in the TSB or known-fault categories - our failure pages cover those separately.

Why are some model pages shorter than others?

Because we only publish recall entries we can source and date. A short page means few confirmed campaigns for that model - not incomplete research. Anything we can't verify is held back rather than guessed.

How often is this index updated?

As new campaigns are confirmed and dated, the relevant model page is updated and the change logged on our public corrections record rather than edited in silently.
