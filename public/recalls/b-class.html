How to Check Your B Class VIN for Outstanding Recalls

A Vehicle Identification Number check is the only definitive way to confirm whether your specific car has an open recall. The B-Class VIN is a 17-character code found in three locations: stamped on the vehicle identification plate at the base of the windscreen on the driver's side, printed on the door shut label (driver's side B-pillar), and recorded in your V5C logbook.

Three methods will confirm recall status:

1. Mercedes-Benz Owner Portal. Log in at the official Mercedes-Benz UK owner portal and enter your VIN. This returns all open recalls and any TSBs that have been flagged against your specific vehicle. You'll need to create a free account, but the recall check itself requires no payment.

2. DVSA Recall Database. The UK Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency maintains a public recall search. Enter your VIN or registration number to see all safety recalls issued for your vehicle, regardless of manufacturer. This is the most independent check available.

3. Any Mercedes-Benz Retailer. A main dealer can run your VIN through their internal system in under five minutes, free of charge, with no obligation. This is the fastest option if you're already booking service work. Ask specifically whether any Technical Service Bulletins apply as well - these don't always appear on the public recall database.

If you're buying a used B-Class and want to check before committing, our VIN decoder tool will confirm the engine code, build date, and original specification from the same 17-character code - useful context before you cross-reference against the recall table above.

What Isn't Covered by a Recall

This distinction matters more for the B-Class than most Mercedes models, because several of the most common issues owners report fall outside recall scope entirely.

Technical Service Bulletins are not recalls. A TSB instructs workshops on the correct diagnostic and repair procedure for a known issue, but the manufacturer does not proactively contact owners. The work is not free unless the car is still under warranty or a goodwill gesture is offered. For the B-Class, the most significant TSB relates to the OM651 diesel engine's timing chain tensioner - documented under TSB reference LI80.20-P-050001 [EM-VERIFIED]. If your B200 CDI or B200d exhibits a cold-start rattle that disappears after a few seconds, this TSB applies. The repair is not free out of warranty, but knowing the TSB reference ensures the workshop addresses the correct component rather than misdiagnosing.

Wear items and service-related failures are never recalled. DPF blockage, EGR valve coking, injector seal degradation, and turbocharger wear on higher-mileage B-Class diesels are maintenance and design-tolerance issues, not safety defects. These are covered in detail on our EGR valve and cooler failure page and our DPF blockage guide, neither of which will ever trigger a recall notice.

Aftermarket modifications void recall eligibility for affected components. If a previous owner has fitted non-standard parts in the area covered by a recall - a replacement steering column from a breaker, for instance - the recall remedy may be refused until the vehicle is returned to standard specification.

Known Faults Beyond Recalls

The B-Class shares its diesel engines with higher-volume Mercedes models, which means the failure patterns are well documented across thousands of enquiries. The OM651 in particular is the most-enquired Mercedes diesel engine in our dataset - 1,250 requests in 2025 alone [EM-VERIFIED] - and the B200 CDI and B200d variants share all of its known weaknesses.

The most significant non-recall faults for B-Class owners:

  • Timing chain tensioner wear (OM651): Cold-start rattle, typically between 80,000 and 150,000 miles. Covered by TSB LI80.20-P-050001. Full mechanism and cost breakdown on our OM651 balance shaft and timing chain page.
  • Injector seal failure ("Black Death"): Carbon buildup around injector bases caused by seal leakage. Most common OM651 failure in our enquiry data. Smell of exhaust gas in the cabin is the early warning.
  • EGR cooler and DPF clogging: Predominantly affects urban-driven B-Class diesels doing short journeys. The B-Class's compact engine bay makes access more labour-intensive than in the C-Class or E-Class, adding to fitted costs.

For petrol B-Class models - the B180 and B200 using the M270 engine - the primary concerns are timing chain stretch on earlier examples and thermostat housing leaks. The B180/B200 petrol variant page covers the full specification and known issues for that engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Mercedes B Class has been recalled?

Enter your 17-character VIN into the Mercedes-Benz owner portal, the DVSA recall database, or ask any Mercedes retailer to run the check. All three methods are free. The VIN is on your V5C logbook, the windscreen base plate, or the driver's door shut label.

Is a recall repair free on a Mercedes B Class?

Yes. Safety recalls are remedied free of charge at any Mercedes-Benz retailer regardless of the vehicle's age, mileage, or service history. You do not need to be the original owner. Technical Service Bulletins, however, are not free unless the car is under warranty.

What is the difference between a recall and a Technical Service Bulletin?

A recall is a safety-critical defect that the manufacturer must fix free of charge and must actively notify owners about. A TSB is a known issue with a documented repair procedure that workshops follow, but the manufacturer has no obligation to contact owners or cover the cost. The OM651 timing chain tensioner TSB is the most relevant example for B-Class diesel owners.

Can I check a B Class recall by registration number instead of VIN?

The DVSA database accepts registration numbers. The Mercedes-Benz owner portal requires the full VIN. If you only have the registration, use the DVSA route or our registration lookup tool to identify the VIN first, then cross-reference against the manufacturer system for TSBs.

Does a recall affect my B Class MOT or insurance?

An outstanding recall does not cause an MOT failure. However, if the recall relates to a safety-critical component like the steering column or airbag, driving with it unremedied could affect an insurance claim in the event of a related incident. Book the recall repair as soon as you're notified.
