Technical Service Bulletins (Non-Safety)

Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) differ from safety recalls: they address known mechanical or electronic faults that do not pose an immediate safety risk but which Mercedes-Benz has documented for workshop reference. TSBs are not automatically repaired free of charge unless covered under warranty or goodwill policy. The following are the most frequently referenced TSBs for C-Class engines in our specialist network [EM-INTERVIEW].

LI80.20-P-050001 - OM651 Timing Chain Tensioner Wear

Affected: OM651 engines, 2008–2015 build
Symptom: Cold-start rattle lasting 2–5 seconds, diminishing as oil pressure builds
Action: Inspect tensioner hydraulic damping function; replace with revised part number A6510500911 if wear exceeds specification. Not a safety recall, but widely recognised as preventative maintenance before chain stretch occurs [PATTERN DATA].

LI07.30-P-059876 - M274 Timing Chain Tensioner Spring Fatigue

Affected: M274 petrol engines, 2012–2018 build
Symptom: Intermittent rattling noise at idle after warm-up; no fault codes stored
Action: Measure chain elongation via camshaft adaptation values in XENTRY; replace tensioner assembly if adaptation exceeds 8° crank angle. Commonly addressed during scheduled service rather than as standalone repair [EM-INTERVIEW].

LI47.10-P-060123 - COMAND NTG5 Infotainment Reboot Loop

Affected: W205 with NTG5 head unit, 2014–2017 build
Symptom: Screen freezes or restarts cyclically; Bluetooth pairing fails intermittently
Action: Update head unit firmware to v18.0 or later; reset MOST bus ring if update does not resolve. Typically resolved under goodwill for vehicles outside warranty [EM-INTERVIEW].

LI14.30-P-061456 - 9G-TRONIC Gearbox Shudder at Low Speed

Affected: W205/W206 with 725.0 transmission, 2016–2021 build
Symptom: Judder or vibration between 30–50 mph in 4th–6th gear; worsens when cold
Action: Perform adaptive learning reset via XENTRY; flush and replace ATF with approved MB 236.17 fluid. Torque converter replacement only required if shudder persists post-adaptation [PATTERN DATA].

TSBs are referenced here for owner awareness and to support informed conversations with specialists. They do not constitute a guarantee of free repair. What Isn’t Covered by a Recall

Recalls address safety-critical defects identified by the manufacturer or regulator. They do not cover general wear items, maintenance failures, or faults arising from previous owner neglect. Understanding this distinction prevents misdiagnosis and unnecessary expectation of free repair.

  • Timing chain wear on OM651/M274 engines - unless specifically cited in a TSB with a revised part number, chain stretch is considered normal wear and is not recalled. Our OM651 balance shaft failure page explains the mechanism and typical repair costs [EM-QUOTE].
  • DPF blockage and EGR valve coking - these result from driving patterns and service intervals, not manufacturing defects. See our DPF blockage guide and EGR cooler failure page for diagnosis and cost bands [EM-QUOTE].
  • Injector seal leaks (“Black Death”) - while documented in TSB LI80.20-P-050001, this is classified as a service item, not a safety recall. Repair costs typically range £400–£1,200 depending on cylinder count affected [EM-QUOTE].
  • Suspension bushings, brake components, and clutch wear - consumable items excluded from all recall campaigns regardless of premature failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Mercedes C-Class has an outstanding recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN into the checker above or visit the official GOV.UK vehicle recall service. Both sources query the same DVSA database and return results instantly. You can also contact any Mercedes-Benz retailer with your VIN - they will confirm status and book the repair if needed.

Are recall repairs really free, even on older C-Class models?

Yes. Safety recalls are mandated by law and must be completed at no cost to the vehicle owner, regardless of age, mileage, or number of previous keepers. This includes parts, labour, and any associated diagnostics. Authorised dealers and many independent specialists in our vetted network can perform recall work.

What’s the difference between a recall and a Technical Service Bulletin?

A recall addresses a safety defect and is legally enforceable - the manufacturer must notify owners and fix it free of charge. A TSB documents a known non-safety fault for workshop reference; repairs are only free if the vehicle is under warranty or the manufacturer offers goodwill coverage. TSBs are valuable for diagnosis but do not carry the same legal obligation.

My C-Class isn’t listed here - does that mean it has no recalls?

Not necessarily. This page covers the most significant campaigns for W204, W205, and W206 generations. Earlier models (W203, W202) and niche variants (CL203 coupe, S202 estate) may have separate campaigns. Always verify via VIN check rather than relying solely on published lists.
