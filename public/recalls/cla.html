Mercedes CLA Recalls & Technical Service Bulletins

Every known recall and TSB affecting the Mercedes CLA across both the C117 (2013–2019) and C118 (2019–present) generations, with affected VIN ranges and remedies. Check your own vehicle below.

The Mercedes CLA has been subject to multiple safety recalls and technical service bulletins across both generations.

  • The C117 (2013–2019) and C118 (2019–present) share platform architecture with the A-Class, meaning some recalls apply across both model lines
  • Recall repairs are always free of charge at any authorised Mercedes-Benz dealer, regardless of vehicle age or ownership history
  • You can verify whether your specific CLA is affected using your VIN — the 17-character code found on your V5C logbook or the base of the windscreen
  • Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) differ from recalls: they address known issues but are not safety-critical and may not be performed proactively

How to Check Your CLA's VIN for Recalls

Your Vehicle Identification Number is the only reliable way to confirm whether your specific car falls within an affected production batch. Here is how to find and use it.

Where to Find Your VIN

The CLA's VIN appears in three locations: stamped on the vehicle identification plate at the base of the windscreen (visible from outside the driver's side), printed on the driver's door B-pillar sticker, and recorded in your V5C registration document. The VIN is always 17 characters — a mix of letters and numbers, never including I, O or Q to avoid confusion with numerals.

Checking Against the Official Database

Mercedes-Benz operates a recall lookup portal where you enter your full 17-character VIN and receive an immediate confirmation of any outstanding campaigns. Alternatively, any authorised Mercedes-Benz dealer can run the same check on their internal system in under two minutes. If you are buying a used CLA — particularly a C117 approaching or past 100,000 miles — requesting a recall status check should form part of your pre-purchase inspection. Our VIN Decoder tool can identify your CLA's build specification and engine code before you cross-reference against the recall list.

What Happens If Your CLA Is Affected

If your VIN falls within an affected range, Mercedes-Benz UK will typically send a letter to the registered keeper. The repair is free regardless of how many owners the car has had or whether it was originally purchased through a Mercedes dealer. You book directly with any authorised repairer; the work usually takes between 30 minutes and three hours depending on the campaign.

What Isn't Covered by a Recall

Recalls address safety-critical defects present at the time of manufacture. They do not cover wear-and-tear items, servicing neglect, or problems introduced by previous owners. Understanding this boundary prevents false expectations and helps you budget correctly for ownership.

Wear Items and Scheduled Maintenance

Brake pads, discs, wiper blades, tyres, drive belts and service fluids are never subject to recall even when they fail prematurely. If your CLA's braking components are corroded due to age rather than a manufacturing defect in the booster housing, the repair cost falls to you. Known Issues Without a Formal Recall

Some problems are well-documented among specialists and forums but have not triggered a formal recall campaign. These include certain software glitches in early MBUX units, intermittent DPF regeneration faults on the OM654 diesel variants, and occasional turbo actuator sticking on higher-mileage examples. These issues are addressed through TSBs or goodwill repairs at dealer discretion, but they are not guaranteed free fixes. If your CLA develops one of these symptoms, our turbocharger failure guide and limp mode diagnostic page explain what to expect and what it typically costs.

Modifications and Non-OEM Parts

If a previous owner has fitted non-standard components — particularly in the steering, braking or airbag systems — a recall repair may be refused until the vehicle is returned to standard specification. This is uncommon on CLAs but worth checking on any car with a modified history.

Related Known Faults on the CLA

Beyond formal recalls, the CLA shares several well-documented failure patterns with its A-Class siblings. These are not safety recalls but are common enough that any prospective buyer or current owner should be aware of them.

The C117 generation (2013–2019) uses the OM651 four-cylinder diesel in its 200 CDI and 220 CDI variants — an engine with documented injector seal and timing chain tensioner concerns covered in detail on our OM651 balance shaft failure page. Petrol C117 models use the M270 turbocharged four-cylinder, which has its own timing chain considerations addressed in our M274 timing chain tensioner guide.

The C118 generation (2019–present) predominantly uses the OM654 diesel, a substantially improved unit. However, AdBlue system faults and occasional EGR cooler issues have been reported by specialists, and the petrol M282 variant has shown early examples of carbon buildup on direct-injection intake valves. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Mercedes CLA has an outstanding recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN into the official Mercedes-Benz recall portal, or ask any authorised Mercedes dealer to run the check. It takes under two minutes and costs nothing. You will need your V5C logbook or the VIN plate at the base of your windscreen to hand.

Is a recall repair really free on a used CLA I just bought?

Yes. Recall repairs are free regardless of the vehicle's age, mileage, or how many owners it has had. The obligation sits with the manufacturer, not the original purchaser. Simply book with any Mercedes-Benz authorised repairer and quote the recall reference.

What is the difference between a recall and a Technical Service Bulletin?

A recall is a safety-critical defect that the manufacturer is legally required to fix free of charge and must proactively notify owners about. A TSB acknowledges a known issue and provides a fix procedure, but it is typically applied during routine servicing rather than via a dedicated owner notification. TSB repairs may or may not be free depending on warranty status and dealer goodwill.

Are CLA recalls the same as A-Class recalls?

Many are. The CLA shares its platform and most mechanical components with the A-Class — the W176 with the C117, and the W177 with the C118. If an A-Class recall affects a shared component like the steering column or airbag ECU, the CLA is almost always included in the same campaign. Always confirm with your specific VIN rather than assuming.
