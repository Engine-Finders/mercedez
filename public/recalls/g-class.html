Recalls Affecting the Mercedes G-Class

Like every vehicle sold in the UK, the G-Class is subject to recall campaigns when Mercedes-Benz identifies a safety-related defect in production. The manufacturer notifies the DVSA, writes to the registered keeper, and carries out the fix free of charge — parts and labour included, whatever the vehicle's age.

Because the G-Class has been in continuous production longer than almost any passenger vehicle on sale, its recall history spans four distinct generations. The table below is our working record. How to Check Your G-Class by VIN

Three routes, fastest first.

1. The DVSA's free UK recall check. Enter your registration or VIN in the gov.uk vehicle recall checker to see outstanding safety recalls recorded against the vehicle [THIRD-PARTY — DVSA]. It takes under two minutes and needs no account.

2. Any Mercedes-Benz retailer. Give them the VIN and they can search open recall campaigns and service actions — including items that may not yet appear on public checkers. This is also the only route that surfaces technical service bulletins relevant to your exact build.

3. Our VIN decoder. Our free VIN decoder tool breaks down your G-Class build data — model year, engine, plant and equipment — which is useful when matching your car against era-specific campaigns.

The VIN itself: on a W463 or W463-II you'll find it on the plate at the base of the windscreen and on the door shut; older vehicles also carry a chassis stamp. If the plate is illegible — common on early W463s after decades of hard use — the chassis stamp is the definitive reference.

One rule matters above all: recalls follow the vehicle, not the owner. If you're buying a G-Class secondhand, run the check before you commit — an outstanding recall is a bargaining point, and sometimes a safety one.

What Recalls Don't Cover

A recall covers a specific safety defect declared by the manufacturer. The problems G-Class owners actually live with often sit outside that scope — known patterns, not campaigns, and they're paid for by the owner.

G350d — OM642 V6 diesel. The two patterns workshops report most are oil cooler seal leaks in the V of the engine, typically £800–£1,500, and swirl flap motor failures, usually £500–£1,200, most often reported between 70k and 150k miles [EM-QUOTE] [PATTERN DATA]. Full background, including prevention, is on the OM642 engine knowledge centre.

G63 AMG — M177 V8. Oil leaks from the valve covers, typically £800–£1,500, plus coolant system issues, most often reported between 60k and 120k miles [EM-QUOTE] [PATTERN DATA]. The unit is covered in depth on the M176/M177 engine page.

W463 structure. Chassis and subframe corrosion on older examples — see our subframe corrosion failure guide — sits in MOT inspection territory, not recall territory, and is the single most important thing to check on any pre-2010 car.

None of this replaces a recall check. But if your VIN comes back clean and the car still has a problem, this is where the real-world G-Class problem list begins.

W463-II 2018–present OM656 diesel, M176 / M177 petrol Re-engineered body with independent front suspension; newest generation, so the fewest completed campaigns — but early-build campaigns do exist

Recall exposure tracks production volume and age. The W463's near thirty-year run means it dominates UK G-Class recall searches, while W463-II campaigns mostly concern early production. The W460 and W461 are now largely historic — surviving open items would be exceptional, but a DVSA check costs nothing.

AMG variants aren't exempt. Campaigns have covered G55, G63 and G65 production, so badge-based assumptions don't work — the VIN check is the only reliable answer.

What to Do If Your G-Class Is Recalled

Book with a Mercedes-Benz retailer. Recall work is free, and parts are supplied under the campaign; the retailer schedules the job against parts availability, which on a low-volume vehicle like the G-Class can matter more than on a C-Class.

Ask about timescales and courtesy vehicles up front. Some G-Class campaigns involve components that aren't stocked at every workshop, and waiting times vary.

If you've already paid to fix a fault that later turns out to be recall-related, ask the retailer about reimbursement before assuming the money is lost — manufacturers do consider retrospective claims on some campaigns, with receipts.

Keep the documentation. Proof of completed recall work is worth having at resale: G-Class buyers are thorough, and an open recall is exactly the kind of item that surfaces in a pre-purchase check.

If your problem isn't recall-related at all — an older W463 with subframe rust, or an OM642 seeping at the oil cooler — the question becomes repair economics. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check Mercedes G-Class recalls by VIN?

Three routes: the DVSA's free UK vehicle recall checker at gov.uk, any Mercedes-Benz retailer (who can also see technical service bulletins), and our own VIN decoder for build-data confirmation. All three are free, and the DVSA check takes under two minutes.

Do I have to pay for a G-Class safety recall repair?

No. Recall repairs are carried out free of charge — parts and labour — regardless of the vehicle's age, mileage or how many owners it has had. That's true even for imported and ex-military vehicles once they're registered in the UK.

Can I sell a G-Class with an outstanding recall?

Yes — an outstanding recall doesn't legally prevent a sale — but you should disclose it, and a buyer should check for it. Completing the work before selling removes a negotiation point; buying with one open gives you legitimate leverage on price.

What's the difference between a recall and a technical service bulletin?

A recall is a safety defect declared by the manufacturer: it's fixed free for every affected vehicle, and the registered keeper is written to. A technical service bulletin is workshop guidance on a known issue — it's carried out when symptoms appear, and it's paid for by the owner unless warranty covers it.

Are G63 AMG models covered by the same recall process?

Yes. AMG variants go through the identical campaign process, and campaigns have covered G55, G63 and G65 production. Don't assume a badge is exempt — or more exposed — and check the individual VIN instead.
