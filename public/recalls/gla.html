GLA Technical Service Bulletins We Track

A recall and a technical service bulletin are not the same thing. A recall addresses a safety defect: the manufacturer must notify owners and the repair is free at any authorised retailer, whatever the vehicle's age [PATTERN DATA]. A TSB is manufacturer guidance to workshops about a known fault pattern: it is not a mandated fix, not free by default, and is usually applied when the fault appears or during related service work [PATTERN DATA]. That distinction matters on the GLA, because the best-documented issues with its engines sit under TSBs rather than recalls.

How to Run a Mercedes GLA Recall Check by VIN

The definitive recall check is free and takes under a minute:

  1. Find your VIN. It's the 17-character number on your V5C logbook, visible at the base of the windscreen and on the driver's door shut. Our VIN decoder explains what each segment tells you about the car's build.
  2. Run the DVSA recall search. The official UK recall database is free, needs no account, and lists every registered safety recall by VIN [PATTERN DATA].
  3. Or check through Mercedes-Benz. Any Mercedes-Benz retailer can pull your car's complete recall and service history from the VIN alone, and the manufacturer's own recall lookup works the same way [PATTERN DATA].
  4. If a recall shows outstanding, book it in. The work is free at any authorised retailer regardless of age or ownership; parts may need ordering, so book early rather than waiting for a reminder letter [PATTERN DATA].

One honest limit: our registration lookup identifies your engine and model specification — useful when cross-referencing the TSB table above — but it is not a substitute for the official recall check, and we never present it as one.

What Isn't Covered by a Recall

Recalls are narrow by design. They cover safety-critical defects — airbag, seat belt, steering, braking and structural items — that the manufacturer or regulator has formally registered [PATTERN DATA]. They do not cover:

  • Wear and service items — chains, injectors, DPFs, EGR valves and turbos age out as maintenance, whatever any forum thread implies
  • Non-safety defects — the OM651 chain tensioner bulletin is a TSB, so the repair is a service matter unless a retailer applies goodwill [PATTERN DATA]
  • Consequential damage — damage caused by driving on a known fault is typically excluded even where a recall exists [PATTERN DATA]

When a fault turns out not to be recall-covered, the question becomes whether repair is worth it against the car's value. This site applies the 70% Mercedes repair-to-value threshold to that calculation, with the full method and worked examples on the repair, replace or scrap framework page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Mercedes GLA recall check free?

Yes. Both the DVSA recall search and any check run through Mercedes-Benz or an authorised retailer are free and need no account. The recall repair itself is also free, whatever the car's age or how many owners it has had [PATTERN DATA].

How do I check Mercedes GLA recalls by VIN?

Find the 17-character VIN on your V5C, the windscreen base or the driver's door shut, then enter it into the DVSA recall search or give it to any Mercedes-Benz retailer. Both routes list every registered recall against that specific car [PATTERN DATA].

How do I know if my Mercedes GLA has an outstanding safety recall?

A VIN check shows the answer immediately — an outstanding recall appears as unresolved until a retailer completes and logs the work. If you've bought a used GLA with no recall paperwork, assume nothing and run the check; letters go to the registered keeper, so previous-owner correspondence may have been missed [PATTERN DATA].

Does a Mercedes GLA safety recall expire?

No. A safety recall remains open and free to have completed for the life of the vehicle, regardless of age, mileage or how long the recall was first announced [PATTERN DATA]. Delaying simply leaves the defect in place.

Are technical service bulletins free like recalls?

Not by default. TSBs are workshop guidance rather than mandated fixes, so bulletin-related repairs — like the OM651 timing chain tensioner work — are normally paid service items unless a retailer offers goodwill [PATTERN DATA].

What if a recall was missed on a used GLA I've bought?

Book it in — the repair is still free at any authorised retailer even years later and regardless of who owned the car before [PATTERN DATA]. There's no penalty for a missed recall; the only cost of waiting is living with the defect.
