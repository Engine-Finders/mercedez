Technical Service Bulletins for the GLC

Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) are distinct from recalls. A recall is a mandatory safety fix initiated by the manufacturer or a regulatory body. A TSB is internal guidance issued to Mercedes-Benz technicians describing a known issue and the recommended diagnostic or repair procedure. TSBs do not trigger a customer notification letter, and the repair is not always covered under warranty if the vehicle is out of its warranty period.

For the GLC, the most frequently referenced TSB categories include:

How to Check Your GLC's Recall Status by VIN

Every Mercedes-Benz GLC carries a unique 17-character Vehicle Identification Number (VIN). This is the definitive reference for determining whether your specific vehicle falls within a recall's affected build range. The VIN is more precise than model year alone, because recall campaigns are tied to production date ranges and specific factory build batches, not calendar years.

Where to Find Your GLC's VIN

  • Windscreen base: Visible through the glass at the bottom of the windscreen on the driver's side
  • Driver's door shut: On a metal or adhesive plate inside the driver's door frame
  • V5C logbook: Printed on your vehicle registration document
  • Insurance documents: Usually listed on your policy schedule

Where to Check the Recall Status

Once you have your VIN, you can verify recall applicability through several channels:

  • Mercedes-Benz UK recall checker: The official Mercedes-Benz website includes a recall lookup tool where you enter your VIN directly
  • DVSA recall database: The UK Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency maintains a public database of all safety recalls issued in the UK
  • Your local Mercedes-Benz dealer: Any authorised workshop can look up your VIN against the global recall register in minutes

What Isn't Covered by a Recall

Understanding the boundary between recall-covered work and owner-responsibility maintenance prevents costly surprises. A recall addresses a specific manufacturing defect or safety non-compliance identified during production or in-service testing. It does not cover:

  • Wear and tear: Brake pads, tyres, wiper blades, clutch components and other consumable items are never recall items regardless of failure timing
  • Service-related issues: Problems arising from missed service intervals, incorrect oil specification, or use of non-approved parts are excluded
  • Damage from external causes: Accident damage, flood damage, rodent damage to wiring, and corrosion from environmental exposure are not recall matters
  • Modifications: If your GLC has been modified — remapped, fitted with non-standard wheels, or had aftermarket electrical work — this may void recall applicability for the affected system
  • TSB-related repairs outside warranty: As noted above, TSBs are guidance for technicians, not warranty obligations. If your GLC is outside its warranty period, a TSB-related repair is chargeable

If you are unsure whether a particular fault on your GLC qualifies as recall work or standard repair, the safest approach is to present your VIN at a Mercedes-Benz dealer and ask them to check the recall register first. Related Known Faults for the GLC

Beyond formal recalls and TSBs, the GLC has several well-documented failure patterns reported by owners and confirmed through our enquiry data. These are not recall items but are important to know when assessing a used GLC or diagnosing an existing problem.

The GLC X253 (2015–2022) is primarily fitted with the OM651 and OM654 diesel engines, plus M274 petrol variants. The X254 (2022–present) uses the OM654 and newer M254/M256 engines. Each carries distinct failure profiles:

  • AdBlue / SCR system faults: The most commonly reported issue across both generations, particularly on OM654-equipped vehicles. NOx sensor failures and AdBlue heater circuit faults are frequent. Full detail on our AdBlue SCR failure page.
  • OM654 injector seal leaks: Carbon buildup around injector seals is a known pattern on higher-mileage OM654 units. See our OM654 injector seal guide.
  • 9G-Tronic transmission behaviour: Rough low-speed shifts and occasional hesitation are reported. Some cases are software-resolvable; others indicate mechanical wear. Covered in our transmission faults page.
  • AIRMATIC suspension compressor: On GLC models equipped with air suspension, compressor relay failure and valve block leaks are recurring issues. Full breakdown on our AIRMATIC compressor page.

None of these are currently subject to a formal recall, but they represent the most frequent reasons GLC owners seek specialist diagnosis. If you own a GLC with any of these symptoms, early intervention typically reduces repair cost significantly. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Mercedes GLC has a recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN into the official Mercedes-Benz UK recall checker on their website, or visit any authorised Mercedes-Benz dealer who can look up your VIN against the global recall register in minutes. The DVSA also maintains a public recall database searchable by make and model.

Is a Mercedes GLC recall free to fix?

Yes, all safety recall work is carried out free of charge at any authorised Mercedes-Benz workshop, regardless of your vehicle's age, mileage or whether you are the original owner. You will never be charged for a recall repair. If a dealer attempts to charge you for recall work, contact Mercedes-Benz Customer Services directly.

What's the difference between a recall and a Technical Service Bulletin?

A recall is a mandatory safety fix initiated by the manufacturer or regulator, communicated directly to registered keepers, and always free. A TSB is internal guidance for technicians describing a known issue and recommended repair procedure — it does not trigger customer notification and the repair may be chargeable if outside warranty.

Which GLC years are most affected by recalls?

Both the X253 (2015–2022) and X254 (2022–present) have been subject to recall campaigns, though the specific issues differ by generation and production period. The X253's longer production run means it has accumulated more campaigns over time. Use your VIN to check your specific vehicle rather than relying on model year alone, as recall applicability is tied to build date ranges.
