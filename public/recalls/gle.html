What Isn't Covered by a Recall

Recalls address safety-critical defects identified by the manufacturer or regulator. They do not cover:

  • Wear-and-tear items: brake pads, discs, suspension bushes, tyres and wiper blades are never recall items even if they fail prematurely [PATTERN DATA]
  • Design characteristics: the OM642's oil cooler seal design is a known engineering limitation, not a safety defect - it is addressed via TSB rather than recall [PATTERN DATA]
  • Emissions-related components outside mandatory scope: AdBlue system faults, EGR cooler degradation and DPF blockage are servicing items unless they trigger a specific emissions recall [PATTERN DATA]
  • Non-safety electrical faults: infotainment glitches, COMAND system resets and battery parasitic drain are not recall-eligible [PATTERN DATA]
  • Damage caused by prior repairs: if a non-dealer repair introduced a fault, the recall remedy may not apply [PATTERN DATA]

What to Do If Your GLE Has an Outstanding Recall

If your VIN check reveals an open recall or service campaign:

  • Book with any Mercedes-Benz dealer. Recall work is free regardless of which dealer services your car normally. You do not need to go to the supplying dealer [PATTERN DATA].
  • Bring your V5C and service history. While not strictly required for recall work, having documentation speeds the process and helps the technician identify any related TSBs that can be addressed simultaneously [PATTERN DATA].
  • Ask about related TSBs. If your GLE is in for a recall, ask whether any open TSBs can be addressed at the same visit. Labour is already booked, so incremental TSB work often carries reduced cost [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Do not delay safety-critical recalls. Seat belt, airbag and brake-related recalls should be addressed immediately. Non-safety TSBs can wait until your next service [PATTERN DATA].
  • Keep records. Note the recall reference number and completion date. This matters for resale value and any future warranty claims [PATTERN DATA].

If the recall relates to the engine or a component that could lead to engine damage, consider checking your options on our repair vs replace calculator before committing to any out-of-warranty repair that the recall doesn't cover.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Mercedes GLE has a recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN into the official Mercedes-Benz recall checker online, or visit any Mercedes-Benz dealer. The check is free and takes under two minutes. You can also use our VIN decoder to confirm your model and engine first.

Is a recall repair free on a Mercedes GLE?

Yes. All recall repairs are completed free of charge at any Mercedes-Benz dealer, regardless of the vehicle's age, mileage or ownership history. This applies whether you are the first owner or the fifth.

What's the difference between a recall and a TSB?

A recall is a mandatory safety fix issued when a defect poses a risk to occupants or other road users. A Technical Service Bulletin (TSB) addresses known issues that don't meet the safety threshold - dealers may fix TSB items during servicing but aren't obligated to proactively contact owners.

Are GLE diesel engines more likely to have recalls than petrol?

Diesel variants (OM642 in W166, OM656 in V167) have generated more TSBs related to emissions systems, oil cooler seals and turbo components. Petrol variants (M276, M256) have fewer TSBs but are not immune - timing chain tensioner issues are documented on the M276 [PATTERN DATA].

Can I get a TSB fix done for free?

Not automatically. TSB repairs are typically carried out at owner expense unless the vehicle is under warranty or the dealer offers goodwill. However, if your GLE is in for a recall, asking the dealer to address a related TSB at the same visit often results in reduced labour cost [EM-INTERVIEW].
