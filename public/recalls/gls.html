Two generations matter here. The current X167 replaced it from 2019. Our X166 guide and X167 guide cover the full model history and engine line-ups.

How to run a Mercedes GLS recall check

A Mercedes GLS recall by VIN is the only check that counts, because campaigns attach to specific build ranges within a model - two identical-looking GLS 450s can have different recall status. Here is the sequence we recommend:

  1. Find the VIN. It's on the plate at the base of the windscreen, the door shut panel, and in the V5C logbook. On a pre-2015 car remember it may be badged GL rather than GLS - the VIN doesn't change, so this doesn't affect the lookup.
  2. Check the official channels. Any Mercedes franchised dealer will run your VIN against the manufacturer's recall register free of charge, and the GOV.UK vehicle recall lookup covers UK-registered vehicles [THIRD-PARTY]. Our VIN decoder also helps you confirm model, generation and engine from the same number.
  3. Cross-reference this page. The sourced table above shows what each confirmed campaign involves and what the remedy looks like, so you know what to expect before booking.
  4. Bought used? Check anyway. Recall entitlement follows the vehicle, not the keeper, and there is no time limit on completing an open recall - previous owners may never have had the work done.
  5. Get it done at no cost. Genuine recall repairs are completed free at a franchised dealer, regardless of age or mileage. If anyone quotes you for a job described as a recall, question it.

TSBs, recalls and known faults - three different things

Owners regularly tell us a garage "did the recall" when something else entirely happened, so the distinction is worth being precise about:

  • A safety recall is a formal campaign notified to the authorities. The manufacturer contacts registered keepers and fixes the affected cars free of charge.
  • A technical service bulletin (TSB) is internal workshop guidance about a known issue. It tells dealers how to fix something - it does not oblige anyone to fix it free, and most owners never hear the bulletin exists.
  • A known fault is a failure pattern documented from real-world experience that never became a campaign at all.

That last category is where most of the GLS conversation actually lives. The issues this platform is known for - the OM642's oil cooler seal, the M276's timing chain, AIRMATIC wear - are TSB and known-fault territory rather than recalled items, which is exactly why the next section exists.

Known faults worth checking alongside recalls

None of the following are confirmed recalls - they are documented failure patterns for the engines fitted to GLS and GL X166 cars, worth inspecting on any example you're buying or owning [PATTERN DATA].

  • OM642 V6 diesel (X166 350d-era cars). Oil cooler seal leaks from the V of the engine, swirl flap motor failures and turbo seal leaks, typically between 70,000 and 150,000 miles. Indicative independent costs run £800–£1,500 for the oil cooler and £500–£1,200 for swirl flaps, and access makes both labour-intensive [PATTERN DATA]. Full detail on the OM642 knowledge centre.
  • M276 V6 petrol (X166 petrols). Timing chain stretch, carbon buildup on intake valves and gasket oil leaks, most common on earlier builds between 60,000 and 120,000 miles; chain work runs £1,500–£2,500 [PATTERN DATA]. See the M276 knowledge centre.
  • OM656 straight-six diesel (X167 350d/400d). A notably more robust design; the area to watch is the AdBlue/SCR emissions system as mileage builds [PATTERN DATA] - covered on our AdBlue and SCR failure page.
  • AIRMATIC air suspension (both generations). Compressor and valve-block wear is the platform's most common non-engine bill on higher-mileage cars - our AIRMATIC compressor failure guide covers the symptoms and costs.

What recalls don't cover - and the money question

A recall fixes a specific safety defect on a specific build range, free of charge. It does not cover wear items, servicing, or anything outside the campaign's scope - which is why owners of out-of-campaign cars end up asking whether major repair work is still worth doing.

For the X166, our data says usually yes: typical age-relevant values run £10,000–£28,000 [THIRD-PARTY] against reconditioned supply of £3,200–£6,500 [EM-QUOTE], which clears this site's 70% repair-to-value threshold comfortably - the ✅ Safe flag in our repair, replace or scrap framework. The predecessor is a closer call: the older GL X164 generated 72 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED] but sits at ⚠️ Watch, since replacement cost can take a large share of a sub-£6k example's value. Check the individual car before committing either way.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if my Mercedes GLS has been recalled?

Use the VIN, not the badge. Any Mercedes franchised dealer will check it against the manufacturer's recall register free of charge, and the GOV.UK recall lookup covers UK-registered vehicles [THIRD-PARTY]. Then cross-reference the sourced table on this page for context on the remedy.

Can I check Mercedes GLS recalls by VIN for free?

Yes. Both the franchised-dealer lookup and the GOV.UK service are free, and our VIN decoder is free too - it confirms your model, generation and engine from the same number.

I bought my GLS used - do recalls still apply?

Yes. Recall entitlement follows the vehicle, not the keeper, and open recalls don't expire. Check as soon as you take ownership; a previous keeper may never have had the work completed.

Do Mercedes recall repairs cost anything?

No. Genuine recall work is carried out at the manufacturer's expense, whatever the vehicle's age or mileage. If you're quoted for a repair described as a recall, question it before paying.

Is a TSB the same as a Mercedes GLS safety recall?

No. A recall is a formal safety campaign with a notification duty and free repairs. A TSB is internal workshop guidance that informs how repairs are done but creates no obligation to do them free - and most GLS issues owners discuss, from OM642 oil cooler leaks to AIRMATIC wear, sit in TSB or known-fault territory.

What's the difference between a GL and a GLS for recall purposes?

Same X166 chassis, different badge era - the car was sold as the GL until the 2015 facelift renamed it GLS. Official records may list campaigns under either name, which is one more reason the VIN, not the tailgate badge, is what you check against.
