How to Check Your Own VIN

The VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) is the only reliable way to know if your Sprinter is affected by a specific recall. It is stamped on a plate visible through the windscreen on the driver's side, and also on the driver's door pillar. Enter your full 17-character VIN on the official DVSA recall checker or ask a Mercedes dealer to run it. Do not rely on the vehicle's registration alone - some recalls apply to specific VIN ranges within a model year, not the entire batch. If you have not received a letter from Mercedes but your van is in the range, it may be because the previous owner never updated their address - checking your VIN is the only failsafe.

What Isn't Covered by a Recall

Recalls address safety-critical defects - not general wear-and-tear. A recall will not cover a failed alternator, a worn clutch, or a leaky air suspension strut, even if they are expensive. Nor will it cover failure that occurred before the recall was issued if the car has already been repaired elsewhere, unless the repair was a direct consequence of the recall fault. If you are facing a failure that is not safety-related, or your van falls outside the VIN range, you will need to pay for repairs yourself. Related Known Faults (Not Recalls)

Some Sprinter failures are not safety recalls but are frequent enough to be documented in TSBs and in our own enquiry data. These include:

  • OM651 Balance Shaft Failure - a common issue on high-mileage Sprinters, not a recall, but expensive to fix.
  • DPF Blockage - urban Sprinters suffer from this, and while a software update exists, it is not a recall.
  • EGR Valve & Cooler Faults - although some EGR recalls exist, not all builds are covered.
  • Turbocharger Failure - common on higher-mileage vans, especially if oil changes have been skipped.

Verdict: Recall vs Replacement

If your Sprinter has a recall, get it fixed for free - there is no decision to make. The value of the van is irrelevant; the work is paid for by Mercedes. The decision only arises if the recall has caused secondary damage (e.g., the EGR cooler leak has already caused engine overheating) or if the failure is not a recall. In those cases, apply the 70% threshold: if the cost of a reconditioned engine exceeds 70% of the van's market value, consider scrapping or selling for parts. For a typical 2015–2018 Sprinter worth £4,000–£8,000, a £3,000–£4,500 repair is borderline - use our Repair vs Replace calculator to run your specific numbers. [EM-INTERVIEW]

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my Sprinter has an outstanding recall?

Use the official DVSA recall checker or enter your full 17‑digit VIN on the Mercedes recall portal. You can also call any Mercedes dealer with your VIN. [THIRD-PARTY]

Can I still drive my Sprinter if it has a recall?

If the recall is for a minor issue like a software update, yes. If it is for a potential fire risk (e.g., EGR cooler) or loss of drive, you should contact your dealer immediately and avoid driving if possible. [THIRD-PARTY]

Is the repair always free?

Yes, if the repair is a safety recall and your vehicle is within the affected VIN range, Mercedes will carry out the work at no cost. This applies regardless of whether the van is still under warranty. [THIRD-PARTY]

What if the recall caused damage before I had it fixed?

You would need to make a claim through Mercedes customer service. It is not guaranteed, but if you can prove the recall defect caused secondary damage, they sometimes cover it. Our data shows about 20% of such claims succeed. [EM-INTERVIEW]
