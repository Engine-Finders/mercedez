Exhaust Symptoms

What's coming out of the exhaust tells a story. Here's what each colour and sound means on a Mercedes diesel or petrol.

Black Smoke

Over-fuelling — often injector, MAF, or turbo‑related. Common on OM651 and OM642 diesels.

Blue Smoke

Oil burning — turbo seals, valve stem seals, or piston rings. Serious on any Mercedes engine.

White Smoke

Coolant ingress (head gasket) or unburnt diesel. Can be urgent — especially on OM642 and M276.

Turbo Whistle

Boost leak or turbo wear. Often precedes turbo failure on OM651 and OM642.

Noise & Vibration Symptoms

Your Mercedes makes noises it shouldn't? Here's what those sounds mean.

Engine Knocking Noise

Bottom‑end or timing chain — often catastrophic on M272, M276 and OM642.

Cold Start Rattle

Timing chain tensioner wear. Classic OM651, OM642 and M272 warning.

Rattle Under Acceleration

Turbo actuator, wastegate or exhaust heat shield. Common on OM651.

Misfire / Rough Idle

Injector, ignition or vacuum issue. Cross‑engine symptom — varies by engine.

Drivability Symptoms

When your Mercedes doesn't drive as it should — loss of power, limp mode, or won't start.

Limp Mode

Reduced power due to a fault — often DPF, EGR, sensor or transmission. Common on all Mercedes.

Cranks But Won't Start

Fuel, air, spark, or timing issue. OM651 and OM642 have specific failure modes here.

Excessive Oil Consumption

Piston rings, valve seals, or turbo seals. Common on higher‑mileage M272 and M276.

Fluid & Temperature Symptoms

Coolant loss, overheating, or mixing fluids — these are often the most urgent Mercedes symptoms.

Coolant Loss — No Visible Leak

Head gasket, EGR cooler, or internal crack. Common on OM642 and M276.

Engine Overheating

Water pump, thermostat, or blocked cooling system. Cross‑engine.

Oil in Coolant

Head gasket or oil cooler failure. Classic OM642 oil cooler seal failure.

Oil Dilution (fuel smell)

Injector leak or DPF regeneration over‑fuelling. Common on OM651.

Warning Lights

If a warning light is on, start here — then head to our dedicated Warning Lights index for the full list.

Engine Management Light

Generic — needs a code read. Often accompanied by a symptom.

DPF Warning Light

Common on all Mercedes diesels. Often linked to black smoke or limp mode.

AdBlue Warning

SCR system alert — can trigger limp mode and countdown to no‑start.

Oil Pressure Warning

Immediate stop — engine‑destroying if ignored. Common on M272 and M276.

Engine‑Specific Symptoms

These pages combine a symptom with a specific engine — because the same symptom means something different on an OM651 than on an M276.

OM651 Black Smoke

Injector or EGR‑specific on the 2.1‑litre four‑cylinder.

OM651 Cold Start Rattle

Timing chain tensioner wear — a known OM651 weakness.

OM651 Blue Smoke

Turbo oil seals or valve stem seals.

OM642 Black Smoke

Swirl flap or injector‑related on the V6 diesel.

OM642 Cold Start Rattle

Timing chain or oil cooler seal related.

OM642 Oil in Coolant

The classic oil cooler seal failure on the OM642 V6.

M272 Black Smoke

Over‑fuelling on the petrol V6 — often injector or MAF.

M272 Cold Start Rattle

Timing chain tensioner — a known M272 weakness.

M272 Knocking Noise

Bottom‑end or timing chain — often catastrophic.

M276 Black Smoke

Direct‑injection carbon issues or injector.

M276 Cold Start Rattle

Camshaft adjuster or timing chain — known M276 issue.

M274 Black Smoke

Turbo or injector on the 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder petrol.

M157 Black Smoke

Over‑fuelling on the AMG V8 — often injector or HPFP.

M157 Cold Start Rattle

Timing chain adjuster — a known M157 issue.

M157 Oil in Coolant

Oil cooler or head gasket — urgent on AMG.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common Mercedes engine symptom?

Black smoke on diesel models (OM651 and OM642) is one of the most common symptoms, often caused by a failing injector or a clogged air filter. Cold‑start rattle is also very common on OM651, OM642 and M272 engines.

What symptoms mean I should stop driving immediately?

Oil pressure warning, coolant temperature warning with steam, severe knocking, or any flashing red warning light. Stop the engine and call for recovery — continued driving can cause catastrophic engine failure.

How do I know if a symptom is engine‑specific or generic?

Our engine‑specific pages (e.g., OM651 black smoke) explain how the symptom presents on that particular engine, what components are most likely to fail, and what the typical costs are. If your engine isn't listed, use the generic symptom page.

Why are some symptoms linked to fault codes?

Many symptoms (e.g., limp mode, black smoke) are often accompanied by a fault code. Our Fault Code index cross‑references symptoms and codes so you can diagnose from either direction.
