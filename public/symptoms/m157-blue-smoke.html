Mercedes M157 Blue Smoke From Exhaust

Blue smoke on this specific AMG V8 is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the M157, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the M157 specifically, blue smoke from the exhaust is a clear sign of oil burning in the combustion chambers — most commonly caused by worn valve stem seals, failing turbocharger seals, or piston ring wear. It's a serious issue that, if ignored, can lead to catalytic converter damage, fouled spark plugs, and eventually engine failure.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general blue smoke guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the M157 the picture narrows considerably. This AMG V8's dominant failure signature is a blue-tinted cloud of smoke from the exhaust, often most noticeable on cold start or after coasting, accompanied by rising oil consumption between services. It is distinct from the black smoke of over-fuelling (injectors, MAF) and the white smoke of coolant ingress (head gasket). On the M157, blue smoke almost always points to oil entering the combustion chambers through worn valve stem seals, failing turbocharger seals, or worn piston rings — all of which are known issues on higher-mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The M157's hot-vee design places the turbochargers in the valley of the V8, exposing critical oil seals to extreme heat cycles. Over time, the valve stem seals — which are made of a heat-resistant rubber compound — harden and begin to leak, allowing engine oil to seep past the valve guides and into the combustion chamber. This is the most common cause of blue smoke on the M157, and it's a design characteristic of this particular engine family. The same symptom on, say, a M276 V6 would point to a very different set of causes. Additionally, the M157's turbocharger oil seals are subject to similar heat degradation, and worn piston rings are a less common but known issue on vehicles that have suffered from poor oil change intervals or sustained high-load driving.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the oil level — an overfilled crankcase can cause oil to be forced past seals and into the combustion chambers. Also check the PCV system (positive crankcase ventilation); a blocked PCV valve can pressurise the crankcase and lead to oil consumption and blue smoke. If the blue smoke persists, especially if it's accompanied by a loss of power, a check engine light, or excessive oil consumption (more than 1 litre per 1,000 miles), it's time for a professional diagnosis. A specialist will perform a compression test and leak-down test to assess piston ring and valve seal condition, and will inspect the turbocharger for oil leakage. Do not ignore blue smoke; it will only get worse and can lead to catalytic converter damage and engine failure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you notice blue smoke. The M157's blue smoke is a clear sign of oil burning, and early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's a simple PCV valve or overfilled oil, the repair is cheap and straightforward. If it's valve stem seals or turbocharger seals, catching it early can prevent secondary damage to the catalytic converters and spark plugs. Frequently Asked Questions

Is blue smoke from an M157 always a valve stem seal issue?

It's the most common cause, but not the only one. Worn turbocharger seals, worn piston rings, a blocked PCV system, or overfilled oil can also cause blue smoke. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a compression test, leak-down test, and turbocharger inspection.

At what mileage does an M157 typically start producing blue smoke?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 100,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving conditions. Regular oil changes and using the correct oil specification can significantly reduce the risk.

Can I drive an M157 with blue smoke?

It's not recommended. Continued driving with blue smoke can quickly damage the catalytic converters and foul the spark plugs, leading to much more expensive repairs. Have it inspected as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix blue smoke on an M157?

Valve stem seal replacement typically costs £3,000–£6,000 at an independent specialist. A turbocharger replacement can cost £800–£2,200. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £6,000–£10,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
