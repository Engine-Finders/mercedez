Mercedes M157 Cold Start Rattle

Cold start rattle on this specific AMG V8 is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the M157, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the M157 specifically, a rattle on cold start is the most common early warning of timing chain tensioner wear or timing chain stretch. If ignored, the chain can skip teeth or break, causing catastrophic engine failure. The rattle is most pronounced on the first start of the day and typically lasts 1–2 seconds before the oil pressure builds.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general cold start rattle guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the M157 the picture narrows considerably. This AMG V8's dominant cold‑start signature is a brief, metallic rattle that lasts one to two seconds immediately after ignition, then disappears as oil pressure reaches the camshaft adjusters and timing chain tensioners. It is distinct from the constant rattle of a loose timing chain (which would indicate imminent failure) and from the knock of bottom‑end bearing wear (which is louder and rhythmic). On the M157, cold start rattle almost always points to wear in the timing chain tensioner or camshaft adjuster mechanism - both known issues on higher‑mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The M157's timing chain system uses hydraulic tensioners that rely on oil pressure to take up slack. When the engine is cold, oil drains from the tensioners, and if the tensioner's internal check valve fails or the tensioner itself wears, it cannot maintain tension until oil pressure builds - hence the brief rattle on startup. Over time, the chain can stretch, and the variable camshaft adjusters (cam phasers) can develop excessive play, exacerbating the rattle. This design characteristic is shared with other Mercedes V8s, but the M157's high‑performance nature and heat‑soak cycles accelerate wear. The same symptom on, say, a M276 V6 would point to a very similar mechanism but with different failure modes.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. The rattle could be caused by a loose heat shield, a failing accessory belt tensioner, or even a worn alternator pulley. Have a specialist listen to the rattle with a stethoscope to isolate its source. If the rattle is brief and only on cold start, it's highly likely the timing chain tensioner. If the rattle persists longer than two seconds or is accompanied by a check engine light (often codes related to camshaft position or timing), it indicates the tensioner is failing and the chain may have jumped. A diagnostic scan can reveal camshaft timing deviations, and a skilled Mercedes specialist can inspect the tensioner and chain through the oil fill cap or by removing the camshaft cover. Do not ignore a cold start rattle; it will only get worse and can lead to catastrophic engine failure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you hear a cold start rattle. The M157's cold start rattle is a clear early warning of timing chain tensioner wear, and addressing it promptly can prevent a catastrophic chain failure that would destroy the engine. If caught early, the repair is costly but manageable; if ignored, you risk a £6,000–£10,000 engine replacement. Frequently Asked Questions

Is cold start rattle on an M157 always a timing chain issue?

It's the most common cause, but not the only one. Worn camshaft adjusters, a failing tensioner, or low oil pressure due to a failing oil pump can also cause a rattle. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a diagnostic scan and a physical inspection of the timing chain components.

At what mileage does an M157 typically start rattling on cold start?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 100,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving style. Regular oil changes with the correct specification are the best prevention.

Can I drive an M157 with a cold start rattle?

It's not recommended. A rattle indicates that the timing chain tensioner is not holding enough pressure, and continued driving can cause the chain to jump or break, leading to catastrophic engine damage. Have it inspected as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix a cold start rattle on an M157?

If caught early, a timing chain tensioner replacement typically costs £1,500–£3,000 at an independent specialist. A full chain and guide replacement can cost £2,500–£4,500. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £6,000–£10,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
