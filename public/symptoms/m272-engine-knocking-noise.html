Mercedes M272 Engine Knocking Noise

Engine knocking noise on this specific V6 petrol is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the M272, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the M272 specifically, an engine knocking noise is a serious warning sign - it can indicate worn main bearings, a failing balance shaft timing chain tensioner, or even the beginning of the dreaded balance shaft gear failure. It's a critical issue that, if ignored, can lead to catastrophic engine failure.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine knocking noise guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the M272 the picture narrows considerably. This V6 petrol's dominant failure signature is a deep, rhythmic knocking or thumping sound from the engine, often most noticeable under load or at idle, and typically accompanied by a drop in oil pressure. It is distinct from the brief rattle of a timing chain tensioner (which occurs only on cold start and lasts a second) and from the high-pitched tap of valve train noise. On the M272, an engine knocking noise almost always points to worn main bearings, a failing balance shaft timing chain tensioner, or in the worst cases, the beginning of a catastrophic balance shaft gear failure - all known issues on higher-mileage units that have suffered from extended oil change intervals.

Why This Engine In Particular

The M272's design places significant stress on the balance shaft assembly, which is driven by a timing chain at the front of the engine. Over time, the balance shaft sprocket can wear, and the chain tensioner can fail, leading to excessive chain slack and a knocking noise. This is exacerbated by the engine's propensity for oil sludge buildup if service intervals are extended, which can block oil passages and starve the tensioner of oil pressure. Additionally, the main bearings can wear, especially on higher-mileage engines, leading to a deep knock that increases with engine speed. The same symptom on, say, a M276 V6 would point to a very different set of causes, often related to camshaft adjusters rather than balance shaft issues.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the oil level - low oil can cause a temporary knock that disappears when topped up. Listen carefully: is the knock rhythmic, and does it increase with engine RPM? A bottom-end knock will be deep and rhythmic, while a timing chain noise will be more of a rattle. A specialist can perform an oil pressure test and an oil analysis to check for bearing material in the oil. A stethoscope can isolate the source of the knock - if it's loudest at the bottom of the engine, it's likely main bearings. If it's at the front of the engine, it could be the balance shaft chain tensioner or guides. Do not ignore an engine knock; it will only get worse and can lead to a thrown rod or seized engine.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist immediately if you hear an engine knock. The M272's engine knocking noise is a serious early warning of balance shaft chain wear or bottom-end bearing failure. Addressing it early - whether it's a simple tensioner replacement or a more complex bearing replacement - can prevent a catastrophic engine failure. Frequently Asked Questions

Is engine knocking on an M272 always a balance shaft chain issue?

It's a common cause, but not the only one. Worn main bearings, a failing timing chain tensioner, or low oil pressure can also cause a knocking noise. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with an oil pressure test, oil analysis, and a stethoscope to isolate the source.

At what mileage does an M272 typically start knocking?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving style. Regular oil changes with the correct specification are the best prevention.

Can I drive an M272 with an engine knock?

Absolutely not. An engine knock indicates metal-to-metal contact in the bottom end or timing system, and continued driving will only cause catastrophic damage. Have it inspected immediately and do not drive the vehicle.

How much does it cost to fix an engine knock on an M272?

If caught early, balance shaft chain tensioner replacement can cost £2,500–£4,500 at an independent specialist. Main bearing replacement is similar at £3,000–£5,500. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £4,500–£7,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
