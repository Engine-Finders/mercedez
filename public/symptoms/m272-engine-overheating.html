Mercedes M272 Engine Overheating

Engine overheating on this specific V6 petrol is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the M272, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the M272 specifically, engine overheating is a critical warning sign that the cooling system is failing — most commonly caused by a failing water pump (often with a plastic impeller that cracks), a leaking radiator, a faulty thermostat, or a head gasket failure. It's a serious issue that, if ignored, can lead to warped cylinder heads, a blown head gasket, and catastrophic engine failure.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine overheating guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the M272 the picture narrows considerably. This V6 petrol's dominant cooling system failure signature is a rising temperature gauge, often accompanied by a loss of coolant and a warning light. Overheating on the M272 is distinct from the gradual temperature creep of a failing thermostat; it often occurs suddenly due to a catastrophic cooling system failure, such as a seized water pump or a burst radiator end tank. On the M272, engine overheating almost always points to a failure in the cooling system — most commonly the water pump (with its plastic impeller known to crack), the radiator, the thermostat, or the head gasket — all of which are known issues on higher-mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The M272's cooling system uses a water pump with a plastic impeller that can crack or separate from the shaft over time, leading to a loss of coolant circulation and rapid overheating. The radiator's plastic end tanks are also prone to cracking with age and thermal cycling, causing sudden coolant loss. The thermostat can stick closed, preventing coolant from reaching the radiator. Additionally, the M272's balance shaft timing chain issue (early models) can cause abnormal engine vibration that can accelerate cooling system component wear. The most serious cause is a leaking head gasket, which allows coolant to enter the combustion chambers, leading to overheating and loss of coolant. This design characteristic is shared with other Mercedes V6s, but the M272's age and commonality mean these failures are well-documented. The same symptom on, say, a M276 V6 would point to a similar mechanism but with different failure modes.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler possibilities. Check the coolant level — low coolant is the most common cause of overheating. If it's low, top it up and monitor for leaks. Check the radiator fan — if it's not running when the engine is hot, the fan motor or relay may have failed. Check the thermostat — if the engine takes a long time to warm up, the thermostat may be stuck open; if it overheats quickly, it may be stuck closed. A specialist can perform a cooling system pressure test to check for leaks, a flow test to check the water pump, and a temperature sensor check to verify the gauge is reading correctly. Do not ignore an overheating engine; continued driving can warp cylinder heads, blow the head gasket, or cause complete engine seizure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you notice the engine overheating. The M272's cooling system is a known weak point, and the water pump impeller and radiator end tanks are ticking time bombs. Addressing the issue early — whether it's a simple thermostat or a more complex water pump or radiator replacement — can prevent a head gasket failure or a catastrophic engine overheat. Frequently Asked Questions

Is engine overheating on an M272 always a water pump issue?

It's a common cause, but not the only one. A leaking radiator, a stuck thermostat, a failed radiator fan, or a head gasket leak can also cause overheating. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a pressure test, flow test, and visual inspection.

At what mileage does an M272 typically start overheating?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving style. Regular cooling system inspections are the best prevention.

Can I drive an M272 if it's overheating?

Absolutely not. Continued driving with an overheating engine can warp cylinder heads, blow the head gasket, or cause complete engine seizure. Pull over immediately and have it inspected by a specialist.

How much does it cost to fix an overheating issue on an M272?

A water pump replacement typically costs £400–£800 at an independent specialist. A radiator replacement can cost £500–£1,000. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £2,500–£4,500 for a head gasket replacement, or £4,500–£7,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
