Mercedes M274 White Smoke

White smoke from the exhaust on the M274 2.0-litre turbo petrol is a serious warning sign. This page covers exactly what causes it on this engine, the real cost to fix it, and our verdict on repair vs replace.

On the M274, white smoke from the exhaust is almost always caused by coolant entering the combustion chamber – most commonly due to a blown head gasket, a cracked cylinder head, or a leaking turbocharger coolant line. This is often a consequence of engine overheating. Ignoring it can lead to severe engine damage. Typical repair costs range from £1,000–£2,500 for a head gasket to £4,000–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

What This Engine Specifically Does

White smoke from the exhaust on the M274 is a thick, persistent cloud that often has a sweet smell (coolant) and does not dissipate quickly like condensation. It is most noticeable on cold start, but unlike the brief steam of a cold morning, this smoke continues once the engine warms up and may get worse under acceleration. On this engine, white smoke is a clear indicator that coolant is leaking into the combustion chamber, where it turns to steam and exits through the exhaust. The most common causes are a blown head gasket, a cracked cylinder head (often due to previous overheating), or a leaking turbocharger coolant line that allows coolant to enter the exhaust stream.

This is distinct from blue smoke (oil burning) or black smoke (over-fuelling). White smoke on the M274 is a serious issue that requires immediate attention. If you see white smoke, stop driving and have the car recovered to a specialist – continuing to drive can cause the engine to overheat and warp the cylinder head, turning a £1,000 repair into a £5,000 engine replacement.

Why This Engine In Particular

The M274 is a high‑output turbocharged four‑cylinder that generates significant heat, and its cooling system is under constant stress. The cylinder head is made of aluminium, which can warp if the engine overheats – a common consequence of a failed water pump or thermostat housing. Once warped, the head gasket can no longer seal properly, allowing coolant to leak into the cylinders. Additionally, the cylinder head itself can develop cracks between the valve seats or in the exhaust port, which is a known weakness on some early M274 engines.

The same symptom on a diesel OM651, for example, might point to a failed EGR cooler or injector seal, but on the M274 petrol, the head gasket and cylinder head are the primary suspects. Overheating is the common thread – and the M274's cooling system components, such as the water pump and thermostat housing, are known to fail, leading to overheating and subsequent head gasket failure.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

First, check the coolant level – if it's dropping without any visible external leak, and you see white smoke, it's likely internal. A specialist will perform a chemical test of the coolant for combustion gases (hydrocarbon test) to confirm a head gasket breach. They may also perform a compression test and a leak‑down test to identify which cylinder is affected. If the coolant is clean and the test is negative, the smoke could be condensation (normal on cold mornings) or a leaking turbocharger coolant seal – but that is less common. Do not confuse white smoke with steam from condensation; condensation dissipates quickly, while white smoke from a head gasket persists.

If you suspect a head gasket issue, stop driving immediately. Continued operation can cause the engine to overheat and warp the cylinder head, turning a manageable repair into a full engine replacement.

Verdict

Do not ignore white smoke on the M274. It is almost always a sign of coolant entering the combustion chamber, and the most common cause is a blown head gasket or cracked cylinder head – both of which are repairable if caught early. On a typical C‑Class or GLC with a market value of £8,000–£25,000, a £1,000–£2,500 repair is a sensible investment. Replacement flags ✅ Safe against our 70% threshold for the majority of M274‑fitted vehicles, but on older lower-value W204/GLK examples it may be a ⚠️ Watch – full reasoning is explained in our Decision Framework.

The key is to act immediately. Have a specialist perform a combustion gas test and inspect the cylinder head as soon as you see white smoke. If the engine has already overheated, the repair will be more extensive, but it is still often worth doing on a high‑value vehicle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke on the M274 always a head gasket issue?

It's the most common cause, but not the only one. A cracked cylinder head, a leaking turbocharger coolant line, or even a failed EGR cooler (on some variants) can cause white smoke. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a chemical test and a pressure test of the cooling system.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an M274?

At an independent specialist, a head gasket replacement is around £1,000–£2,500. If the cylinder head is cracked, you're looking at £2,500–£4,500. A full engine replacement is £4,000–£6,500. Main dealer prices are higher.

Can I drive my M274 with white smoke?

No. White smoke indicates that coolant is entering the combustion chamber, which can lead to overheating and severe engine damage. Stop driving and have the car recovered to a workshop immediately.

At what mileage does the M274 head gasket typically fail?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, often as a result of previous overheating. Regular coolant checks and replacing the water pump at 60k–80k can reduce the risk.

Can a cooling system pressure test confirm a head gasket leak?

Yes. A pressure test can reveal a drop in pressure, but a combustion gas test of the coolant is more definitive. A specialist will use a chemical tester to detect hydrocarbons in the coolant, confirming a head gasket breach.
