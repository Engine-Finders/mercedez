Mercedes Misfire & Rough Idle

A misfire or rough idle can be caused by many things – from a simple spark plug to a serious engine fault. This page covers the most common causes across Mercedes engines, with real cost data and clear next steps.

A misfire or rough idle on a Mercedes engine is often caused by a faulty ignition coil, a worn spark plug, a vacuum leak, a failing MAF sensor, or a fuel system issue. It can also be a symptom of a more serious problem like a timing chain issue or head gasket failure. The cost to fix it depends on the cause – from £50–£200 for a spark plug to £1,500–£3,000 for timing chain repairs.

What It Feels Like

A misfire or rough idle is one of those symptoms you can't miss – the engine feels uneven, lumpy, or like it's running on fewer cylinders than it should. At idle, you might feel the car shaking, the tachometer needle wavering, or a noticeable vibration through the seat and steering wheel. Under acceleration, a misfire can feel like a hesitation, a stutter, or a loss of power, often accompanied by a flashing check engine light.

Different owners describe it differently: “it feels like it's missing a beat”, “the car jerks when I press the pedal”, or “it sounds like a Subaru” (a reference to the uneven idle of a boxer engine). On a Mercedes, a rough idle can range from a barely perceptible wobble to a violent shaking that makes the car undriveable. The severity often correlates with the underlying cause – a simple spark plug issue might cause a slight stumble, while a timing chain problem can cause a persistent, aggressive misfire across multiple cylinders.

How to Narrow It Down Yourself

Before taking the car to a specialist, there are a few safe checks you can do to narrow down the cause:

  • Check the engine management light: If it's flashing, that indicates a severe misfire – stop driving immediately.
  • Listen to the idle: Does the engine sound uneven? Does the tachometer fluctuate? If it's a consistent misfire on one cylinder, it might be an ignition coil or spark plug.
  • Check for vacuum leaks: With the engine running at idle, listen for a hissing sound. Spray a small amount of carb cleaner or WD-40 around the intake manifold gaskets and vacuum hoses – if the idle changes, you've found a leak. (Do this carefully and avoid spraying onto hot surfaces.)
  • Check the air filter: A severely clogged air filter can cause a rough idle and hesitation. Replacing it is cheap and easy.
  • Check the oil level and condition: Low oil or sludge can affect the timing chain tensioner, which can cause a misfire on engines with variable valve timing.

If you can't find the cause, or if the engine is running very roughly, don't drive it – have it recovered to a workshop. A misfire can damage the catalytic converter if left unchecked.

When It's Serious vs When It Can Wait

Serious – Don't Drive

  • Flashing check engine light
  • Engine shaking violently
  • Loss of power on acceleration
  • White or blue smoke from exhaust
  • Oil pressure warning light

Can Wait – But Don't Delay

  • Intermittent stumble at idle
  • Hesitation under light acceleration
  • Check engine light on (not flashing)
  • Slight vibration at idle
  • Poor fuel economy

A rough idle that's just an annoyance can quickly become a serious problem – especially if it's caused by a timing chain issue or a fuel injector that's leaking. If you're unsure, get a diagnostic scan as soon as possible.

Verdict

Do not ignore a misfire or rough idle on your Mercedes. It's often a simple, inexpensive fix – but it can also be the first warning of a major problem. The key is to get a diagnostic scan as soon as possible. A specialist can read the fault codes and tell you exactly which cylinder is misfiring, and from there, the cause can often be pinpointed quickly.

If you're experiencing a misfire, check the M274 engine knocking noise, M276 cold start rattle, or OM642 black smoke pages for engine-specific issues. Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a misfire on a Mercedes engine?

The most common causes are a faulty ignition coil, a worn spark plug, a vacuum leak, a failing MAF sensor, or a fuel injector problem. It can also be a sign of a more serious timing chain or head gasket issue. A diagnostic scan is the fastest way to pinpoint the cause.

Can I drive with a misfire?

If the check engine light is flashing, stop driving immediately – this indicates a severe misfire that can damage the catalytic converter. If the light is on but not flashing, you can drive gently to a workshop, but don't delay. A misfire can cause secondary damage if left unchecked.

How much does it cost to fix a misfire on a Mercedes?

It depends on the cause. A spark plug replacement is around £50–£200, an ignition coil is £150–£400, a MAF sensor is £200–£500, and timing chain work can be £1,500–£3,000. Main dealer prices are higher.

Can a vacuum leak cause a rough idle?

Yes. A vacuum leak allows unmetered air to enter the engine, causing the air‑fuel mixture to become lean. This can lead to a rough idle, hesitation, and sometimes a misfire. It's a common cause on higher‑mileage engines.

Is a rough idle on a Mercedes always a misfire?

Not always. A rough idle can also be caused by a dirty throttle body, a stuck EGR valve, a failing idle air control valve, or even a fuel pressure issue. However, a misfire is the most common cause of a rough idle on modern Mercedes engines.
