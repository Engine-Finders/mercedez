Mercedes OM642 White Smoke

White smoke from the exhaust on this V6 diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the OM642, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the OM642 specifically, white smoke from the exhaust is a clear sign of coolant entering the combustion chamber. The most common causes are a failed EGR cooler, leaking injector seals ("Black Death"), or a cracked cylinder head or head gasket. Ignoring it can lead to severe engine damage, hydrolock, and complete failure.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the OM642 the picture narrows considerably. This V6 diesel's dominant white smoke signature is a thick, sweet-smelling cloud from the exhaust, often most noticeable on cold start or under heavy load. It is distinct from the black smoke of over-fuelling (injector issues) and the blue smoke of oil burning (turbo seals or piston rings). On the OM642, white smoke almost always points to coolant entering the combustion chamber-either through a failed EGR cooler, leaking injector seals, or a cracked cylinder head. The smell is a key differentiator: sweet coolant vapour has a distinctive odour that's hard to mistake.

Why This Engine In Particular

The OM642 has several design features that make white smoke a more common symptom than on other engines. The EGR cooler, which uses engine coolant to cool recirculated exhaust gases, is a known weak point. Over time, its internal tubes can corrode and crack, allowing coolant to enter the exhaust stream and produce white smoke. Additionally, the injector seals are prone to leaking-the infamous "Black Death"-which allows combustion gases to escape past the seal and, in some cases, allows coolant to enter the cylinders through cracked seal seats. Finally, the OM642's aluminium cylinder heads can warp or crack if the engine has been overheated, allowing coolant to enter the combustion chambers. This is a design characteristic of this specific engine family; the same symptom on, say, a four-cylinder OM651 would more likely point to a head gasket failure, as the EGR cooler on that engine is less prone to internal leaks.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the coolant level-if it's dropping without an external leak, the coolant is going somewhere, likely into the engine. A cooling system pressure test will reveal internal leaks that don't show externally. A combustion leak test (sniff test) will detect exhaust gases in the coolant, which indicates a head gasket or EGR cooler failure. A specialist will also inspect the injector area for signs of "Black Death"-black carbon buildup around the injectors. Do not continue driving if thick white smoke is present; it can lead to hydrolock and catastrophic engine failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke from an OM642 always a coolant issue?

Yes-white smoke on the OM642 is almost always a sign of coolant entering the combustion chamber. The most common causes are a failed EGR cooler, leaking injector seals ("Black Death"), or a cracked head gasket.

At what mileage does an OM642 typically develop white smoke?

Most cases in our data occur between 70,000 and 150,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving conditions. Regular cooling system maintenance is critical.

Can I drive an OM642 with white smoke?

It's not recommended if the smoke is thick and persistent. Continued driving can cause hydrolock and catastrophic engine failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an OM642?

An EGR cooler replacement typically costs £600–£1,200 at an independent specialist. Injector seal replacement runs £400–£1,200. If the head gasket is damaged, expect £1,500–£3,000. Severe damage may require a replacement engine at £4,500–£7,500.
