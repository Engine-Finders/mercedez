Mercedes OM651 Cold Start Rattle

Cold start rattle on this four-cylinder diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the OM651, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the OM651 specifically, a cold start rattle is often the first audible warning of a developing issue. The most common causes are timing chain tensioner wear (oil drains back overnight), injector seal failure ("Black Death") causing carbon buildup, or low oil pressure from a failing oil pump. Ignoring it can lead to chain jump, engine damage, or eventual failure.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general cold start rattle guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the OM651 the picture narrows considerably. This four-cylinder diesel's characteristic cold start rattle is a metallic, rattling noise that lasts from one to several seconds after cranking, most noticeable on first start of the day. Owners often describe it as "a diesel clatter that shouldn't be there" or "a rattle that disappears once oil pressure builds." On the OM651, this noise almost always points to one of three issues: a timing chain tensioner that's lost its oil prime (a known problem, documented in Mercedes TSB LI80.20-P-050001), worn injector seals causing carbon buildup ("Black Death"), or a failing balance shaft chain tensioner. Unlike the V6 OM642, where cold start rattle is often linked to oil cooler seals, on the OM651 the primary culprit is almost always chain-related.

Why This Engine In Particular

The OM651's timing chain tensioner relies on oil pressure to maintain proper chain tension. Over time, the tensioner's internal check valve can fail, allowing oil to drain back into the sump overnight. On startup, the chain momentarily slackens, creating a rattle until oil pressure returns. This is a well-documented issue with the OM651, and Mercedes issued a TSB (LI80.20-P-050001) addressing timing chain tensioner wear. Additionally, the balance shaft chain on this engine is known to stretch or wear its guides, producing a rattle that can be mistaken for timing chain noise. The injector seals-infamous for causing "Black Death"-also play a role: carbon buildup around the injectors can cause uneven combustion, which can sound like a rattle on cold start until the engine warms up. This is a design characteristic of this specific engine family, and the same symptom on the V6 OM642 would more likely point to a different set of causes.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Listen carefully to the noise: a rattle that lasts more than 2–3 seconds on cold start is more concerning than one that fades instantly. Check the oil level and service history-low oil or extended intervals can cause tensioner issues. A specialist will perform an oil pressure test to check for leaks, inspect the injector area for signs of "Black Death" (carbon buildup), and use diagnostic equipment to check for timing chain deviation. If the injector seals are leaking, a leak-off test will confirm. Do not ignore a persistent rattle; it can lead to timing chain jump, bent valves, and catastrophic engine failure. A simple tensioner replacement is far cheaper than a full engine rebuild.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cold start rattle on an OM651 always a timing chain issue?

Not always. It can also be caused by worn injector seals ("Black Death") or balance shaft chain wear. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with oil pressure tests, leak-off tests, and timing deviation measurements.

At what mileage does an OM651 typically develop cold start rattle?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 150,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and oil quality. Regular oil changes (every 10,000 miles or annually) with the correct MB spec oil are critical.

Can I drive an OM651 with a cold start rattle?

It's not recommended if the rattle lasts more than 2–3 seconds. Continued driving can cause timing chain stretch, jump, and catastrophic engine failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix a cold start rattle on an OM651?

A tensioner replacement typically costs £600–£1,500 at an independent specialist. Injector seal replacement runs £400–£1,200. If the balance shaft or engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £1,500–£6,000 depending on the scope of repair.
