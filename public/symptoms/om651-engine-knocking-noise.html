Mercedes OM651 Engine Knocking Noise

Engine knocking on this four-cylinder diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the OM651, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the OM651 specifically, a knocking noise from the engine is a serious audible warning. The most common causes are injector seal failure ("Black Death") causing uneven combustion, timing chain tensioner wear, or balance shaft chain wear. Ignoring it can lead to catastrophic engine failure, including valve train damage and a seized engine.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine knocking noise guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the OM651 the picture narrows considerably. This four-cylinder diesel's dominant knocking signature is a metallic, rhythmic noise that often increases with engine speed and load. Owners frequently describe it as "a hammering sound from the top end" or "a diesel knock that gets louder under acceleration." On the OM651, knocking almost always points to one of three issues: injector seal failure allowing combustion gases to escape and causing uneven firing, timing chain tensioner wear leading to chain slap, or balance shaft chain stretch that produces a rattle that can be mistaken for knocking. Unlike the cold-start rattle that fades quickly, engine knocking is persistent and indicates an active fault that requires immediate attention.

Why This Engine In Particular

The OM651's common-rail injection system is sensitive to injector condition. Over time, injector seals can fail—a problem so common it's earned the nickname "Black Death"—allowing combustion gases to escape past the seal, which in turn affects injector spray pattern and causes uneven combustion. This produces a distinct knocking noise that is often louder at idle. Additionally, the timing chain tensioner is a known weak point (Mercedes TSB LI80.20-P-050001); its check valve can fail, causing the chain to slacken and slap against the guides, producing a knocking sound that can be heard at various RPMs. Finally, the balance shaft chain on this engine can stretch or its guides can wear, creating a rattle that can be misdiagnosed as engine knock. This is a design characteristic of this specific engine family; the same symptom on the V6 OM642 would more likely point to oil cooler seal failure or bearing knock.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler issues. Check the oil level and condition—low or contaminated oil can cause knocking. Listen with a mechanic's stethoscope to isolate the noise: injector knock is loudest near the injector area, timing chain slap is heard at the front of the engine, and balance shaft noise may be deeper. A specialist will perform an injector leak-off test to check for faulty injectors, measure timing chain deviation using diagnostic equipment, and inspect the balance shaft chain for wear. If the injector seals are leaking, there may be visible carbon buildup around the injectors. Do not ignore engine knocking; it will only worsen and can lead to timing chain jump, valve damage, or a seized engine, which is often uneconomical to repair.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you hear engine knocking. The OM651's knocking noise is a clear warning sign, and early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's an injector seal, replacement is relatively straightforward. If it's timing chain or balance shaft, catching it early prevents catastrophic damage. Ignoring it risks a seized engine, which is often uneconomical to repair. Frequently Asked Questions

Is engine knocking on an OM651 always an injector seal issue?

Not always. It can also be caused by timing chain tensioner wear, balance shaft chain stretch, or even low oil pressure. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with leak-off tests, timing deviation measurement, and oil pressure tests.

At what mileage does an OM651 typically start knocking?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 150,000 miles, particularly on vehicles with extended service intervals or poor oil quality. Regular oil changes with the correct MB spec are critical.

Can I drive an OM651 with an engine knocking noise?

It's not recommended. Continued driving can cause timing chain failure, valve damage, or a seized engine. Have it inspected immediately.

How much does it cost to fix engine knocking on an OM651?

An injector seal replacement costs £400–£1,200. Timing chain tensioner work costs £600–£1,500. If the balance shaft or engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £1,500–£6,000 depending on the scope of repair.
