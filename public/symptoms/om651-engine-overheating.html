Mercedes OM651 Engine Overheating

Engine overheating on this four-cylinder diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the OM651, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the OM651 specifically, engine overheating is a critical red flag. The most common causes are a failed EGR cooler (internal coolant leak), a stuck thermostat, a failing water pump, or a cracked cylinder head. Ignoring it can lead to head gasket failure, warped cylinder head, and catastrophic engine damage.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine overheating guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the OM651 the picture narrows considerably. This four-cylinder diesel has several known cooling system weaknesses that cause the temperature gauge to climb into the red. Owners often report the temperature rising under load, the coolant bubbling in the expansion tank, or the engine going into limp mode. On the OM651, overheating without an obvious external leak almost always points to one of three internal issues: a failed EGR cooler that allows coolant to escape into the exhaust, a water pump that's not circulating coolant properly, or a cracked cylinder head/head gasket breach that allows coolant into the combustion chambers. Unlike a simple thermostat failure, which is common on many engines, the OM651's cooling system is complex, and overheating often signals a more serious underlying problem.

Why This Engine In Particular

The OM651's cooling system is tightly integrated with its emissions control components. The EGR cooler, which uses engine coolant to cool recirculated exhaust gases, is a known weak point. Over time, internal passages can become blocked with soot or the cooler can crack, restricting coolant flow and allowing coolant to be lost into the exhaust. Additionally, the water pump is driven by the serpentine belt and can suffer from bearing wear or impeller failure, reducing circulation. The thermostat can stick closed, preventing coolant from reaching the radiator. Finally, the aluminium cylinder head can warp or crack if the engine has been overheated—often as a result of the first two failures—leading to a head gasket breach. This is a design characteristic of this specific engine family; the same symptom on, say, a V6 OM642 would more likely point to oil cooler seal failure rather than the EGR cooler, though the OM642 also suffers from EGR cooler issues. On the OM651, the EGR cooler is the prime suspect in many cases.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the coolant level—low coolant is the most common cause of overheating. If the level is fine, check for external leaks (hoses, radiator, water pump weep hole). If none are visible, a cooling system pressure test will reveal internal leaks or blockages. A specialist will also check the operation of the thermostat, the water pump flow rate, and the condition of the EGR cooler. They may use thermal imaging to detect hot spots. If the EGR cooler is suspected, a chemical test (sniff test) can detect combustion gases in the coolant. Do not continue driving an overheating OM651; it will quickly warp cylinder heads and destroy the engine. Have it towed to a specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is overheating on an OM651 always a serious issue?

Yes—overheating is always serious. It can cause rapid engine damage. Immediate action is required to prevent head gasket failure, warped heads, and seized pistons.

Can a faulty thermostat cause overheating on an OM651?

Yes, a stuck thermostat is a common cause. It's relatively inexpensive to replace and should be the first check if the cooling system has no leaks.

At what mileage does an OM651 typically overheat?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 150,000 miles, often related to neglected coolant changes or EGR cooler failure. Regular coolant flushes (every 3–4 years) are important.

How much does it cost to fix overheating on an OM651?

A thermostat replacement costs £200–£400. A water pump costs £400–£800. An EGR cooler costs £600–£1,200. If head gasket damage has occurred, expect £1,500–£3,000. Severe damage may require a replacement engine at £4,000–£6,000.
