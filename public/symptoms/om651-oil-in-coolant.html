Mercedes OM651 Oil In Coolant

Oil contamination in the coolant on this four-cylinder diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Mercedes engine. Here's exactly what it means on the OM651, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the OM651 specifically, oil in the coolant is a serious red flag. The most common cause is failure of the oil cooler seals (located at the oil filter housing), allowing oil and coolant to mix. Other possibilities include a head gasket failure or a cracked cylinder head. Ignoring it leads to degraded coolant, overheating, and eventual engine damage.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general oil in coolant guide covers several possible causes across the whole Mercedes range, on the OM651 the picture narrows considerably. This four-cylinder diesel's known weak point is the oil cooler, which is integrated into the oil filter housing. The seals that separate oil and coolant circuits are prone to failure over time, allowing oil to enter the cooling system and coolant to enter the oil. Owners often first notice a milky residue in the expansion tank, a sweet smell from the engine bay, or an unexplained drop in oil level. On the OM651, oil in the coolant without any external leak almost always points to internal seal or gasket failure, and the most common culprit is the oil cooler seals—a well-documented issue on this engine family.

Why This Engine In Particular

The OM651's oil cooler is part of the oil filter housing, which is mounted on the side of the engine block. The seals between the oil and coolant passages can degrade over time, particularly on vehicles that have seen extended oil change intervals or have been driven hard. When they fail, oil and coolant mix—often slowly at first, so the contamination may only be visible as a slight film on the coolant surface. If left unchecked, the mixture can form a sludge that blocks coolant passages, leading to overheating and, eventually, head gasket failure. This is a design characteristic of this specific engine family; the same symptom on, say, a V6 OM642 would more likely point to oil cooler seals in the V of the engine, as the OM642's oil cooler is in a different location.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler issues. Check the coolant for a milky or creamy appearance—this is a strong indicator of oil contamination. Also check the oil level; if coolant has entered the oil, the oil level may rise and appear milky. A cooling system pressure test can help identify internal leaks. A specialist will also perform a combustion leak test (sniff test) to check for exhaust gases in the coolant, which would indicate a head gasket failure. If no head gasket issue is found, the oil cooler seals are the prime suspect. Do not ignore oil in coolant; it will only worsen, leading to overheating and eventual engine damage.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you notice oil in the coolant. The OM651's oil cooler seal failure is a well-known issue, and early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If caught early, replacing the seals is straightforward. Ignoring it leads to contaminated coolant, overheating, and potential head gasket failure. Frequently Asked Questions

Is oil in coolant on an OM651 always an oil cooler seal issue?

Not always, but it's the most common cause. It can also be due to a head gasket failure or a cracked cylinder head. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with pressure tests and combustion leak tests.

At what mileage does an OM651 typically develop oil in coolant?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 150,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving conditions. Regular oil and coolant changes are critical.

Can I drive an OM651 with oil in the coolant?

It's not recommended. Continued driving can cause severe overheating, sludge buildup, and engine damage. Have it inspected as soon as you notice contamination.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on an OM651?

An oil cooler seal replacement typically costs £400–£800 at an independent specialist. If the head gasket is damaged, expect £1,500–£3,000. Severe damage may require a replacement engine at £4,000–£6,000.
