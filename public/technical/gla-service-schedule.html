Mercedes-Benz GLA Service Schedule & Intervals

Factory-recommended service intervals for the Mercedes‑Benz GLA (X156 and H247 generations). Covers oil changes, filter replacements, brake fluid, coolant, and essential checks across petrol and diesel variants.

The Mercedes‑Benz GLA requires a service every 12 months or 15,500 miles (whichever comes first) [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs MB workshop manual], with a Major Service (including spark plugs on petrol, fuel filter on diesel) every 24 months / 31,000 miles.

  • Oil change interval: 12 months / 15,500 mi; use MB‑Approval 229.5 (petrol) or 229.51 (diesel) [PLACEHOLDER — confirm spec]
  • Brake fluid: every 2 years [PLACEHOLDER — confirm MB interval]
  • Coolant: first change at 10 years / 155,000 mi, then every 5 years [PLACEHOLDER]
  • Air filter & cabin filter: every 4 years / 50,000 mi (or sooner in dusty conditions) [PLACEHOLDER]

Variant Differences: X156 vs H247

The GLA has been produced in two generations: the first‑generation X156 (2014–2020) and the second‑generation H247 (2020–present). While the core service schedule is similar, engine choices and some fluid specs differ.

Notes & Caveats

The manufacturer's 15,500‑mile interval assumes motorway‑weighted driving. In real UK conditions — particularly with the diesel OM651 on short urban trips — oil dilution from DPF regeneration can degrade the oil sooner [EM-INTERVIEW]. Many independent Mercedes specialists we've spoken to recommend reducing oil change intervals to 10,000 miles or 12 months for predominantly urban use, regardless of what the service indicator says. This is especially important for X156 diesel models, which share the well‑documented OM651 injector seal and chain tensioner vulnerabilities covered on our OM651 Failure Page.

Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time — the 2‑year interval is not a suggestion. Ignoring it can lead to internal corrosion of the ABS pump and reduced braking performance, especially in emergency stops. Similarly, the coolant change at 10 years uses a long‑life formulation, but if you're topping up with tap water (common mistake), you should change it much sooner.

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring the brake fluid interval. “It looks fine” is not a valid test; moisture content must be measured. Replace every 2 years regardless.
  • Using the wrong oil specification. Petrol engines require MB‑Approval 229.5; diesels require 229.51 (low‑SAPS) — using the wrong one can damage the DPF and after‑treatment systems. Check your engine code first via our Engine Code Decoder.
  • Not resetting the service indicator. Even if you do the work, the car's computer still counts down based on time and mileage; an overdue warning can affect resale value.
  • Assuming the transmission fluid is "lifetime". Mercedes no longer claims lifetime fill for 7G/9G‑Tronic; many specialists recommend a fluid and filter change at 60,000 miles to extend gearbox life [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Forgetting the AdBlue refill (H247 diesel). The system has its own tank and a warning light; running out can prevent engine restart.

How to Verify on Your Actual GLA

The service history is stored in the car's digital service booklet (accessible via the steering wheel buttons) and can also be retrieved by a Mercedes dealer using the VIN. To confirm which service items are due, use the dashboard service menu: navigate to Service → Service Due. The car will show a countdown in days or miles. For a more detailed check, plug an OBD‑II scanner that reads MB‑specific codes (e.g., via X‑entry or a high‑end generic tool) to read the service counter and any pending maintenance reminders. Always cross‑reference with the physical service book if you have one — discrepancies between digital and paper records are a red flag.

If you're unsure which engine variant you have, locate the engine number stamp (see our Engine Number Location guide) or use the VIN decoder linked below.

Why This Schedule Matters

Following the manufacturer's service schedule is the single most important factor in preserving your GLA's reliability and resale value. A missed oil change can lead to sludge formation, blocked oil pick‑up pipes, and eventually catastrophic engine failure — particularly on the OM651 diesel, where the balance shaft and timing chain tensioner are sensitive to oil quality. Brake fluid neglect can cause ABS pump failure, a repair that often exceeds the cost of a full service. Coolant that's past its life loses corrosion inhibition, leading to heater core or radiator blockages that are expensive to flush. By sticking to the schedule — and adjusting for severe use — you avoid these predictable failures. The same logic applies to transmission fluid: a 60,000‑mile change costs a few hundred pounds; a new 7G‑Tronic costs several thousand.

If you're considering buying a used GLA, check the service history meticulously. GLA models with incomplete or deferred services are far more likely to develop the issues detailed on our Failure Pages. Conversely, a fully stamped history with evidence of the major services is a strong indicator of a well‑maintained car.
