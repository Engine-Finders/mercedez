The Mercedes‑Benz M270 uses a timing chain, not a belt [PLACEHOLDER - confirm vs OEM workshop data]. The chain drives the camshafts and is located at the front of the engine, with hydraulic tensioners and plastic guides that require periodic inspection.

  • Chain type: Duplex roller chain with hydraulic tensioners [PLACEHOLDER - confirm spec]
  • Recommended inspection interval: every 60,000 miles or 5 years (specialist recommendation) [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Turbocharger heat caveat: heat soak can accelerate tensioner wear, especially in high‑output variants [EM-INTERVIEW] - see below
  • Applies across A‑Class (W176/W177), B‑Class (W246/W247), CLA (C117/X117), GLA (X156), and other MFA‑platform models

Notes & Caveats

The M270's turbocharged design - especially in higher‑output variants - generates significant heat that can accelerate the degradation of plastic chain guides and tensioner seals [EM-INTERVIEW]. In stop‑start urban driving, the turbo can heat soak, leading to higher under‑bonnet temperatures that affect the tensioner's hydraulic performance and guide durability.

Independent specialists we've spoken to frequently recommend a chain inspection at 60,000 miles, regardless of what the service schedule indicates. If you hear a rattle on cold start that lasts more than 1–2 seconds, this is an early warning of tensioner wear. Addressing it early - replacing the tensioner and guides - costs around £500–£1,000 at a specialist. Ignoring it can lead to chain skip, bent valves, and a full engine rebuild or replacement, which on an M270 can cost £3,000–£6,000 [EM-INTERVIEW].

We cover the full failure mechanism on our M274 Timing Chain Tensioner Failure page (the M270 shares many of the same turbo‑related dynamics). The key takeaway: the chain itself is robust, but the tensioner and guides are the weak points - and they're accelerated by the turbocharger's heat.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming the M270 has a belt. It does not - it has a chain. If a seller or mechanic tells you otherwise, they are misinformed.
  • Ignoring cold‑start rattle. A brief rattle on start‑up is not normal; it indicates tensioner wear and should be investigated immediately.
  • Using the wrong oil specification. The hydraulic tensioner relies on oil pressure; using non‑MB‑Approved 229.5 oil can reduce tensioner performance and accelerate wear - see our M270 Oil Capacity page for details.
  • Confusing M270 with M274. The later 2.0‑litre turbo (M274) has a revised tensioner design and different failure patterns - check your engine code first via our Engine Code Decoder.
  • Not inspecting the guides during chain work. Replacing the chain without replacing the guides and tensioner is a false economy - the guides are usually the source of the problem.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The timing chain is not directly visible without removing the engine front cover, but you can check for chain wear indirectly:

  • Listen for cold‑start rattle: A rattle lasting more than 1–2 seconds on a cold start is the most common early indicator of tensioner wear.
  • Check for chain noise at idle: A rhythmic rattling or slapping noise at idle, particularly when hot, may indicate chain stretch or guide wear.
  • Inspect oil condition: If the oil is sludgy or has metallic particles, the chain guides may be wearing.
  • Use diagnostic tools: A Mercedes‑specific diagnostic tool can read camshaft position deviation values - if the deviation exceeds specification, the chain may be stretched.

For definitive confirmation, the front timing cover must be removed for visual inspection - a job that requires specialist knowledge. Use our Engine Number Location guide to confirm your engine variant.

Why This Specification Matters

Knowing whether the M270 uses a timing chain or belt matters because it affects maintenance planning, cost expectations, and failure risk assessment. A chain is generally more durable than a belt - it doesn't require routine replacement at a fixed mileage interval - but it is not maintenance‑free. The M270's chain tensioner and guides are known wear points, accelerated by the turbocharger's heat.

If you're considering buying an M270‑powered car - such as an A250, CLA250, GLA250, or B200 - check the service history for evidence of chain tensioner or guide replacement. Cars that have had this work done (especially with the revised tensioner) are far less likely to experience the catastrophic failure described on our failure page (similar principles apply to the M270). Conversely, a car with no chain work and a cold‑start rattle should be approached with caution - the repair cost can exceed £1,500 at a specialist, and significantly more at a main dealer.
